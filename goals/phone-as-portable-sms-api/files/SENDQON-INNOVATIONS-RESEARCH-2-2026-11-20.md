## #4 Gawah — SIM-Sealed Verbal Agreements

**Verdict up front: GO WITH CONDITIONS — narrow scope only.** The product is buildable on the live Sendqon stack, and the underlying oral contract is, in most everyday cases, already valid in Pakistani law; what Gawah sells is *proof* — exactly what courts demand most and litigants have least. But the concept overreaches in three places to cut before any build: (1) it must never be called "notarisation" — no private company in Pakistan can notarise anything; (2) land-sale promises must be excluded — the Electronic Transactions Ordinance 2002 (ETO) expressly does not apply to contracts for the sale or conveyance of immovable property; and (3) no certificate may be marketed as court-proof or presumptively authentic unless its seal is an *advanced* electronic signature under the ETO — which a self-issued Sendqon hash-and-timestamp seal is not. What survives is real: a consent-announced, identity-anchored, tamper-evident recording of a spoken bargain, sold cheaply to people who today have a *parchi*, a memory, and nothing else. The formal legal opinion the owner specified is the product's foundation, and it gates everything, including the pilot.

**The concept as given:** two people on a call invoke Gawah; the system announces recording in Urdu (consent), captures the promise segment, binds it to both SIM identities plus Raast account titles, timestamps and seals it, and issues both parties a Gawahi certificate checkable later by SMS/IVR code — built on Sendqon's PBX recording and SIM identity, no third-party API, with every record feeding the Number Trust Bureau (#1).

**Two corrections to the brief.** First, the "Notaries Act 1952" it cites is the *Indian* statute; Pakistan's law is the **Notaries Ordinance, 1961 (XIX of 1961)** [official — ss. 3, 5, 8, 9], and (d) proceeds on that basis. Second, the hypothesis that digital lenders record verbal consent calls could **not** be verified as stated — what the regulator mandates is adjacent but different (see (b)).

### (a) Problem + payer (Pakistan first)

**The spoken economy is most of the economy.** The Labour Force Survey 2024–25 (Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, Feb 2026) puts **80.8% of employed Pakistanis in informal work** (72.1% under the narrower non-agriculture definition); average monthly wages were Rs 39,042 [official — PBS LFS 2024–25, via The News/Profit]. An SBP survey found 47.6% of informal firms paying below minimum wage and 73.5% with *no pattern at all* for wage-setting [official — SBP Working Paper No. 75]. Shadow-economy estimates run from ~34–38% of GDP to a contested US$400–500 billion [industry — Express Tribune, 20 Apr 2025]. Either way, most bargains — *udhaar*, rent, wages, piece-rates, small loans — are struck by voice.

**The *parchi* is the incumbent system.** In Jodia Bazaar and Faisalabad's yarn markets it is a scrap bearing a name (often a coded alias), an amount and a date — "no stamps, no official seals, and certainly no biometric verification" — circulating as a reputation-backed promissory note [industry — Express Tribune, "Parchi economy keeps Pakistan running"]. It fails exactly where Gawah aims: when a party denies, leaves, or calculates the other cannot prove anything. A Gawah record is a parchi that survives denial.

**Why the weaker party loses: the law is on their side, the evidence is not.** An oral agreement is a valid contract if the Contract Act 1872 s.10 essentials are met — free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration and object; s.10 "does not exclude an oral contract from being enforced," and s.9 permits acceptance otherwise than in writing [official — ss. 2(h), 9, 10]. But the Supreme Court sets the proof bar high: in *Muhammad Riaz v. Mst. Badshah Begum* (2021 SCMR 605) and *Sadruddin v. Sultan Khan* (2021 SCMR 642), the party relying on an oral agreement must plead and prove date, time, place, terms and witnesses present, with "the clearest and more satisfactory evidence" demanded because oral claims invite "dishonest improvements" [official — Supreme Court judgments, via Courting The Law]. The law already asks for exactly what a Gawah record captures automatically — **when, who, what was said, before a (system) witness**. That alignment is this report's strongest point.

**The cost of failing to prove it.** A claimant enters a system carrying **over 2.2 million pending cases** (Law and Justice Commission: 2,362,135 at 31 December 2024, over 80% in the district judiciary; ~2.22 million at end-2025) [official — LJCP Judicial Statistics 2024; Bi-Annual Report Jul–Dec 2025]. The last World Bank Doing Business measurement (2020, discontinued) recorded **1,071 days and 20.5% of claim value** to enforce a standard contract [industry — Doing Business 2020, historical benchmark]. Court fee is trivial — Punjab ad valorem 7.5% capped at Rs 15,000, nil up to Rs 25,000 [official — Court Fees Act 1870 as amended 2024] — so the barrier is years of appearances plus a lawyer: agreement drafting **Rs 15,000–35,000**, a legal notice Rs 10,000–25,000, breach-of-contract litigation Rs 150,000–600,000 [industry — AskWakeel guideline; TaxHouse from Rs 12,000]. For a Rs 50,000 udhaar or a month's wages, enforcement costs more than the claim. That arithmetic — not ignorance — is why the weaker party rationally abandons a valid claim.

**What protection costs today:**

| Protection | Price | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| Punjab e-stamp, "other agreements" (Art. 5(d)) | **Rs 500** (from Rs 100, Punjab Finance Act 2024) [official — Act via KPMG summary; PITB/PLRA e-Stamping] | Duty paid on a *written* document. Nothing about a spoken deal. |
| Punjab e-stamp, affidavit | **Rs 300** (from Rs 100, 2024) [official] | Duty on a sworn written statement. |
| Punjab e-stamp, property sale agreement | **Rs 3,000** (from Rs 1,200, 2024) [official] | Duty only; drafting, signing, witnesses still needed. |
| Notary public attestation | "A few hundred to a few thousand PKR per document" [industry — Lawzana guides; **no published statutory fee schedule was found**] | An identified person executed a *written instrument* before the notary (Notaries Ordinance 1961, s.8(1)(a)). |
| Lawyer-drafted agreement | **Rs 12,000–35,000** [industry] | A written contract — if both sides will go to a lawyer. |
| Doing nothing | Rs 0 now; the whole amount at risk later | Nothing. |

The cheapest state artefact (Rs 500 e-stamp) still presupposes literacy, a drafted text, both parties present, and witnesses. No product at any price documents a bargain *at the moment it is spoken, over the phone*. That empty cell is the market.

**Who pays — ranked** [planning assumption — our judgement; no willingness-to-pay survey exists [unverified — searched, none found], and the (f) pilot must convert this inference into a measured number]: **(1) Wholesalers/distributors extending udhaar** — repeat, high-frequency; amounts (Rs 50,000–several lakh) where a Rs 300–500 fee is noise and lawyers absurd; they already document (the parchi), so Gawah upgrades a habit. **(2) Micro/informal lenders** — a sealed record per disbursement is their recovery file. **(3) Landlords** — rent and especially deposit promises, per tenancy. **(4) Employers of informal labour** — wage/piece-rate promises; the employer gains as much as the worker, so either party must be able to initiate and pay. **(5) Individuals** — price-sensitive, non-repeat; an acquisition channel, not the revenue base.

### (b) Competitors and substitutes, with live evidence

**Punjab e-Stamping (PITB/Board of Revenue, now with PLRA and Bank of Punjab)** digitised *stamp duty*: generate Challan 32-A online, pay at Bank of Punjab or by PSID, receive an e-stamp with a unique number and QR code, verifiable thereafter [official — PLRA/PITB portal and FAQ]. Sindh runs an equivalent (Board of Revenue Sindh; operation transferring from PITB to the Sindh Information Technology Company) [official/industry — Sindh cabinet decision via UrduPoint]. What it did **not** digitise is the agreement itself — taxed paper onto which parties must still write, sign and witness their bargain. A complement and price anchor (Rs 500), not a competitor; and its "pay a small fee, get a checkable number" pattern — which Gawah's SMS/IVR check copies — is already understood by Pakistani users.

**Pakistani e-signature services — thin, document-based, none voice-first.** Three names have live web presence: **DocsMove** (document management + e-signature, claiming ETO-aligned signatures and e-sealing) [unverified — vendor claims on docsmove.pk; no independent verification of ECAC accreditation, clients or volumes found]; **Dastkhat** (whose compliance page correctly restates the ETO s.31 exclusions and lists rental/employment agreements as e-signable) [unverified — dastkhat.pk, Nov 2026; scale unconfirmed]; and **Wukla/PakSign** (lawyer-founded 2015; Dawn reported a 2020 B2B pivot for digital contracts) [industry — Dawn, 2020; **current operating status unverified**]. All are document-centric — upload, sign, store. None records a spoken bargain or serves a feature-phone user mid-call; none publishes ECAC accreditation or court-tested outcomes. A consumer "digital ikrarnama" product for verbal agreements **does not appear to exist** [unverified — searched in English and Roman-Urdu terms; absence reported as such].

**Regulated digital lenders — the brief's hypothesis, corrected.** SECP's NBFC digital-lending regime (Circular No. 12 of 2024) requires, before disbursement, a **Key Fact Statement** — amount, APR, tenor, instalments, all fees — displayed **through video/audio, screenshot and email/SMS, in English and Urdu**, with nano-loans capped at Rs 25,000 per app / Rs 75,000 aggregate, minimum 30-day tenure [official — SECP Circular 12 of 2024; PID release, 28 Dec 2022]. That is a *disclosure played to* the borrower, not a *recording of the borrower's spoken consent*; consent in licensed apps is an in-app act. No SECP/SBP instrument found requires recorded verbal-consent calls as the contract-forming act. Banks' "this call may be recorded" announcements are practice, not a statutory regime [industry]. So Gawah cannot claim "lenders already do this." The narrower, useful precedent: a regulator has accepted **Urdu audio presentation of key terms** to bind a low-literacy counterparty — supporting Gawah's Urdu-first read-back design.

**Global analogues — why they do not transfer.** **Proof (formerly Notarize)**, the US remote-online-notarisation (RON) leader, charges **US$25 for the first seal**, US$15 per additional seal, US$10 per additional signer, US$4 per eSign [official — published pricing, Oct–Nov 2026]. Its model rests on pillars Pakistan lacks: state-commissioned notaries whose RON acts are statutorily authorised in 45+ states; knowledge-based authentication against deep US data sources; a smartphone/video-first user. At PKR 277 = US$1, a US$25 seal is ~Rs 6,925 — about 18% of the average monthly wage — for a voice-first, often feature-phone base. **DocuSign** and its class presume a document exists. The transferable lesson is architectural — *recorded session + identity proofing + tamper-evident trail + checkable certificate* sells — but the "notary" in the middle cannot be supplied by a private platform in Pakistan ((d)); the shape must be re-based on consent recording and evidence engineering.

**Net position:** the true competitor is the Rs 0 status quo (memory + parchi + a bazaar witness) and, at the top end, the Rs 500 e-stamp plus a deed-writer. No live competitor occupies "spoken bargain, sealed at the moment of promise, checkable by SMS" — genuine white space, with the standard warning that white space in Pakistan usually signals a legal or distribution problem. Section (d) identifies the legal one; (f) tests the distribution one cheaply.

### (c) Technical feasibility on the Sendqon stack

Judged against the brief's stack facts — Asterisk PBX with recording/IVR/DTMF in production, Android SIM-gateway on real Pakistani SIMs, own SMPP (Jasmin), WhatsApp bridge, HLR plugin, Raast route with account-title verification, AI voice agents, Rektrs ID SSO [planning assumption — as given; Sendqon's systems were not independently audited].

**1. Orchestration, consent, segment capture — feasible now.** Both parties connect *through* the PBX (bridged/call-me-back conference — essential for custody, (d)); an Urdu announcement states the call will be recorded as a *gawahi* record and asks each party to state their name and agreement; DTMF/voice confirmation is logged; an IVR/AI-voice prompt walks the terms (who gives what, how much, by when) and reads a summary back for both to confirm; the promise segment is marked in the timeline. No third-party API is needed.

**2. Sealing (hash + timestamp) — technically trivial, legally weaker than it looks.** On hang-up: SHA-256 of the audio plus metadata (both numbers, SIM names, Raast titles, times, consent timestamps, read-back transcript), signed with Sendqon's key, stored in write-once (WORM) storage with a hash-chained log; any later alteration breaks the hash. But: (i) a self-issued seal is at best an ordinary *electronic signature*, carrying **no statutory presumption** (ETO s.9 reserves it to *advanced* signatures — (d)); and (ii) **no publicly operating trusted timestamping authority (RFC 3161 TSA) in Pakistan was found** [unverified — searched ECAC/NIFT/vendor sources; ECAC describes establishing a national PKI/Root CA but publishes no operational public TSA]. Sendqon's timestamp is self-asserted time, corroborated by artefacts to accumulate deliberately: SMPP delivery receipts, CDR/HLR records, and daily anchoring of a Merkle root of all seals into an external timestamped medium. None creates a presumption; all help prove the system was "in working order" (QSO Arts. 46-A, 73, 78-A).

**3. The ECAC/NIFT route — the one external dependency worth pricing.** ETO s.2(e)(ii) gives "advanced" status to a signature "provided by an accredited certification service provider and accredited by the Certification Council as being capable of establishing authenticity and integrity" [official]. The landscape is thin: **NIFT was the first — and, on ECAC's own 2020 account, the only — accredited CSP**, renewal then in process [official — ECAC via ProPakistani, 16 Nov 2020]; ECAC later issued NIFT a show-cause notice, with reports accreditation was withdrawn [industry — PhoneWorld et al., ~2022]. **NIFT's status at November 2026 could not be confirmed from a current ECAC list** — check directly at contracting time. A CSP countersignature is thus the only known route to the s.9 presumption, via a near-monopoly counterparty of murky status: no launch dependency. Design the seal so one can be added later without changing the user flow.

**4. SIM identity binding — strong anchor, honest limits.** Since the Biometric Verification System (PTA/NADRA, 2014–15; Multi-Finger BVS since Dec 2022), every SIM sale, duplicate, ownership change and port requires a live fingerprint match against NADRA; a CNIC may hold at most 8 SIMs (5 voice + 3 data) [official — PTA BVS framework; Subscribers Antecedents Verification Regulations 2015]. The binding proves something real: *the number that spoke is registered, after biometric verification, to a named CNIC holder* who bears initial legal responsibility for it. Limits to design for: a match proves a finger touched a scanner, not that the owner holds the phone now — PTA fined all operators a cumulative ~Rs 740 million in July 2026 over shop-counter identity failures [industry — TechBullion, Jul 2026]; family members routinely use each other's SIMs; and Sendqon, a non-operator, cannot query NADRA. Mitigations: each party states their full name on the recording; the certificate goes to *both* numbers so a non-participating holder can repudiate immediately ((d)); the binding is described as *attribution evidence*, never identity proof.

**5. Raast account-title check — an independent second anchor.** Raast (SBP's instant payment system; Raast ID = mobile number linked to one account) returns the beneficiary's **account title from core-banking records before a transfer is confirmed** [official — SBP Raast design across bank apps; bank FAQs instruct verifying the displayed title]. Sendqon's Raast plugin already performs title verification (per the brief). Reused at Gawah time — each party optionally confirms the Raast ID on the same number; the fetched title enters the seal — it cross-checks the SIM name against a *bank-KYC* name from a different institution. Two registries agreeing is materially stronger attribution; a mismatch is itself Bureau signal. Limit: many target users hold only a wallet or no account — the Raast anchor must be an enhancement grade, never a gate excluding the unbanked.

**6. Certificate and SMS/IVR verification — feasible now; the feature-phone path is the point.** The certificate is a short code (e.g., `GW-` + 8 characters) plus a hash fragment, delivered to both parties by SMS via Jasmin/own gateway and as WhatsApp/PDF where available. Verification mirrors PTA's 668 pattern: SMS the code or call the IVR and receive date/time, both names (masked numbers), the terms summary, and "record intact — hash verified." Court/mediator playback is a controlled, logged function (both-party consent or court order — (d)). Storage is undemanding (~1–2 MB per 5-minute segment; a million records a year is low single-digit TB). Call minutes, not storage, are the cost driver — see (e).

**Feasibility verdict:** everything exists on the stated stack except a trusted third-party timestamp and an accredited-CSP countersignature — legal-weight upgrades, not functional blockers. Engineering risk is low; the risk sits entirely in (d).


### (d) Legal and regulatory exposure — the decisive section

*Research, not legal advice — it scopes the mandated formal opinion, which must answer five questions (end of section) before any paying customer is sealed.*

**D1. Is the underlying promise enforceable? Mostly yes — with one hard exclusion.** Oral contracts are valid under the Contract Act 1872 (ss. 9, 10) [official]. Two carve-outs bind scope. First, immovable property: sale requires a registered instrument (Transfer of Property Act 1882), and Punjab's post-2015 regime requires a registered deed with biometric verification for a sale mutation [official]. Second, decisively for the *electronic* record, **ETO s.31(1)(e): nothing in the Ordinance applies to "a contract for sale or conveyance of immovable property or any interest in such property"** [official]; s.31 also excludes negotiable instruments (a), powers of attorney (b), trusts (c) and wills (d). Consequences: (i) the concept's "land deals" are **out of scope** — no ETO recognition, and marketing for that use invites misrepresentation exposure; (ii) no styling as a promissory-note substitute; (iii) **rent is different** — a tenancy is not a sale/conveyance and rental agreements are treated as e-signable [industry — Dastkhat, Nov 2026]; but Sindh's urban tenancy statute requires writing (Sindh Rented Premises Ordinance 1979, s.5) [official], so there the record is supporting evidence, not a replacement agreement. The opinion must confirm the rent line province by province.

**D2. Will a court admit the recording, and what will it demand? Admissible in principle; the bar is a 21-point checklist, and Gawah must be engineered against it.** The ETO inserted into the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order 1984 (QSO): **Article 46-A** (electronic statements recorded by an automated information system in working order are relevant facts); **Article 73, Explanations 3–4** (system output is not denied primary-evidence status for being electronic, with a presumption the system was in working order; output to which a *security procedure* was applied is treated as primary evidence); **Article 78-A** (where an electronic document/signature is denied, the security procedure must be proved) [official — QSO as amended by the ETO Schedule]. **Article 164**, recast by the Criminal Laws (Amendment) Act 2023 toward court-recorded evidence, was read by the Lahore High Court in *Raja Shahid v. The State* (2026 LHC 7319) as now mainly procedural, admissibility flowing from ETO s.3 plus Arts. 46-A/73/78-A [official — LHC judgment, Nov 2026; single-bench reading]. The operative test remains **Ishtiaq Ahmed Mirza v. Federation of Pakistan (PLD 2019 SC 675)** — the Arshad Malik case — consolidating **21 requirements** for audio/video evidence [official]. Those shaping Gawah's design: **proved genuine, not tampered with**; accuracy proved; the **actual record as and when made**; the **person recording produced**, producing it himself; **played in court**, **clearly audible**; **voices identified**; **safe custody proved**; **transcript under independent supervision**; **source and date disclosed**; and the recorder one whose **routine duty** is recording — *not* someone who recorded "for the purpose of laying a trap." Read as a specification, Gawah scores unusually well *by design*: a neutral platform whose routine business is recording; both parties consenting; hash-chained WORM custody; a seal-time transcript. Residual exposures: Sendqon must **appear and produce** records in customers' disputes (price this into the terms), and voice identification will still often rest on admission or a recognising witness. A Punjab Forensic Science Agency report is *per se* admissible under s.9(3), PFSA Act 2007 [official — per PLD 2019 SC 675], so the dispute flow needs a paid PFSA route.

**D3. What weight does Sendqon's own seal carry? Less than the concept implies — the central legal finding.** ETO s.3: no document shall be denied legal recognition, admissibility, effect or enforceability "on the ground that it is in electronic form" [official]. Ss. 4–8 deem writing, original-form and signature requirements satisfied by compliant electronic forms; s.8 lets an ordinary electronic signature be *proved in any manner* — attribution and intent are questions of fact [official — ETO ss. 3–8]. The **presumption** — document authentic, integrity intact, signature the signer's affixed with intent — is reserved by **s.9 to advanced electronic signatures** [official], defined in s.2(e) as either (i) unique to the signer, identifying them, created by means under their *sole control*, later change detectable, or (ii) provided by an ECAC-accredited CSP and accredited as capable of establishing authenticity and integrity [official]. A Gawah seal fails both limbs: the key is under **Sendqon's** control, not each signer's sole control (i), and Sendqon is not an accredited CSP (ii). ECAC's public position — accredited-CSP certificates are "the only digital certificates acceptable in courts of law" for authenticity purposes [official — ECAC statements 2018–2020] — overstates s.3 (unaccredited records remain *admissible*; they carry no presumption and must be proved ordinarily, per Art. 78-A), but signals how an opposing argument would be framed. **Bottom line:** a Gawahi certificate is *evidence a court may admit and weigh*, engineered to satisfy PLD 2019 SC 675 — **not** a presumptively authentic instrument. Any "court-proof" or "legally binding certificate" claim would be misleading and is launch-blocking. Only an accredited-CSP countersignature ((c)(3)) changes this paragraph, and that counterparty situation is murky.

**D4. Consent to recording — not a clean one-party jurisdiction; announced all-party consent is a legal necessity, not a courtesy.** No statute says in plain words that a participant may record their own call. What exists: **PECA 2016 s.19** criminalises *unauthorised interception* of a non-public transmission, with **dishonest intention** — up to 2 years and/or Rs 500,000 [official]; **Telecommunication (Re-organization) Act 1996, s.31** penalises intercepting/disclosing telecom contents without authority (up to 3 years and/or Rs 10 million on summaries reviewed) [official]; **Article 14 of the Constitution** (dignity; privacy of home), applied to phone tapping in *Benazir Bhutto v. President* (PLD 1998 SC 388) [official]. In **March 2026 the Supreme Court (Kakar J.) upheld an acquittal resting on a clandestine recording**, holding secret recording without consent — there, to lay a trap — criminal under Art. 14 and PECA, and recordings lacking a verified source/owner inadmissible [official — judgment via ProPakistani/Express Tribune, 10 Mar 2026; a secondary site's "PLJ 2026 SC 114" citation was unverifiable and is not relied on]. Practitioner sources now describe Pakistan as effectively **all-party consent** [industry — RecordingLaw.com, 2026; secondary, indicative]. Gawah's design — loud Urdu announcement, recording only after affirmative consent from *both* parties, consent itself on the record — is precisely the posture this jurisprudence rewards, and answers the "trap" objection. Rules: **no consent, no recording** (IVR aborts and logs it); **no silent retention** of pre-consent audio. Record-first-ask-later must be technically impossible in the build.

**D5. The name problem: "notarisation" is legally unavailable.** Under the **Notaries Ordinance, 1961**, a notary is *appointed by the provincial government* (s.3), registered with a certificate of practice (ss. 4–5), exercising s.8 functions — attesting instruments, administering oaths, noting protests — under an official seal (s.7); s.9 bars practising without a certificate [official]. A private company cannot become a notary by branding; anything *called* "notarisation" (or "SIM-notarised," the concept's subtitle) misrepresents a statutory office and hands opposing counsel a free credibility attack on the genuine evidence underneath. What it **can** be called is the concept's better half: **Gawah/Gawahi** — a *witness* record ("recorded verbal agreement service"). The certificate must state plainly, in Urdu and English, what it is and is not: a tamper-evident record of a consented conversation between two identified numbers, producible as electronic evidence — **not** a notarial act, registered deed, or government certificate. A naming condition, not a product-killer; "gawah" is culturally stronger than "notary" here anyway.

**D6. Storing voices: PECA exposure today, data-protection exposure tomorrow.** Voice recordings are biometric-adjacent, highly sensitive data; PECA ss. 3–4 and s.19 create criminal exposure around their misuse [official — PECA generally; application to a storage breach is for the opinion]. There is **no enacted data-protection statute**: the **Personal Data Protection Bill 2023** (MoITT, May 2023; Cabinet-approved July 2023) **has not been passed by either House** and remains a draft at late 2026 [official — MoITT status via Senate/committee reporting]. The draft would treat voice/biometric data as sensitive, require consent-based processing, create a National Commission, localise critical data, and propose penalties up to 1% of Pakistan gross revenue / US$2 million in specified cases [official — draft summaries; **proposed, not law**]. Build to the *draft* now — in-Pakistan storage (already SECP-mandated for lending data [official — Circular 12 of 2024]), consent artefacts, purpose limitation, breach runbook. Retention is a legal choice the opinion must fix (proposed for testing: 6 years for metadata/transcript, shorter and extendable for raw audio [planning assumption]).

**D7. Chain of custody — the answers Gawah must give under oath, each as a document, not an assurance:** (1) *Who recorded?* The platform, in the ordinary course, on a bridge it controlled — never a party's handset. (2) *Consent?* Announcement and both affirmative responses at the head of the same audio file; aborts logged. (3) *Integrity?* Hash at hang-up, WORM storage, hash-chain, external anchoring, logged re-verification. (4) *Time?* NTP-disciplined clocks plus SMS receipts/CDRs. (5) *Identity?* SIM names, on-record self-identification, optional Raast title — each labelled with source and limits. (6) *Custody?* Unbroken attributable access log; every playback/export authorised and logged. (7) *Production?* A named officer able to testify to (1)–(6), a standard evidence pack, the PFSA route for contested voices. Fail any item and the record's value collapses to that of any phone recording — the March 2026 acquittal fact-pattern. This checklist, counsel-signed, *is* the pilot's real deliverable.


**The five questions the formal opinion must answer (the gate):** (1) On the D2 authorities, is a platform-recorded, all-party-consented record admissible, and what weight should a district court give it, by agreement type (udhaar/loans, wages, rent, goods)? (2) Confirm the s.31 exclusions in D1, province by province for tenancy. (3) Confirm the seal is an ordinary electronic signature with no s.9 presumption, and specify lawful marketing language. (4) Confirm the consent flow satisfies PECA s.19 / Telecom Act s.31 / Art. 14, including abort and repudiation flows. (5) Confirm the name, certificate wording and disclaimers avoid the Notaries Ordinance 1961 and any holding-out of public office, and fix retention against limitation periods and the draft PDPB. An opinion answering "it depends" to (1) or "no" to (4) stops the product; no pilot begins until the opinion is in hand.

### (e) Unit economics

**What the market can bear — anchored to substitutes.** Rs 500 buys the state's taxed paper (drafting, travel, witnesses extra); Rs 12,000–35,000 buys a lawyer; Rs 0 buys the parchi. Above ~Rs 500, users will compare Gawah unfavourably with "stamp paper" — the only priced artefact they know — even though it does more. Price architecture [planning assumption — our judgement; no willingness-to-pay survey exists]: **retail Rs 499** per certificate (one party pays; both receive it); **tenant-bundled Rs 299** in volume packs (schools, clinics, e-commerce sellers — fee-commitment calls, supplier udhaar, salary advances); **verification free** for the record's life (charging to check would destroy its network value); **dispute evidence pack Rs 4,999** [planning assumption] — transcript, custody extract, officer statement, PFSA handling — against the Rs 10,000–25,000 legal-notice alternative, charged only when a dispute happens and willingness to pay peaks.

**Marginal cost per sealed agreement.** Anchors: bulk/transactional SMS retails at **Rs 3.80–4.80/message** [industry — SendPK rate card, Nov 2026; Pak Sender Rs 4.30]; PTA prepaid voice is **Rs 2.68–3.60/minute** before tax [official — PTA data via TechJuice, Sep 2026]. ### (f) Smallest honest pilot

**Gate zero:** the formal opinion answering (d)'s five questions, from a Pakistani firm with evidence-law and telecom practice, briefed on the *Notaries Ordinance 1961* (not the Indian Act of 1952), PLD 2019 SC 675 and the March 2026 judgment. No build beyond paper prototypes, no customer, no marketing claim precedes it; a narrower opinion means a narrower pilot.

**City (one): Faisalabad** — a trade hub whose parchi markets are documented in (a), dense enough to onboard 15–25 wholesalers in person, in the province (Punjab) whose e-stamp, PFSA and court-fee facts this report verified most completely. (Karachi is the second city: Sindh specifics add legal surface the pilot doesn't need.)

**Shape:** 90 days; 15–25 wholesalers via existing tenant/channel relationships; retailers consent per call (no standing-consent fiction); certificates free during the pilot (price tested separately); the evidence-pack function staffed and timed; all records built to D7; independent counsel reviews 20 sealed records against PLD 2019 SC 675 at day 45, reporting gaps in writing.

**Success criteria (all four must pass):** **(1) Completion** — ≥60% of initiated calls reach a sealed certificate; below that, the flow, not the market, is presumed broken. **(2) Behavioural truth** — sealed deals show measurably better on-time payment than the same wholesalers' unsealed deals (target ≥10 points), *or* ≥3 disputes settled using the record without court. **(3) Price truth** — at day 60, real-money pricing is offered; success = ≥40% convert at Rs 299+ effective price. Payment is measured, not enthusiasm. **(4) Legal truth** — counsel's day-45 review finds zero launch-blocking D7 defects and no consent/repudiation incident the logs cannot explain.

**Deliberately not tested:** land/plot deals (excluded, D1), tenancy (phase 2, after the province-wise opinion), consumer one-off lending (no repeat economics), and any "notary" language anywhere (prohibited, D5).

### Verdict & conditions

**GO WITH CONDITIONS — for a narrowed product: a consent-recorded, SIM- and Raast-anchored, tamper-evident *witness record* of spoken bargains in goods, credit, wages and rent — never a notarisation, never for land-sale promises, never marketed as court-proof.**

The core insight is strongly supported: the law validates the oral bargain, then demands exactly the proof (*Muhammad Riaz*, 2021 SCMR 605 — date, time, place, parties, witnesses, terms) that a bridged, consent-announced, sealed recording manufactures as a by-product; alternatives cost Rs 500 to Rs 35,000 inside a system carrying 2.2 million pending cases; no live competitor records the spoken deal; and every component except a trusted third-party timestamp already exists on Sendqon's stack. The conditions are hard — each unmet condition converts this verdict to NO-GO for the affected scope:

1. **Legal-opinion gate first.** The (d) five-question opinion is obtained and acted on before any customer build or pilot. An adverse answer on admissibility (Q1) or consent (Q4) is a NO-GO, not a redesign prompt.
2. **Name and claims discipline.** The product is *Gawah/Gawahi* — a witness record. "Notary/notarised/notarisation" in any language, and any claim of court-proof status, statutory presumption, or equivalence to a registered deed, are prohibited in product, certificate and marketing. The certificate carries the D5 Urdu/English "what this is / is not" statement.
3. **Scope exclusions enforced in the product, not just the terms:** no immovable-property sale/conveyance contracts (ETO s.31(1)(e)); nothing styled as a negotiable instrument, power of attorney, trust or will (s.31(1)(a)–(d)). The IVR itself screens out land-sale promises. Tenancy only after D1's province-wise confirmation.
4. **Consent engineering as specified:** announced Urdu consent; affirmative agreement from *both* parties on the record before the promise segment; recording without it technically impossible; abort logging; a repudiation flow — notice to both numbers, objection attached to the record, never silently deleted.
5. **Evidence engineering to the D7 checklist**, counsel-signed against PLD 2019 SC 675: platform-side recording only; WORM + hash-chain + external anchoring; seal-time transcript; custody logs; a named officer able to produce and testify; a priced PFSA route. The seal is described everywhere as an ordinary electronic signature (ETO s.8) with **no s.9 presumption** unless condition 6 is met.
6. **Accredited-CSP upgrade kept open, never pre-claimed:** verify ECAC's *current* list directly at contracting time (NIFT's status was unconfirmable at research time); if an accredited countersignature becomes contractible at a per-seal cost Rs 499 can absorb, adopt it — and only then upgrade the certificate's legal description, with counsel's sign-off.
7. **Data protection to the draft PDPB 2023 standard now:** in-Pakistan storage, purpose limitation, counsel-fixed retention, controlled logged playback (both-party consent or court order), breach runbook.
8. **Pilot exactly as (f):** one segment (wholesale udhaar), one city (Faisalabad), 90 days, four pass and four kill criteria, price tested with real money at day 60. Scale and external use of the Bureau feed wait for the pilot's written results.

If conditions 1–5 are met, what remains is honest and first in its market: not a notary in every pocket — illegal for anyone to sell — but a *gawah* in every pocket, which is what Pakistan's spoken economy has been using, and losing for want of proof, all along.

## #6 Insaan Check — Proof-of-Human for the Voice-Clone Age

**Verdict up front: GO WITH CONDITIONS — build the ceremony, not the voiceprint.** The mid-call challenge that combines SIM possession, a random-phrase readback and a pre-agreed code-word is buildable today on Sendqon's Asterisk + SIM-gateway stack, has no direct competitor in Pakistan, and answers a fraud pattern that is already documented by PTA, SBP and the FBI. Passive voiceprint matching, sold on its own as "we recognise your customer's voice", does **not** survive the evidence: on clean wideband audio the best open-source engine errs roughly 7 times in 1,000, on the harder, codec-degraded, adversarial test that resembles a real attack it errs roughly 86 times in 1,000, and a targeted voice clone is *designed* to be accepted by exactly that matcher. Insaan Check is therefore viable only if it is marketed and engineered as a layered human-ceremony API — in which the voiceprint is the weakest, most replaceable leg — and only if voice templates are never pooled across tenants. The shared-infrastructure claim in the original concept is the product's best commercial idea and its worst legal idea at the same time; §(d) resolves that tension by sharing the *ceremony and the verdict*, never the biometric.

**Grading key used in this section:** **[official]** = primary source (regulator, standards/challenge organiser, vendor's own pricing or product page); **[industry]** = published by market participants, researchers, analysts or reputable press; **[unverified]** = claimed but not independently confirmed; **[planning assumption]** = Sendqon's own internal estimate, not market data. Where no published figure exists, this section says so. FX throughout: **PKR 277 = USD 1**.

The concept under research, verbatim: *"STIR/SHAKEN proves a number is real; nothing proves the human is. As voice cloning explodes, any tenant can challenge any caller mid-call: SIM possession + voiceprint + a family/business code-word ceremony, returning a live verdict — 'insaan hai, wahi hai'. A cross-business human-authentication layer over the ordinary phone network, sold as an API; banks do voice biometrics inside their own walls, nobody sells it as shared infrastructure in Pakistan. Pairs with #5 (Machine Exchange): in a world of machine callers, proof-of-human becomes a billable primitive."*

Two premises in that paragraph need correcting before anything else, because they change the go-to-market. First, STIR/SHAKEN does not operate in Pakistan at all (see §(b)): there is no cryptographic caller attestation on Pakistani mobile calls today, so Insaan Check is not filling a gap *next to* STIR/SHAKEN — it is filling a larger, emptier gap, which is good for differentiation and bad for any assumption that the number leg can be trusted. Second, Pakistani banks **do** already run voice biometrics inside their own walls — Bank Alfalah enrols a CNIC-tagged voiceprint over its helpline today **[official]**, and Bank AL Habib announced VB Verify voice biometrics in its new contact-centre stack in June 2025 **[industry]**. The owner's second clause is therefore the accurate one: the technology exists in Pakistan, the *shared, API-sold, cross-business* form of it does not. That distinction is the whole opportunity, and the whole procurement problem.

### (a) Problem and payer — Pakistan first

#### The global loss picture, graded

Voice-clone fraud is no longer anecdotal, but the headline numbers need to be handled carefully because three different things are routinely added together: all AI-enabled fraud, all imposter scams, and the much smaller subset that is specifically a cloned-voice call.

- The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) recorded **22,364 complaints with an AI component in 2025, with roughly $893 million in reported losses**, the first year IC3 published AI as a formal descriptor **[official — FBI IC3 2025 report, as reported]**. Within that total, distress / "family in trouble" scams using voice cloning accounted for **over $5 million** in reported losses **[official]**, and adults aged 60+ carried about **$352 million** of the AI-related losses **[official]**. The $893 million figure must not be quoted as "voice-clone losses": most of it is AI-assisted investment and impersonation fraud across channels. The honest voice-specific floor from the same source is the $5 million distress-scam line — a floor, because IC3 counts only what victims report and many victims never realise AI was involved.
- The best-known consumer survey remains McAfee's *Beware the Artificial Impostor* (2023, 7,054 adults across seven countries): **1 in 4 adults had experienced an AI voice scam or knew someone who had, 1 in 10 had been targeted personally, and 77% of victims lost money** **[industry]**. The same study popularised the "three seconds of audio" claim — that a short clip could produce a voice match its testers rated at around 85% similarity **[industry]**. Both figures are from 2023, before the current generation of real-time cloning tools; treat them as a baseline that has almost certainly worsened, not as a current measurement.
- In the UK, Starling Bank's 2024 survey (3,010 adults) found **28% of adults said they had been targeted by a voice-cloning scam in the previous year, while 46% had never heard of the scam type** **[industry]**.
- Pindrop reports a **1,300% rise in deepfake fraud attempts against call centres in 2024, from roughly one a month to seven a day** across its customer base **[industry — vendor-measured, not independently audited]**. Vendor telemetry is directionally useful and commercially interested at the same time; it is graded accordingly.
- Deloitte projects US generative-AI-enabled fraud losses reaching **$40 billion by 2027**, a 32% compound growth rate from $12.3 billion in 2023 **[industry — forecast, not outcome]**.
- The canonical single-incident case is still Arup (Hong Kong, early 2024): an employee joined a video call in which the other participants were deepfakes and transferred **about HK$200 million (≈ $25.6 million)** **[industry — widely reported, police-confirmed case]**. It was a video call, not a phone call, but it is the case every board now cites, and it is the case World (see §(b)) uses to sell proof-of-human to banks.

The pattern across all of these sources is consistent even where the totals are not: the attack is cheap (seconds of source audio, consumer tools), it targets urgency and kinship rather than technical defences, and human listeners are poor detectors — University College London work cited across the literature puts unaided human detection of deepfake speech at roughly **73%** **[industry]**, barely better than the decision a frightened parent makes at midnight.

#### Pakistan-specific evidence — what is actually reported, and what is not

This research found **no published Pakistan-specific loss total for voice-clone fraud, and no official FIA/NCCIA or SBP statistic that isolates cloned-voice cases**. Any report that quotes a Pakistani rupee figure for "AI voice fraud" is extrapolating. What *is* published is the surrounding fraud environment, and it is bad enough to make the threat credible without inventing a number:

- **PTA's January 2026 advisory** warns that fraudsters spoof UAN (111-style) numbers to impersonate **PTA, FIA/NCCIA, banks, JazzCash and Easypaisa**, claiming a SIM, bank account or wallet will be blocked, and asking for **OTPs, CNIC details, PINs, passwords and biometric information**; PTA states no genuine organisation asks for any of these on a call or message **[official — PTA advisory, 7–16 January 2026]**. This matters for Insaan Check in two ways: the impersonation pattern is officially recognised, and the regulator's own public advice ("never share codes on a call") will collide with any product that asks a user to speak a code on a call — see the design constraint in §(f).
- **SBP and the banking sector** have repeatedly warned against fake bank calls and messages asking for PINs, passwords and OTPs, and since 2023 SBP has required banks to maintain digital-fraud risk-management units, with banks liable to compensate customers where the bank's delay in blocking a channel caused the loss **[industry — reported SBP framework]**. In February 2026 SBP launched *Cyber Shield*, a cyber-resilience strategy for banks running to 2030 **[industry]**.
- Reported aggregate scam harm is large even on conservative counting: over the last three years **around 227,000 people formally reported being defrauded, with reported losses of Rs 8.32 billion (≈ $30 million)**, and in 2024 the Banking Mohtasib resolved **about 28,000 digital-fraud complaints, recovering about Rs 1.65 billion** **[industry — The News, citing official reports, November 2026]**. The Global Anti-Scam Alliance / Feedzai *Global State of Scams Report 2025* estimate that Pakistan loses **about 2.5% of GDP to scams annually** is also in circulation **[industry — modelled estimate, not a measured loss account]**; it should be used, if at all, as context, never as an addressable-market input.
- Coverage of the FIA Cyber Crime Wing's 2025–26 reporting states **online fraud complaints in Pakistan rose about 40% year-on-year, with most cases involving fake banking calls and WhatsApp job scams**, and notes INTERPOL's 2026 trend reporting on rising AI-voice scams across South Asia imitating relatives to demand emergency payments **[industry — The Nation coverage via FraudGuard summary, October 2026]**. Pakistani public-awareness material on the specific "beta in trouble" pattern — a call in a son's cloned voice claiming an accident and demanding an immediate transfer, with advice to hang up, call back on the saved number, and use a **family code word** — is now circulating in Urdu/Roman-Urdu social content aimed at Pakistani families, citing the NCCIA helpline **1799** and complaint portal **[industry — awareness content, November 2026, not a case register]**. The awareness exists; a verified Pakistani case series with amounts does not, in any source this research could find. The nearest fully reported family case in that same material is from Hyderabad, **India** (March 2025, a homemaker losing ₹1.97 lakh after hearing a cloned confirmation) **[industry]** — it must not be relabelled as a Pakistani case.
- The honest summary for Pakistan is therefore: **the preconditions are all documented** (spoofed official numbers, fake bank/wallet calls at scale, OTP harvesting, rising complaint volumes, a population that banks by phone and pays by Raast/JazzCash/Easypaisa), **the voice-clone variant is officially anticipated and publicly warned about, and the local loss accounting for that variant does not yet exist**. A pitch that claims otherwise will be caught by the first competent bank risk officer who reads it. A pitch that says "your customers are already being called by people pretending to be you; the voice is the next thing they will fake, and here is the ceremony that stops it" is fully supported.

Banks, despite being the largest theoretical market, are the *last* realistic buyer — a fact the concept's framing ("banks do it inside their own walls") already implies: institutions that build walls do not rent a shared gate from a startup until the gate is proven elsewhere.

### (b) Competitors and substitutes — with live evidence

#### The voice-biometrics / fraud vendors

All five named vendors exist, all sell primarily to large contact centres, and — this is the commercially decisive fact — **none of them publishes a self-serve per-call price**. Every engagement is an enterprise contract. That pricing opacity is itself evidence: the category is sold, not bought.

- **Pindrop (US, founded 2011)** sells three engines: *Protect* (real-time call risk scoring), *Passport* (passive multifactor caller authentication, intended to replace knowledge questions and OTPs in the call centre) and *Pulse* (deepfake / synthetic-voice detection, launched February 2024), plus newer meeting/bot products **[official — vendor product pages, summarised in industry vendor profiles]**. Named customers include First National Bank of Omaha and M&T Bank **[industry]**. Pindrop publishes no list price on its own site; its **AWS Marketplace listing shows 12-month contract prices of $50,000 (Call Verification), $250,000 (Anti-Fraud), $250,000 (Caller Authentication) and $300,000 (Anti-Fraud + Deepfake Detection), each scoped to up to 1,000,000 calls** **[official — AWS Marketplace listing, as reported October 2026]**. At full utilisation that is **$0.05–$0.30 per call (Rs 14–83)** — a useful per-call anchor — but the *entry ticket* is $50,000 (**Rs 13.85 million**), which excludes every Pakistani business except a handful of banks and telcos before the conversation starts.
- **Nuance Gatekeeper (Microsoft)** is the incumbent enterprise voice-biometrics and fraud product, sold as part of Microsoft's contact-centre / customer-engagement stack after the $19.7 billion Nuance acquisition (2022) **[industry]**. Pricing is enterprise/quote-only **[industry — no published per-call rate found]**.
- **Veridas (Spain)** sells voice + face identity verification with anti-spoofing, positioned for digital onboarding and OEM/platform licensing **[industry]**. Pricing is enterprise/OEM, not published **[industry]**.
- **ID R&D (Mitek)** supplies voice-matching and liveness as SDKs/components embedded in other vendors' products **[industry]** — a reminder that in this market the engine is often a component, not the product the bank thinks it bought. Pricing not published **[industry]**.
- **Auraya (Australia)** sells the EVA / ArmorVox engine for text-dependent and text-independent voice verification, licensed to integrators, banks and telcos **[industry]**. Pricing not published **[industry]**.

Two conclusions follow. First, **there is no published per-call price for dedicated voice biometrics anywhere in this vendor set except the Pindrop Marketplace arithmetic above** — any competitor price quoted more precisely than that range is invented. Second, none of these vendors sells a *Pakistani, PKR-priced, self-serve API that a school, clinic or 200-employee e-commerce company can integrate in a week*. The competitive gap is not accuracy; it is distribution, price point and jurisdiction.

#### Do Pakistani banks run voice biometrics today? Yes — verified

- **Bank Alfalah** operates a live voice-biometrics service: a customer calls the helpline (**021-111-225-111**), a phone banking officer verifies them, the system records a voiceprint in **about 20–25 seconds**, tags it to the customer's **CNIC**, and thereafter authenticates the caller by voice on the helpline; enrolment requires calling from the registered mobile number and is limited to individual account / credit-card holders **[official — Bank Alfalah product page and FAQs]**.
- **Bank AL Habib** announced in June 2025, with C Square and wAI Industries, a Genesys Engage contact-centre ecosystem in which **VB Verify's voice biometrics provide customer authentication**, described as Pakistan's first AI-powered contact-centre ecosystem **[industry — ProPakistani, June 2025]**. "Announced" is graded as such; this research did not independently verify live enrolment volumes.
- **United Bank Limited (UBL)** selected VoiceTrust's VTAssure platform in **2014** to authenticate retail/enterprise customers and pensioners by voice, including a spoken-passphrase "Proof-of-Life" for pensioners, with English and Urdu support **[industry — vendor press release, 2014]**. Its current production status is **[unverified]**.
- **Standard Chartered** announced in **2016** a rollout of voice + fingerprint verification including Pakistan **[industry — APP via UrduPoint, 2016]**; current status **[unverified]**.
- **Easypaisa / Telenor Microfinance Bank** trialled voice-enabled IVR authentication **in Urdu** under GSMA's Biometrics for All (B4LL) programme, with favourable pilot feedback and adoption under consideration as of GSMA's 2022 mobile-money report **[industry — GSMA via Business Recorder]**.

So the concept's market observation survives verification in its precise form: **voice biometrics in Pakistan is real, bank-owned, siloed, and invisible to every non-bank business.** No Pakistani bank offers its voiceprint capability to third parties, and no third party offers a shared one. That is the white space.

#### NADRA (Verisys / BVS) — the state substitute, and its hard boundary

NADRA is the authoritative identity verifier for Pakistan: SBP requires NADRA verification for bank account opening and mobile-wallet enrolment, and regulated institutions call NADRA's **Verisys** channel to confirm that a CNIC number matches a name, attributes or a biometric sample, receiving a match / no-match response without access to the underlying database **[industry — integration guides describing SBP/SECP requirements]**. The dominant live modality is **fingerprint BVS** (Biometric Verification System): a reader captures the fingertip, a template is matched against the CNIC record, and payment or service proceeds only on a match — the mechanism behind BISP payments, SIM issuance and bank KYC **[industry/official — programme documentation]**. From **20 January 2026**, NADRA also issues **facial-recognition-based biometric verification certificates** (iris now also legally recognised) for citizens whose fingerprints fail, at a nominal **Rs 20** fee, valid **seven days**, obtained by attending a NADRA registration centre (and via Pak-ID for services in NADRA's own jurisdiction) **[official — Dawn report of NADRA statement, January 2026]**.

What NADRA proves is therefore exact and narrow: **that a live person, physically present at a reader or centre (or operating an enrolled app flow), matches the state's record for a CNIC, at that moment.** What it cannot do is the thing Insaan Check exists to do:

1. It cannot authenticate a voice on an ordinary phone call — there is no NADRA voiceprint service, and no API by which a tenant can ask NADRA "is the person speaking on this call the holder of CNIC X?" **[not published / not found — no such service is offered in any NADRA material reviewed]**.
2. Its strongest flows are in-person or agent-assisted; a remote caller on a feature phone has no fingerprint reader and no Pak-ID app.
3. It proves *identity against the state record*, not *intent or authority in this conversation* — it cannot know that the genuine account holder is being coerced, is reading a script a fraudster gave them, or is not the person who set the business's refund policy. A code-word ceremony set by the family/business itself answers a different question ("does this caller know what only we know?") that the state, by design, never asks.

Insaan Check must therefore never be sold as a NADRA alternative for regulated KYC — legally it cannot be one (see §(d)) — and always as the layer NADRA does not provide: **in-call, remote, relationship-specific assurance.**

#### Twilio Verify / OTP — the cheap substitute, and why it is not enough

The incumbent substitute is the OTP. **Twilio Verify charges $0.05 per successful verification plus channel fees** — US SMS adds $0.0083 per message, for an all-in US figure of about **$0.058 (≈ Rs 16)** **[official — Twilio Verify pricing page, October 2026]**. Sendqon's own SMS/OTP over its SMPP and SIM-gateway routes can undercut the delivery leg, but the *security* comparison is what matters:

- An OTP proves **possession of a channel that can receive a code at that moment** — nothing about the human. It is routinely defeated by (i) **SIM swap** (the number is reissued to the fraudster's SIM; Pakistan's biometric SIM-issuance regime raises the bar but does not eliminate insider- and document-fraud-driven swaps, and PTA's own warnings treat SIM misuse as a live risk **[official — PTA advisories]**), (ii) **real-time social engineering** — the dominant Pakistani pattern in §(a), in which the *genuine* customer is talked into reading the OTP aloud to the impersonator, at which point every possession check passes perfectly, and (iii) **SS7 / aggregator leakage and SMS pumping** internationally **[industry]**.
- Insaan Check's ceremony is specifically stronger against (ii), the commonest local attack: a customer can be tricked into reading out a code *someone sent them*, but a challenge that requires them to state *their own pre-agreed code-word* and read back a *random phrase* gives the impersonator nothing reusable, and gives the genuine customer a moment in which the fraud script breaks — the impersonator on the other leg cannot supply a secret they never knew. This asymmetry, not biometrics, is the product's real security claim, and §(c) grades each leg accordingly.

#### Shared / cross-business human-authentication networks — do they exist anywhere?

For **voice**, this research found **none**: no network in any country in which a voiceprint enrolled with one business authenticates the same person at another business as a commercial service **[not published / not found]**. Banks silo voiceprints deliberately (see §(d) on why sharing them is legally and reputationally toxic).

The closest proof-of-human network in the world is not voice-based at all: **World (Worldcoin / Tools for Humanity)** enrols people via iris-scanning Orbs into **World ID**, and in October 2026 launched a banking/finance suite combining Proof of Human (one-time Orb verification of uniqueness), Proof of Presence (phone selfie liveness) and Proof of Attributes (government-ID claims), with design partners including Bain Capital and Pantera and a claimed **18 million+ Proof-of-Human holders** **[industry — company announcements via Decrypt/Biometric Update, October 2026]**. World proves *a unique live human exists behind this wallet* — it does **not** prove that the human is a *specific named person* known to the business, it requires Orb infrastructure and a smartphone app (no ordinary-phone-call path), no bank was among its named launch design partners **[industry]**, and its iris programme has drawn bans, fines and deletion orders in Spain, Kenya, Brazil, Hong Kong and South Korea **[industry]**. World validates the *category* ("proof-of-human becomes billable infrastructure in the deepfake age" — it explicitly cites the same Deloitte $40 billion AI-fraud forecast) while demonstrating, at great expense, exactly the centralised-biometric trap Insaan Check must avoid.

**Pairing with #5 (Machine Exchange):** the pairing logic in the concept is sound and worth stating precisely. #5 builds signed identity and authority for *machine* callers; every machine-mode call that succeeds increases the value of being able to prove the *opposite* — that this caller is a human, and this specific human. Insaan Check is #5's mirror primitive, and the two share the same registry, ceremony and settlement-record machinery. Neither requires the other to launch, but a tenant who buys both gets a complete answer to "who — or what — was on that call?"


### (c) Technical feasibility — the honest core

Insaan Check is three different checks wearing one name. They have completely different security properties, and conflating them is how voice-biometrics products get oversold. This subsection grades each leg separately, then assembles the only defensible combination.

#### Leg 1 — Passive voiceprint: what the numbers actually say

A voiceprint system enrols a speaker (typically 5–30 seconds of speech reduced to a 192–256-dimension embedding) and later scores a test utterance by embedding similarity. The standard metric is **EER (Equal Error Rate)** — the threshold at which false accepts equal false rejects. Lower is better; EER is *not* an accuracy percentage and must never be marketed as one.

- On the clean benchmark everyone quotes, the best-known open-source engine is genuinely good: **SpeechBrain's ECAPA-TDNN (`spkrec-ecapa-voxceleb`, Apache-2.0) achieves 0.69% EER on the VoxCeleb1 test set (cleaned)**, trained on VoxCeleb 1+2 **[official — SpeechBrain model card]**. Independent recipe documentation reports **0.80% EER with score normalisation** on the same split **[official — SpeechBrain recipes]**. VoxCeleb is, however, wideband (16 kHz) celebrity interview audio — about as far from a Pakistani mobile call as speech data gets while still being speech.
- The adversarial benchmark tells the other half of the story. **ASVspoof** is the field's vendor-independent anti-spoofing challenge series (replay, TTS and voice-conversion attacks). Its trajectory is the single most important evidence in this section: best-system EER went from **0.22% (2019)** to **15.64% on the ASVspoof 2021 deepfake task**, and the **ASVspoof 5 (2024) closed condition was won at 8.61% EER against a 29.12% baseline**, with 32 attack algorithms, ~2,000 speakers and, for the first time, adversarial attacks designed to fool the detector **[official — ASVspoof challenge papers, via challenge-results compilations]**. On ASVspoof 2021 deepfake *evaluation* data, **all 33 submitted systems exceeded 15% EER** even though 23 of them were under 10% on progress data **[industry — challenge analysis]**. Cross-dataset generalisation summaries put deepfake detection at roughly **95% AUC within-dataset falling to ~80% cross-dataset** **[industry]**. In plain language: a detector that looks near-perfect on the attacks it was trained on degrades to *wrong one time in six to one time in twelve* on new attacks, new speakers and new channels — which is the only condition production ever presents.
- The reason is structural, not a temporary model gap. A voice clone is *optimised against the same similarity the verifier measures*. A passive matcher asks "does this sound like the enrolled person?" and a good clone is, by construction, audio that answers yes. Anti-spoofing (liveness / synthetic-artifact detection) is a separate model asking a different question ("does this sound *generated*?"), it is in an arms race with the generators, and ASVspoof 5's inclusion of adversarial attacks is the field's admission that generators now train against detectors too. **No vendor-independent evaluation found in this research supports a claim that passive voiceprint + anti-spoofing, alone, reliably defeats a targeted real-time clone of a known victim.** Vendor claims of ~99% detection **[unverified — vendor-published, no independent replication found]** should be treated as marketing until reproduced on ASVspoof-style unseen attacks over telephone channels.
- **Open-source vs commercial:** SpeechBrain ECAPA-TDNN is the credible self-host path (Apache-2.0 code and weights, PyTorch, CPU-feasible inference for a single short utterance) **[official]**. **Resemblyzer** and similar lightweight embedding demos are materially weaker, unmaintained for production verification, and have no meaningful anti-spoofing **[industry — library documentation / community assessment]**; they are suitable for a demo and disqualifying for a fraud product. Commercial engines (Veridas, ID R&D, Auraya, Nuance) may beat SpeechBrain on telephony-tuned data — their training corpora include real call-centre audio, which is a genuine advantage — but none publishes ASVspoof-comparable telephony EERs that this research could verify **[not published / not found]**, so the honest planning figure for *any* engine on Pakistani mobile audio is the degraded range below, not the VoxCeleb headline.

#### The narrowband constraint — the fact that governs everything

A Pakistani mobile call is narrowband. The PSTN/mobile voice channel carries **300–3,400 Hz, sampled at 8 kHz** (Nyquist limit 4 kHz), then compressed by **AMR-NB (4.75–12.2 kbps)** or transcoded between codecs on multi-leg paths **[official/industry — telephony engineering literature]**. Consequences, each independently documented:

1. **The information is physically absent, not degraded.** Fricative energy (/s/, /f/) concentrates above 3,500 Hz and up to 10 kHz; at 8 kHz sampling none of it is captured, and upsampling to 16 kHz for a wideband model cannot regenerate it **[industry — telephony STT robustness analysis]**. Speaker-discriminative detail lives disproportionately in exactly those higher bands.
2. **Wideband-trained speaker-recognition models perform poorly on narrowband audio** due to train/test mismatch — a result the speaker-recognition literature treats as settled, with an entire bandwidth-extension research line devoted to mitigating (not eliminating) it **[official — Odyssey 2020 bandwidth-expansion study and related work]**.
3. **Humans degrade too:** in controlled spoofing-detection listening tests, **error rates on narrowband data roughly doubled versus wideband**, with 20% of narrowband human trials misclassified as artificial versus 12% at 16 kHz **[official — Interspeech 2015 human-vs-machine spoofing study]**.
4. AMR's parametric (ACELP) encoding resynthesises speech from vocal-tract parameters; it preserves intelligibility for humans while discarding waveform detail that both speaker embeddings and synthetic-artifact detectors rely on. Codec variation (AMR mode switches, transcoding, packet loss concealment on VoLTE/VoWiFi legs) makes the same speaker's embedding *move* from call to call.

**Planning consequence [planning assumption, derived from the evidence above]:** budget for a telephony voiceprint operating point in the region of **5–12% EER** on real Pakistani mobile audio until Sendqon's own pilot (§(f)) measures otherwise — i.e., at a threshold set for a 1% false-accept rate, expect double-digit false rejects of genuine, enrolled customers (ill, tired, in a rickshaw, on a different handset). Any user experience that *blocks* a customer on a voiceprint mismatch alone will punish the innocent at scale. The voiceprint can therefore only ever be a **risk signal that raises or lowers ceremony friction**, never a sole gate. This single finding determines the product architecture in §(f) and the verdict.

#### Leg 2 — Active challenge–response (random phrase readback): the leg that actually fights clones

Instead of passively listening, Asterisk's IVR prompts the caller to **read back a freshly generated random phrase** (e.g., a random 4-word / digit sequence, in Urdu, English or Roman-Urdu numerals, valid for seconds). This changes the attack in three provable ways:

- **Replay dies.** A recorded sample of the victim — the cheapest clone input and the entire basis of "play a voice note down the phone" — cannot contain a phrase generated after the recording was made.
- **Offline clones die; real-time clones are taxed.** The attacker must now synthesise the *specific phrase* in the victim's voice, in real time, inside the call. Real-time voice conversion exists, so this is a cost/latency/quality tax, not an impossibility — but it forces the attacker onto a live conversion pipeline whose output can additionally be checked for (i) correct phrase content via STT, (ii) voiceprint similarity, and (iii) conversion artefacts, simultaneously. Each check is mediocre; their *conjunction on an unpredictable input* is materially stronger than any one of them on free speech.
- **Liveness is inherent.** A live human reading a new phrase *is* a liveness proof in a way a passive sample never is.

What it does **not** stop: a well-resourced attacker running high-quality real-time conversion who has also obtained the code-word (Leg 3), or the genuine person being coerced into performing the ceremony themselves. No phone ceremony stops coercion; the design goal is to make the *impersonation* path fail, and to make the coercion path visible (recorded, logged, delayable) rather than silent.

#### Leg 3 — The code-word ceremony: a shared secret, and the strongest cheap leg

The code-word is not biometrics at all. It is a **knowledge factor agreed in advance inside a trusted relationship** — a family phrase set in person, a business phrase set by an account manager in the tenant panel — and produced on demand during the challenge. Its security properties are unfashionable and excellent:

- A voice clone, however perfect, **does not know it**. The entire generative-AI threat is irrelevant to this leg; the attacker must obtain the secret by social engineering, insider leak or guessing, all of which are rate-limitable, loggable and revocable (rotate the word — something you cannot do with a voice).
- It is **channel-independent**: it works identically on AMR narrowband, on a landline, through a bad connection, for a customer with a cold whose voiceprint has drifted. It has no EER.
- Its weaknesses are human and manageable: customers forget (mitigate: allow a small set of ceremony questions / a phrase chosen by the customer, plus a human fallback path that is *slower*, not a bypass); secrets leak inside families and offices (mitigate: per-relationship words, rotation on suspicion, never reuse the bank's existing security questions — the ceremony word must be *new* and used *only* for Insaan Check, or an attacker who phishes the bank's questions owns this too); and staff must never be able to view it in plaintext (store hashed, verify by hash, exactly like a password — see §(d)).

This is also the leg the public has already been taught: Pakistani and international family-safety advice for the "beta in trouble" scam converges on *exactly* this control — hang up, call back, ask for the family safe word **[industry — awareness guidance, §(a)]**. Insaan Check industrialises a behaviour regulators and families are already trying to adopt, which is a far easier sale than teaching the market a new biometric.

#### Leg 0 — SIM possession: what it proves, and its hole

Before any voice leg, the ceremony checks **possession of the enrolled SIM/number**: an inbound call from the enrolled number, or an outbound callback / SMS / flash-call / DTMF confirmation to it, executed over Sendqon's own SIM-gateway and SMPP routes. This proves **control of the number at that moment** — valuable, cheap, and native to Sendqon's stack (no third-party dependency, no per-check carrier API fee **[planning assumption — architecture]**). Its hole is equally clear: **SIM swap / fraudulent reissue** transfers that control to the attacker, after which the possession leg passes for the wrong person; and number recycling means enrolments must expire and re-bind. Mitigations available without carrier cooperation are partial — enrolment-age checks (a number enrolled yesterday gets a harder ceremony), velocity and device-change signals from the gateway app where present, and never letting SIM possession alone authorise anything. Where a tenant needs swap detection proper, that is a carrier/PTA-integrated signal Sendqon does not control today **[not published / not found — no public Pakistani carrier SIM-swap API was found in this research]**; the product must say so rather than imply it.

#### Assembly — what each layer actually stops

| Attack | SIM possession | Passive voiceprint | Random-phrase challenge | Code-word |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stranger guessing / cold fraud call | **Stops** (wrong number) | Stops | Stops | **Stops** |
| Replay of victim's recorded voice / voice note | Passes if from spoofed/enrolled route* | Often **fails** (it *is* the voice) | **Stops** (phrase is new) | **Stops** |
| Offline clone (pre-rendered audio) | — | Often **fails** by design | **Stops** (cannot predict phrase) | **Stops** |
| Real-time voice conversion, live attacker | — | Weak / arms race | Taxes + triple-check | **Stops** (secret unknown) |
| Genuine customer socially engineered into cooperating | Passes (genuinely) | Passes (genuinely) | Passes (genuinely) | **Only friction** — ceremony creates a pause and a record; cannot fully stop |
| SIM swap, then impersonation | **Fails** (attacker controls number) | Weak | Taxes | **Stops** (secret unknown) |
| Insider who knows the code-word | Passes | — | Passes | **Fails** — rotate, per-person words, audit |

\* Caller-ID alone is *not* SIM possession — Pakistani UAN/number spoofing is officially documented (§(a)); possession must be proved by a callback/flash/DTMF loop to the number, never by trusting the presented CLI.

**Latency budget on Asterisk [planning assumption, from component figures]:** the ceremony adds an estimated **45–75 seconds** to a call — prompt + readback (~10 s), SIM callback/flash overlap, STT verification of the phrase (streaming STT returns in ~300 ms–1 s; Deepgram-class streaming is priced and benchmarked for exactly this use **[official/industry — vendor pricing/latency, §(e)]**), embedding extraction and scoring (<1 s on CPU for a short utterance with ECAPA-TDNN **[planning assumption — to be measured in pilot]**), code-word DTMF/speech entry (~10 s), verdict assembly and webhook (<2 s). The *verdict computation* is not the bottleneck; the *human performing the ceremony* is. That is acceptable for a refund-change or disbursement call and unacceptable for routine low-value calls — which is why Insaan Check must be invoked **risk-triggered** (amount threshold, bank-detail change, new device/number age, tenant rule), never on every call. A product that adds a minute to every call will be switched off within a month.

### (d) Legal and regulatory — where the shared-infrastructure idea meets the law

*This subsection is research, not legal advice; a formal Pakistani counsel opinion is a gating condition in the verdict.*

#### Voiceprints are biometric, and biometric means sensitive

Pakistan has **no enacted comprehensive data-protection law**. The **Personal Data Protection Bill (PDPB)** has been in draft since 2018; the **2023 draft was approved in principle by the federal cabinet in July 2023 but has not been passed by Parliament**, and as of late 2026 it remains a draft, with the Ministry seeking more time in Senate committee in 2025 **[official/industry — ICLG/TerraLex Pakistan chapters; MoITT draft history]**. That absence is not a free pass — it is a cliff edge: the product must be built to the draft's standard now, because the draft is the only credible preview of the law it will eventually be judged under, and because tenant contracts (especially any financial-sector tenant) will impose that standard contractually long before Parliament does.

Under the 2023 draft's definitions, **"sensitive personal data" expressly includes biometric data** (alongside financial data, health data, CNIC/passport data, genetic data and more) **[official — PDPB 2023 draft text, via ICLG summary]**, and processing of sensitive data requires the data subject's **explicit consent** (subject to narrow exceptions) **[official — draft, via JD Supra/MoITT summary]**. The draft proposes a National Commission for Personal Data Protection, data-subject rights (including a right not to be subject to solely automated decisions), and significant penalties **[industry — legal commentary on the draft]**. Design consequences that follow immediately:

- **Enrolment is a consent ceremony, not a settings toggle.** Recorded, specific, revocable consent — in the customer's language — covering: what is captured (voice samples / template), why (in-call verification for named tenants/purposes), how long it is kept, and how to withdraw. Withdrawal must actually delete the template, and deletion must be provable in logs.
- **Raw voice recordings are more dangerous than templates.** A recording can be replayed, cloned and re-enrolled elsewhere; a well-designed template (an embedding vector) is far less reusable — though it is *not* anonymous and must still be treated as sensitive personal data. Default to: extract the template, encrypt it, and delete raw enrolment audio on a short, published schedule, retaining call recordings only under the tenant's existing call-recording consent and retention rules.
- **PECA 2016 exposure runs the other way as well.** The Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 criminalises unauthorised access to information systems and data and identity-related offences; it gives Sendqon and its tenants a prosecution route against impersonators, but it also means a breach or misuse of the template store is not merely a civil/privacy event **[official — PECA 2016 framework, as summarised in legal commentary]**. PECA confers no data-subject rights and creates no regulator — it is a criminal statute, not a compliance regime, and must not be cited as one.

#### SBP / NADRA: a private voiceprint can only ever be an additional factor

For regulated financial activity, identity is anchored to the state: **SBP requires NADRA verification for account opening and wallet enrolment** **[industry — §(b)]**, and no private biometric — voice, face or otherwise — substitutes for that KYC anchor. A Sendqon voiceprint is therefore legally positioned, for any bank/fintech tenant, as **an additional authentication factor for an already-KYC'd customer**, never as identity proofing, never as KYC, and never as a basis to open or recover an account on its own. This is not a limitation to apologise for; it is the same position every bank's own voice-biometrics programme occupies (Bank Alfalah authenticates *existing, CNIC-verified* customers on its helpline **[official — §(b)]**). Marketing copy must be policed accordingly: the words "KYC", "identity verification" and "NADRA alternative" must never appear. The accurate claim is "caller authentication / human-presence ceremony for an existing relationship".

Sector rules compound this for bank tenants specifically: SBP-supervised outsourcing / technology-risk expectations mean a bank tenant will require security audits, data-residency answers and exit/deletion commitments before a single production voiceprint is stored **[planning assumption — procurement practice, consistent with §(a) payer analysis]**. Non-bank tenants (e-commerce, lenders under SECP rather than SBP, clinics, schools) present a materially lighter path — another reason the pilot in §(f) starts there.

#### Call recording and analysis consent

Sendqon already records calls in production, but Insaan Check *analyses* voice for biometric purposes, which is a new purpose beyond recording for quality/dispute purposes. Enrolment consent (above) must therefore be matched by **in-call transparency**: the ceremony announces itself ("for your security, we will now verify…"), the challenge is performed by a consenting enrolled person, and a non-enrolled caller is never silently voiceprinted. **Covert enrolment — building a voiceprint from ordinary calls without specific consent — is prohibited as a design rule**, regardless of the current absence of an enacted PDPB: it is precisely the conduct that produced biometric-privacy litigation against voice vendors abroad, including the Illinois BIPA suit over Pindrop-processed voiceprints that reached the US Third Circuit in 2026 (dismissed on a financial-institution exemption — a defence a Pakistani SaaS should not plan to rely on) **[industry — case reporting, §(b) sources]**. One covert-enrolment story would end this product and damage every other Sendqon tenant relationship.

#### Cross-tenant sharing — the legal problem at the heart of the concept

The concept's boldest phrase — *"cross-business human-authentication layer… sold as shared infrastructure"* — is also its most dangerous, and the danger is specific:

1. **A voice cannot be reissued.** A leaked password is rotated; a leaked card is replaced; a leaked voice template exposes a characteristic the person will carry for life, usable against every service that trusts that voice. A central store spanning many tenants concentrates that irreversible harm into a single breach target. This is not hypothetical risk rhetoric: Pakistan has already seen reporting of **2.7 million citizens' particulars allegedly compromised from NADRA-linked offices between 2019 and 2023** **[industry — Dawn-reported joint investigation]** — the market and the press will judge a private voiceprint honeypot against that memory.
2. **Consent does not travel.** Consent given to Tenant A (a school) for purpose A cannot authorise Tenant B (a lender) to authenticate against the same template for purpose B under the PDPB draft's purpose/consent logic **[official — draft principles]**. Cross-tenant matching would require a separate, explicit, Sendqon-level consent that most customers will not understand and many will not give — and a consent flow customers don't understand is not consent in any sense that survives a dispute.
3. **Function creep is the regulator's nightmare case.** A shared human-auth network that begins as fraud prevention becomes, with one integration, a tracking network ("where else has this human authenticated?"). Even the *metadata* (which tenants checked which person, when) is sensitive relationship data no tenant should be able to query about another tenant's customers.

**Resolution — share the ceremony, never the biometric [design rule]:**

- **Templates live in per-tenant silos**: separately keyed, encrypted embedding stores, one per tenant (and, for families, per family), with no cross-tenant matching query in the API at all — not "disabled by default", *not implemented*. A person who enrols with three tenants enrols three times, with three consents, producing three unrelated templates.
- **What is shared is the protocol and the verdict format**: any tenant can *run* an Insaan Check ceremony against *its own* enrolment, via the same API, and receive the same signed verdict object ("insaan hai, wahi hai" + which legs passed + timestamp + call reference) that it can log, webhook and dispute from. The network effect is real — common tooling, common customer familiarity ("I know this ceremony"), common red-team intelligence about attack patterns (shared as *patterns*, never as personal data) — without a shared biometric ever existing.
- **Code-words are hashed per relationship** and never leave the tenant silo either; the family product enrols a word known to the family and verified by hash, so Sendqon staff and systems never hold a readable list of family secrets.
- **Breach blast radius is thereby bounded to one tenant's customers**, who can be notified, whose templates can be deleted and re-enrolled, and whose harm, while serious, is not "every voice in Pakistan that ever used Insaan Check". This architecture is also the only version of the pitch a bank CISO or an SBP examiner can ever approve — silos are not a compromise of the vision; they are what makes the vision sellable.

Template-storage corollaries: encrypt at rest with per-tenant keys; store embeddings, not audio, beyond the short enrolment window; log every verification access; support tenant-triggered and subject-triggered deletion; keep all processing in the same residency posture as Sendqon's existing call data, and state that posture in the tenant data-processing addendum before the first enrolment, not after the first enterprise questionnaire.


### (e) Unit economics

Published anchors first. Twilio Verify: **$0.05 per successful verification + channel** (US SMS $0.0083; all-in ≈ $0.058 ≈ **Rs 16**) **[official]**. Pindrop via AWS Marketplace: **$0.05–$0.30 per call at 1M-call utilisation, behind a $50,000 (Rs 13.85M) minimum** **[official]**. Speech-to-text for the short readback phrase only: Deepgram Nova-3 **$0.0043/min** pre-recorded / ~$0.0048 streaming, OpenAI Whisper API **$0.006/min**, Google Cloud STT V2 **$0.016/min** **[official — vendor list prices, Oct 2026 compilations]** — 10 seconds of audio therefore costs $0.001–$0.003. Self-hosted Whisper or SpeechBrain removes the fee at GPU/CPU operating cost.

Per completed check, built up **[planning assumption, from the published components above]**: incremental ceremony minute on Sendqon's own Asterisk/gateway routes Rs 3–7; SIM-possession leg over own SMPP/SIM-gateway Rs 1–3; STT Rs 0.2–1.2; voiceprint + anti-spoofing scoring on self-hosted ECAPA-TDNN (<1 s CPU per short utterance) under Rs 2 combined; platform, logging, signed verdict, webhook and support amortisation Rs 3–6. **Total marginal cost ≈ Rs 8–18 ($0.03–$0.065) per completed check.** Bill on completed verdicts only (Twilio's successful-verification trigger), not on abandoned ceremonies.

**Recommended price [planning assumption — proposal, not market data]: Rs 35 per completed check** (≈ $0.126); Rs 25 at >10,000 checks/month committed volume; Rs 49 for a high-assurance verdict requiring all three legs plus human review of failures. The one-sentence justification the numbers support: about twice an OTP, for a check an OTP cannot perform (it verifies the human, not the handset), and well under the enterprise alternative's per-call range with no minimum contract. Against the human alternative — 3–5 minutes of manual callback verification at ≈ Rs 19 per in-house human minute (sibling research) = Rs 57–95 with no signed record — Rs 35 is cheaper before any fraud saving. Gross margin at Rs 35 is roughly 50–75% **[planning assumption]**. The binding economic risks are enrolment friction and invocation discipline, not unit margin: pair the price with an included enrolment allowance and published risk-trigger templates, and never price a cross-tenant check — §(d) forbids the product, so the price list must not imply it exists.

### (f) Smallest honest pilot

**One segment, one pattern.** One existing Sendqon e-commerce tenant; one fraud pattern: **refund bank-detail change by phone** — a caller asks for a refund to be redirected to a new IBAN/wallet. High value, irreversible on Raast, low frequency (friction is tolerable), clean ground truth.

**Ceremony:** (1) possession callback to the enrolled number; (2) random-phrase readback with STT content check, voiceprint score recorded *as a signal only*; (3) customer-set code-word, hashed, set at enrolment via an already-trusted channel — never on an unverified inbound call. **Decision rule:** possession + code-word are gates; a low voiceprint score can only escalate to human review, never pass a caller who failed a gate and never block a caller who passed both. Failure routes to today's slower manual process — no genuine customer is ever worse off, only slower.

**Enrolment:** 300–500 customers over 2–3 weeks, explicit recorded consent (§(d) script, Urdu + English), code-word of their choosing, optional 20-second voiceprint phrases. Enrolment completion rate is itself a kill criterion: below 50% completion, the product fails regardless of matching accuracy **[planning assumption — fix the exact threshold with the tenant before launch]**.

**Targets fixed in the pilot agreement before enrolment begins:**

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Red-team false accepts on the code-word gate | **0** across the full set; any one stops the pilot |
| Genuine-customer false rejects (possession + word, first attempt) | **≤ 5%**; ≥ 95% complete within two attempts or human fallback in ≤ 3 extra minutes |
| Voiceprint on real pilot audio | Measure and report the **actual EER / score distributions on AMR call audio** — success is a measured number, whatever it is; it decides whether the leg survives into v1 |
| Ceremony duration | Median ≤ 75 s, p90 ≤ 120 s added to the call |
| Fraud outcome | Zero successful phone-channel refund-redirections during the pilot, against a documented prior-period baseline |
| Customer experience | ≥ 80% rate the ceremony "reassuring" rather than "annoying" in a post-call SMS survey |

**Red team (the acceptance test):** for consenting participants ("attack my account"), attempt (i) replay of a genuine recorded excerpt; (ii) an offline consumer-tool clone (ElevenLabs-class, from under a minute of consented audio) played into the call; (iii) real-time voice conversion against the random phrase; (iv) pure social engineering with correct name/order/address but no code-word; (v) enrolled-number-only attempt (stolen unlocked phone simulation). **Pass = all five fail to obtain a pass verdict, and logs correctly name the leg that stopped each.** If (iii) defeats the voiceprint signal but is stopped by the code-word, that is a pass for the product and a published internal finding about the voiceprint — the outcome §(c) predicts, designed in advance to strengthen rather than sink the launch.

**Out of scope:** bank tenants, account recovery, any cross-tenant enrolment or matching, voiceprint as a sole gate, minors, and any marketing using "accuracy", "99%" or "fraud-proof". Duration 6–8 weeks including enrolment **[planning assumption]**; engineering is a bounded Asterisk IVR flow + STT + hashed-secret store + siloed embedding store + verdict API/webhook on the existing platform. If #5 proceeds, share the verdict-object format from day one so one call log can show verified machine, verified human, or unverified — that unified record is the long-term asset.

### Verdict & conditions

**GO WITH CONDITIONS.** The fraud environment is officially documented, no shared/API competitor exists in Pakistan, the build fits Sendqon's live Asterisk + SIM-gateway + multi-tenant API stack, and the economics clear at Rs 35 against a Rs 16 OTP anchor and a Rs 14–83 enterprise anchor behind a Rs 13.85M minimum. The GO holds only in this shape — removing any condition converts it to NO-GO:

1. **Sell the ceremony, never the voiceprint.** SIM possession + random-phrase challenge + code-word, returning a signed verdict. Voiceprint/anti-spoofing is an internal signal that may escalate to human review and may never alone pass or block a caller. No accuracy percentages in marketing. ( §(c): ASVspoof 5's best system errs 8.61% on modern adversarial tests; every system exceeded 15% on unseen 2021 deepfake evaluation data.)
2. **Tenant-siloed biometrics by architecture, not policy.** Per-tenant encrypted template stores; no cross-tenant matching function in code or API; code-words hashed per relationship. Share the protocol, verdict format and attack-pattern intelligence — never templates, words or cross-tenant metadata. (§(d))
3. **Consent-first enrolment; no covert voiceprinting, ever.** Explicit, recorded, revocable consent in the customer's language; in-call ceremony announcement; provable deletion on withdrawal. Build to the PDPB 2023 draft standard now, despite its unenacted status.
4. **Additional factor only; never KYC.** No positioning as identity proofing, a NADRA substitute, or account-opening/recovery assurance. KYC stays anchored to NADRA; Insaan Check authenticates a caller inside an existing, already-KYC'd relationship.
5. **Formal Pakistani legal opinion before the first paying tenant enrols anyone** — PDPB-draft posture, PECA exposure, recording/analysis consent, breach notification, tenant data-processing addendum. Build and consented pilot design may proceed in parallel; commercial enrolment may not precede the opinion.
6. **Run the §(f) pilot as specified, with pre-registered targets and the real-clone red team as the acceptance gate.** Publish the measured telephony voiceprint EER internally whatever it shows. Any code-word-gate false accept, or genuine-customer false rejects above ceiling after one remediation round, stops expansion.
7. **Risk-triggered invocation only.** Ship tenant rule templates (amount thresholds, bank-detail changes, number-age/velocity triggers). Checking every call is a churn event, not a security posture.
8. **Regulator-language discipline.** PTA's public advice is that no genuine organisation asks for codes on a call; the ceremony must therefore ask only for the customer's *own pre-agreed Insaan Check word* — never an OTP, PIN, password or CNIC — and explain that distinction at enrolment and at the start of every ceremony. If a low-literacy customer in Urdu cannot be made to understand the distinction unmistakably, move the word to DTMF entry and re-test the script before launch.

**First three steps:** (1) Commission the condition-5 legal opinion. (2) Build the pilot ceremony on Asterisk — IVR challenge, STT phrase check, hashed code-word store, siloed SpeechBrain scoring in signal-only mode, signed verdict + webhook — and measure the §(c) latency budget on real gateway calls. (3) Sign one existing e-commerce tenant to the pilot agreement with the §(f) targets written into it, and enrol the first 300 customers. No bank outreach, no family B2C launch, no cross-tenant anything until the pilot reports.

## #7 The Reality Oracle — Fleet-Verified Physical Truth

> **Concept as given:** "An API that answers questions about the physical world — 'is this shop open, at this address, right now?' — by dispatching the nearest fleet phone/person to look, photograph and attest (GPS + time + SIM identity). Digital Pakistan runs on unverifiable claims (fake addresses, ghost shops, phantom deliveries); a verification network with a signed attestation is infrastructure none of the data-collection apps (Premise, Streetbees) sell as a real-time API here. Extends #3's human layer (Human by the Minute) from voice to presence."

**Verdict up front: GO WITH CONDITIONS — as a same-day verification API first, not as a "right now" API.** The paying market is real and already outsourced: a National Bank of Pakistan (NBP) tender pricing residence and business visits separately, region by region, proves buyers already purchase exactly this act. What the evidence does *not* support on day one is "right now." That is a fleet-density promise, not a software promise, and Sendqon's live fleet is tenant-operated gateway phones in schools, clinics and shops — not a roaming crowd within 30 minutes of an arbitrary address. The honest product is a signed, spoof-resistant, same-day (in dense pockets, sub-hour) attestation API, sold first where verification is already a budgeted line item, with "real-time" earned zone by zone — never claimed nationally in advance. The three conditions that matter most: (1) sell observation, not guarantee; (2) no attestation without the full anti-spoof stack in §(c); (3) the pilot must pass deliberate-fraud tests before any buyer sees a paid result.

**Grading:** **[official]** = regulator, statute, tender, or the company's own published material; **[industry]** = trade press, practitioner guides, competitor-review and earnings sites (directional, not audited); **[unverified]** = single weak secondary source; **[planning assumption]** = our own modelling, never to be mistaken for a market fact. **FX:** PKR 277 = USD 1, per the brief.

### (a) Problem + payer (Pakistan first)

Pakistan's commerce runs on descriptive rather than postal addresses, on cash-on-delivery (COD) orders placed against numbers and addresses that may not exist, and on business claims — a shop, warehouse, clinic, garage — that a remote decision-maker cannot see. Every institution that lends, insures, lists, disburses or ships against such a claim can trust it, send its own employee, or pay someone else to go and look. The third option is already an industry; the Oracle makes that slow, opaque purchase fast and API-shaped.

**1. Banks and verification agencies — the anchor payer, with published prices.** NBP procured "Physical Verification Services: All Consumer, Structured and Program Lending Products" by tender (No. NT-08/26/2019-1, PPRA Ref. TS398420E). The published evaluation report names two lowest bidders — International Credit Information Limited (ICIL), 26 of 37 regions, and Business Credit Information Limited (BCIL), 11 of 37 — and prints the accepted rate per region, split into a **residence visit** and a **business visit** **[official]**. ICIL's per-visit rate runs **PKR 165** (Hyderabad, Sukkur, Multan, Faisalabad and others) through PKR 180 in Lahore and PKR 185 in most regions to **PKR 200** in Abbottabad; BCIL's regions (Balochistan, Azad Kashmir, northern KP, Gilgit) run **PKR 197.50–247.50**. A residence + business pair cost PKR 330–400 (ICIL) or PKR 395–495 (BCIL) **[official]**.

Three caveats matter for §(e). The tender is from 2019 (evaluation January 2020) and inflation since has been severe: it is a *floor from 2019*, not a 2026 price — and **no current published per-visit rate card for ICIL or BCIL was found; both sell by quotation/tender, and no 2025/26 published price exists in the sources reviewed**. The price is also achievable only at bank scale (thousands of cases monthly, batched by neighbourhood), not as the marginal cost of one urgent unbatched visit; and its level reveals the labour model underneath (§(e)).

ICIL's own description confirms the shape banks already accept: the bank enters an enquiry; ICIL allocates it to field staff; the officer records the **GPS coordinates of the exact address, captures a photograph and the applicant's signature**, and delivers the report online. ICIL states it has served banks for over ten years and was sole nationwide provider for the Kamyab Jawan scheme **[official — company's own site]**; Bank of Punjab's documentation lists four such firms — ICIL, Cloud Data, RMS and BCIL **[official]**.

Why do banks still need this when onboarding is digital? Because SBP digitised *identity*, not *place*. Its Consolidated Customer Onboarding Framework requires NADRA biometric verification or Verisys, CNIC–MSISDN pairing, and to "collect and record geo-location / IP address of the digital gadgets through which [the] customer has requested for digital onboarding" **[official]** — which proves where the *applicant's phone* was, not that a business exists or trades at a stated address. Its decision TAT (two working days for individuals, five for entities **[official]**) is a window same-day verification fits, and inside which 30 minutes is a premium, not a requirement.

**2. Digital lenders, wallets and merchant onboarding.** JazzCash Business onboarding, as published, is digital: mobile number + OTP, CNIC scan, live photo matched to CNIC, biometric verification, business details, then a Raast QR **[official]** — no premises visit. That cuts both ways: the largest onboarding machines chose *not* to pay for a visit at the basic tier, but the physical truth about those merchants (does the kiryana exist? is the QR displayed? is the "shop" a house?) is unverified by design, and every later lender, aggregator or brand relying on that record inherits the gap. The reachable buyer is the *next* decision-maker — a lender sizing a merchant loan, an aggregator auditing QR acceptance, a distributor checking an outlet before extending stock on credit. **No published price for digital-lender premises checks in Pakistan was found [unverified — no source].**

**3. Insurers and surveyors.** Insurance already institutionalises the physical look: an insurer-appointed surveyor inspects vehicle or premises, documents damage with photographs, estimates cost and reports before a claim proceeds; pre-inspection surveys are standard **[industry]**. One concrete figure: a 2025 vehicle-financing scheme records an inspection fee of **PKR 5,000** for a PakWheels quality inspection/valuation of a used vehicle, within 1–2 days **[industry — single employer's published scheme document]** — a skilled evaluation, not an existence check, but it marks the range: hundreds at tender scale for a simple look, thousands for a skilled inspection. **No published tariff for loss surveyors in Pakistan was found**; fees are negotiated per assignment — itself the opportunity, since an API with a printed price is a novelty here.

**4. FMCG brands and retail measurement — the volume case.** Nielsen's Pakistan reporting put the FMCG outlet universe at **582,652 outlets across 472 cities and 17,000+ villages**, roughly one store per 356 Pakistanis **[industry — Nielsen Pakistan presentation, 2017/18 data]**. Nielsen's method is the incumbent Oracle-at-scale: a full **retail census every 3–4 years**, a stratified panel, auditors visiting panel outlets periodically for sales, distribution, pricing and stock **[industry]**. Business Recorder notes only a handful of (mostly multinational) FMCG companies buy this data; most Pakistani companies do not, "due to the 'perceived' higher costs" **[industry]**. Demand is proven and local brands are priced out — the opening for a cheaper per-outlet product. But brands need 500 outlets in a district verified this month (existence, open/closed, fascia photo, competitor display, own product stocked/priced), not one outlet in 30 minutes: batch tolerance is the natural base load for a fleet that cannot yet promise real-time (§(c), §(f)). **No published Nielsen/Kantar per-outlet price for Pakistan was found [no published source]**; §(e) builds FMCG economics bottom-up from labour cost.

**5. Marketplaces and COD sellers.** Daraz seller onboarding is a documents flow: shop name, verified email/phone, business/warehouse/return addresses as text, CNIC upload, bank verification via cheque image and IBAN, NTN documents for corporate sellers **[industry — seller-registration guides]**. The address is *declared*, not *visited*. The cost appears downstream: practitioner sources put first-attempt delivery failure at roughly **18–25%** and national COD return-to-origin at roughly **18–20%** (unoptimised stores 25–35%+) **[industry — seller guides; directional, not official statistics]**, and one guide itemises a refused order on a ~PKR 2,500 basket at roughly **PKR 825–875** sunk **[industry]**. Couriers thus generate address truth as a by-product, but **no evidence was found that TCS or Leopards sells it as a standalone product [no published source]**. The honest pitch is selective — seller warehouse/return addresses above a risk threshold; buyer addresses only where order value justifies a check against the ~PKR 825 a refusal burns. For ordinary low-basket COD a pre-dispatch check is uneconomic; do not sell it there.

**6. NGOs and government.** BISP shows the scale of state verification: survey data cross-checked against NADRA, biometric verification at payment points, field visits monitoring the payment network, audits blocking ineligible beneficiaries **[official/industry — parliamentary reporting]**. Facility verification is a long-standing Premise-class use case (§(b)) — but tender cycles, lowest-bid dynamics and payment delays make this the least reachable channel; rank it last.

**Buyer ranking (willingness to pay × reachable by a small SaaS):** (1) *Mid-tier banks, MFBs, digital lenders* — overflow/urgent cases; already budgeted, but benchmarked against tender rates, so win on speed/API/evidence, not price. (2) *FMCG/distribution brands* priced out of Nielsen — huge universe, batch tolerance, repeat demand. (3) *Insurers/inspection networks* — observation tier only; licensed surveyor work is not contestable. (4) *Marketplaces/large COD sellers* — risk-tiered checks only. (5) *NGOs/government* — largest volumes, slowest procurement, last.

### (b) Competitors and substitutes — with live evidence

**Premise** is the closest global analogue: **over 6 million contributors across 140+ countries** completing surveys, price checks, photo verification and infrastructure mapping, delivered via dashboards and API integration, marketed as "same-day" and "near real-time" **[official/industry — platform descriptions]**; Pakistan is among its tracked markets **[industry]**. Contributor pay is a long tail of small amounts: simple tasks at roughly **USD 0.05–0.10**, a broader range of about **USD 0.25–5.00 per task**, Kenyan reports of surveys at KSh 15–40 (≈ USD 0.12–0.30) and location tasks up to KSh 250 (≈ USD 1.90), and a World Bank-cited report of active contributors earning USD 40–60 per week **[industry]**. Premise publishes **no rate card** **[official — absence on premise.com, as reviewed Oct 2026]**.

The concept's claim — that Premise/Streetbees do not sell this "as a real-time API here" — is **substantially true, with one nuance**: Premise *does* offer API integration and "real-time" data *flow* once tasks are deployed. What no source shows any platform selling is the Oracle's shape: a **per-query API with a response-time commitment for an arbitrary, client-supplied address**. Their model is inverse — the client designs a *campaign*, deployed to whoever is nearby and willing; results accumulate opportunistically. No published latency SLA for a single ad-hoc query exists, and **no Pakistan-specific latency, density or per-query price was found for any of these platforms [no published source]**. The differentiation is real but narrow: dispatch + SLA + signed single-question attestation, in Pakistan, priced per answer.

**Streetbees** ("stories"): mostly under GBP 1–2, occasionally GBP 10+ **[industry]**. **Roamler** (invite-/level-gated): ~GBP 2–5 per retail task, occasionally GBP 10–20 **[industry]**. **BeMyEye**: ~GBP 1–10 per mission (commonly GBP 4–6); clients named in reviews include L'Oréal, Nestlé, Samsung **[industry]**. **Field Agent** (US-centric): ~USD/GBP 2–10+ per job **[industry]**. At PKR 277/USD, ~USD 5–20 equals PKR 1,400–5,500 per task — an order of magnitude above the NBP tender rate. That gap is a labour-market difference, not arbitrage: Pakistani supply clears at Pakistani wages (§(e)). Import their operating lessons, not their prices: level gating, fast rejection of bad submissions, photo-first design.

**Pakistani agencies are the real incumbents.** ICIL, BCIL, Cloud Data and RMS **[official]**, plus screening firms such as A&A Verity, publishing bands of roughly **PKR 3,000–7,000** (basic), **PKR 5,000–10,000** (education/employment) and **PKR 12,000+** (comprehensive), with field address verification inside the package **[industry — firm's own pricing page]**. Their strengths are a startup's weaknesses: bank relationships, tender eligibility, nationwide field networks, accepted formats. Their weakness is the opening: portal-and-PDF workflow, relationship sales, turnaround in days, no public API, price list or sub-day SLA product.

**Google Maps / Street View — the free substitute that barely exists for Pakistani streets.** Street View in Pakistan is **landmarks-only**: Google imagery covers selected heritage sites (Lahore Fort/Walled City, Badshahi Mosque, Shalimar Gardens, Hiran Minar, Rohtas Fort, Derawar Fort, Harappa, Katas Raj), with **no Google road-network coverage** **[industry — Wikipedia, Street View in Asia; a September 2026 country audit classifying Pakistan among ten "landmarks only" countries]**. User photos fill gaps for prominent businesses but are stale/undated, unverifiable as to capture time, and absent for the long-tail kiryana/workshop/godown addresses buyers care about. Satellite view confirms a building exists; it cannot confirm it is open, trading, stocked or occupied by the claimed business *today*. In richer-mapped markets Street View freshness would be the hardest free competitor; in Pakistan it is structurally weak — the concept's premise is *stronger* here than its author may have realised. Honest limit: for a prominent urban business, a recent Maps photo plus reviews may be "good enough" for low-stakes questions; do not sell the Oracle there.


### (c) Technical feasibility — the honest core is spoofing

A verification API is a claim about the physical world made by a stranger with a phone, paid per claim — every part of that invites fraud. If attestation can be faked cheaply, the product is worse than none: buyers will *act* on it (disburse, ship, approve) with more confidence than an unverified declaration ever earned.

**How attestations are faked.** (1) *Mock location:* Android Developer Options allow a mock-location app to override GPS. Naïve apps are caught by `Location.isMock()` (Android 12+, API 31) **[official — Android API reference]**, but practitioners call that flag alone "security theatre" in 2026: rooted devices with Magisk, framework patchers and LSPatch-style hooking strip the mock flag before the target app reads it, and spoofing tools market that bypass **[industry]**. (2) *Rooted devices/emulators* can falsify location, sensors, timestamps and the app's code path. (3) *EXIF editing:* any design trusting embedded GPS/time, or accepting gallery uploads, is broken by construction. (4) *Photo-of-photo/screen* defeats "is there a photo?" checks trivially. (5) *Reused photos:* one genuine visit sold to answer five queries — marginal fraud cost is zero without reuse detection. (6) *Collusion:* the shopkeeper is the applicant's cousin and confirms anything; no sensor fixes a cooperative location. (7) *Identity sharing:* one "trusted" account, several humans — or the applicant himself holding a fleet phone. Binding must be to SIM + device + person, not a login.

**What actually stops it — defence in depth, with honest limits.** No single control suffices; the claim must rest on the stack, and the stack's honest output is a *risk-scored observation*, not certainty.

- **In-app camera capture only; no gallery uploads, ever.** Photo taken inside the verifier app at task time, hashed on-device at capture, within a short dispatch-to-capture window and with a continuous approach track from accept to capture (a track is far harder to fake than a point). Kills EXIF editing and most reuse-at-source. ICIL's tablet/app model follows the same principle **[official]**.
- **Play Integrity API as the device gate.** Google's API returns server-verifiable app/device integrity verdicts (including `MEETS_DEVICE_INTEGRITY` / `MEETS_STRONG_INTEGRITY`, hardware-backed on capable devices), bound to a server-issued nonce so tokens cannot be replayed **[official — Android Developers]**. Require a passing verdict *at attestation time*, verified server-side. Honest limit: an arms race — rooted setups that pass are documented, and broken spoof chains get re-broken **[industry]**. It raises attacker cost from "install a free app" to "maintain a rooted, hidden, fingerprint-spoofed device" — decisive against casual fraud, no guarantee against a professional.
- **Mock-location signals as one input, not the verdict.** Check `isMock()`, mock-provider extras, developer-options state, known spoofing packages; feed them into the risk score. Never market "mock detection" as the proof **[industry]**.
- **Cross-location corroboration.** Compare GPS against signals an attacker must fake *consistently and simultaneously*: cell-tower identity, nearby Wi-Fi BSSIDs, IP geolocation — and, uniquely available to Sendqon, the **gateway's own network view of that SIM/device** via sim_fleet. Faking GPS alone produces disagreement; faking all coherently in real time is far harder. Add movement sanity: teleportation between distant attestations minutes apart, straight-line travel and constant-accuracy traces are classic spoof signatures **[industry]**.
- **Challenge objects — the oldest trick, still among the best.** Each dispatch carries a unique, short-lived challenge that must appear *in frame* (a code word/number held in shot, or a randomised directed action) — the digital descendant of the newspaper proof-of-date photo. It defeats reused photos and photo-of-old-photo (the challenge did not exist then). It does not defeat a live colluder at the real location — nothing photographic does — but collusion is a *truth* problem (two-verifier rule), not a *presence* problem.
- **Perceptual hashing for reuse detection.** Store a pHash (DCT fingerprint robust to resizing/recompression) of every attestation photo; compare by Hamming distance — 0–5 on a 64-bit pHash indicates near-duplicates **[industry]**. Limits: crops/rotations/overlays defeat pHash, so pair it with embedding similarity for high-value checks and with the challenge rule, which makes reuse pointless even when the hash is evaded.
- **Two-verifier consensus for high value.** Above a buyer-set threshold, dispatch two independent verifiers, blind to each other; pay both; attest only on agreement. It doubles cost (§(e) prices it as a tier) and is the only serious answer to collusion and single-verifier error — campaign platforms get redundancy statistically; a per-query API must buy it explicitly.

**What can honestly be claimed?** This, and no more: *"A named, SIM-identified verifier, on an integrity-checked device, was present within [reported GPS accuracy, typically 5–30 m in urban conditions — planning assumption from consumer-GPS norms, not a measured Sendqon figure] of the stated coordinates at time T, and captured the attached photographs in-app in response to a unique challenge issued at dispatch."* Do **not** claim the business is legitimate, solvent, owned by the applicant or open tomorrow, or proof against a professional attacker; and do not pretend a coordinate identifies a unit inside a dense bazaar, where 20 m spans a dozen shops — there the *photo set* (signboard + wide shot + street context), not the coordinate, carries identification.

**Dispatch latency and density math — why "right now" fails first.** A 30-minute attestation for an *arbitrary* address requires an available, willing, integrity-passing verifier within ~30 minutes' travel at call time. Model, all **[planning assumption]**: restrict Lahore's service zone to a dense core of ~300 km²; a motorbike verifier at 20 km/h has a realistic effective catchment of 30–80 km² once accept-delay, traffic and address-hunting are priced in. With only ~1 in 3 of the "active" pool accepting within 5 minutes at any moment, covering the core needs on the order of **15–40 simultaneously active, dispatch-ready verifiers** — sustained all day, before the first paying query, replicated per launch city. That is a standing-army cost, not a gig cost. Sendqon's existing fleet does not meet this description, and #3's paid human layer is a design, not a recruited, density-mapped workforce. **Launch as same-day (4 working hours inside covered zones), publish the zone map, and let sub-hour appear only as an earned, zone-specific tier.** A buyer promised 30 minutes who receives 5 hours will not renew; one promised same-day who usually receives 90 minutes will.

### (d) Legal and regulatory

*Research orientation, not legal advice.*

**Photographing premises from public space.** Pakistan has no single "photography law," and no statute found here generally prohibits photographing a shopfront or building exterior from a public street — the Oracle's core act. Risk concentrates at the edges: (i) *sensitive installations* (military, police, airport, court, secured sites) — dispatch must carry a proximity blocklist; (ii) *interiors and homes* — Article 14 of the Constitution protects dignity and, subject to law, the privacy of the home **[official]**; an exterior street shot of a residence is the decade-old norm for bank verification **[official — ICIL practice]**, but entering premises or photographing inside a home or its occupants must be consent-based or not done; (iii) *confrontation* — a challenged verifier needs a script, an ID, and an instruction to withdraw, not argue.

**PECA 2016 — real, but specific.** Its relevant offences target *harmful* conduct, not observation as such: §20 (dignity — publicly transmitting information known to be false that harms reputation/privacy; up to 3 years and/or PKR 1 million fine), §21 (modesty — transmitting images harming a person's modesty/privacy, enhanced penalties involving minors), §24 (cyberstalking — including photographing a person and distributing it without consent *in a manner that harms that person*, per Dawn's summary of PEMRA proceedings) **[official — PECA text; industry — Dawn summary]**. The through-line is intent to harm + non-consensual private imagery + publication. An Oracle attestation — exterior photo of business premises, sent privately to one contracting buyer for a stated purpose, faces/plates minimised — sits far from those offences. Exposure grows if the product *publishes* imagery, retains identifiable images of people (especially women and minors, where legal and cultural risks compound), or is misused to surveil a person rather than verify a place. Rules: photograph *premises, not people*; auto-blur faces/plates in the buyer copy; never task photography of individuals; contractually prohibit buyer surveillance/publication use; log purpose per query. PECA's January 2025 amendment created the National Cyber Crime Investigation Agency (NCCIA) **[industry]** — rising enforcement attention argues for this conservative design, not against the product.

**Verifier location data — the draft PDPB problem.** The most sensitive dataset is the *verifier's*: location tracks of identified, SIM-bound individuals. Pakistan has **no enacted comprehensive data-protection law**: the Personal Data Protection Bill has been redrafted since at least 2018; the 2023 version was Cabinet-approved but, per the Senate record and the Ministry of IT's index, not passed, and reviews dated May–October 2026 still list it as a draft with no regulator **[industry — concurring legal-status reviews]**. Drafts treat geolocation as sensitive/critical and propose Pakistan-localisation for critical personal data **[industry]**. Today, handling rests on contract, PECA and constitutional privacy; tomorrow, a PDPB in drafted form will impose consent, purpose-limitation and localisation duties on exactly this dataset. Build the pilot *as if* the draft were law — explicit consent, on-task-only tracking, Pakistan-hosted storage, retention limits, verifier access/deletion rights. Buyers receive a *derived observation*, never the raw track.

**Worker classification and safety (brief; cross-reference #3).** Verifiers inherit #3's labour questions (gig classification, no gig-worker statute, wage anchoring in §(e), JazzCash/EasyPaisa payment). Two additions: *safety* — dispatch sends a person to a stranger's premises, sometimes to observe an unwelcome fiction; mitigations are daylight residential tasking, no-entry rules, one-tap abort with full pay on hostility, never announcing an adverse purpose, and accident cover priced into the pilot; and *bribery pressure* — structural answers are two-verifier dispatch, audit re-visits (§(c)), and exclusion-and-clawback for a proven false attestation.

**Liability when an attestation is wrong.** It will happen: a shop open at T is shuttered at disbursement; a genuine shop is misread as closed because the verifier arrived during a prayer/lunch closure; a determined fraud beats the stack. The wording in §(c) is the foundation: an **observation at time T by an identified observer using stated methods** — not a warranty, credit recommendation or guarantee of future state. Buyer terms should define it so, cap liability at a multiple of fees paid, bar sole reliance for high-value decisions (sell the two-verifier tier for more), and offer dispute re-attestation — a fresh visit remedies most errors better than damages. Allocate fraud losses honestly: if Sendqon's stack certified a submission its own rules should have rejected, that is Sendqon's process failure and should carry a defined service credit — also the strongest sales proof the stack is real. Banks already buy on this logic: the NBP tender buys a "verification report," not an indemnity **[official]**. Counsel must review the wording and terms before the first paid pilot.

### (e) Unit economics — price from evidence, cost from wages

**Prices buyers already pay (recap).** Tender visit (2019): **PKR 165–247.50 [official]**. Skilled inspection: **PKR 5,000 [industry]**. Screening bundle incl. field check: **PKR 3,000–12,000+ [industry]**. **No current published Pakistani rate card exists [no published source]** — the Oracle must *discover* its price in the pilot, between the 2019 floor and the skilled-inspection ceiling.

**Cost stack, anchored to wages.** Punjab's notified minimum wage is **PKR 40,000/month from July 2025 — PKR 1,538/day, PKR 192/hour** (Sindh/KP PKR 40,000; federal/ICT and Balochistan PKR 37,000) **[industry — provincial notifications as compiled by FAFEN and legal trackers]**. One unbatched visit consumes 45–90 minutes including travel **[planning assumption]**: roughly PKR 145–290 at the statutory floor, *before* fuel, data, payout fees and QA cost. A sustainable payout must beat the alternative use of that hour for a worker supplying his own bike, fuel and phone; pricing at the bare floor is a modelling error. Modelled **[planning assumption]**: payout **PKR 300–450** per single visit (~1.5–2.3× the statutory cost of the time), plus travel allowance beyond a base radius; **double payout** for the two-verifier tier; platform cost **PKR 80–150** per attestation at pilot scale. Fully loaded pilot cost per standard attestation: **~PKR 450–650 [planning assumption]**.

**Reconciling cost with the NBP floor — the batching insight.** PKR 165–185 is what a visit costs when an officer already in a neighbourhood clears a *batch* of cases on a route; marginal cost collapses when travel is amortised. The Oracle is two businesses wearing one API:

| Tier | Buyer price (modelled) | Cost / margin logic |
|---|---|---|
| **Batch / same-day** — FMCG audits, portfolio sweeps; ≥50 addresses in a zone; by end of day | **PKR 350–550 per visit [planning assumption]** | Routed batches of 15–30 visits/day push payout per visit toward PKR 150–250; sampled QA. The base load that funds fleet density |
| **On-demand single** — one address, 4-working-hour window, covered zone | **PKR 900–1,400 [planning assumption]** | Unbatched travel dominates: payout PKR 300–450 + travel + full QA. Still <⅓ of a PKR 3,000 screening bundle |
| **Two-verifier consensus** — high-value lending, disputes | **PKR 1,800–2,600 [planning assumption]** | Two independent visits + adjudication; priced against loss on a bad disbursement |
| **"Real-time" (<60 min)** | **Do not sell until density is proven (§(c))** | Needs standing availability payments, not per-task payouts; selling early converts a density problem into refunds and churn |

All table prices are **[planning assumption]** — pilot hypotheses, not market facts. Sanity checks: a PKR 900 check is rational wherever a ghost address risks even PKR 50,000 of principal, and against the ~PKR 825 a refused COD parcel burns **[industry]**; a PKR 350–550 batch price is rational if it replaces even a fraction of an enterprise retail-measurement contract. The table also concedes where the Oracle *loses*: head-on tender competition with ICIL at ~PKR 200/visit (2019 money) — a routed, salaried incumbent field force will undercut gig dispatch on batched bank volume even inflation-adjusted. Bid where ICIL's model is structurally slow or absent.

**FMCG volume case (illustrative).** A brand auditing 2,000 outlets in a province in a month **[planning assumption scenario]**: at PKR 450/outlet, PKR 900,000 (~USD 3,250) — 100 verifier-days at 20 routed visits/day, i.e. five verifiers for a month: *recruitable* in one city, unlike §(c)'s standing army, repeatable monthly, and every campaign builds the density map enabling faster tiers.

### (f) Smallest honest pilot

**Scope.** One city: **Lahore** (dense; NBP tender rate PKR 180/visit **[official]**). Two buyers in parallel: (1) a mid-tier lender/MFB or digital lender supplying real overflow verifications from its live pipeline; (2) an FMCG/distribution brand supplying a fixed outlet-audit batch. One tier only: **same-day**, with a 4-hour *target* (measured and reported, not SLA-promised) inside two declared covered zones. No "real-time" language in pilot contracts or marketing.

**Fixed numbers [planning assumption — stated in advance].** Six weeks. **600 paid verifications**: 300 lender + 300 FMCG audits in routed batches. **25–35 verifiers**, SIM- and device-registered, Play-Integrity-gated, level-gated (first 10 tasks low-value, 100% QA-reviewed). Two named zones published to buyers as a map; out-of-zone queries are declined. Test prices inside §(e)'s ranges: lender singles **PKR 999**; FMCG batch **PKR 449/outlet** — elasticity tests, not final prices.

**Spoof-resistance acceptance tests — the gate that matters most.** Before any buyer result is delivered as "verified," and weekly thereafter, the pilot team (not the verifiers) submits deliberate fraud through the real pipeline: (i) mock-location; (ii) rooted device; (iii) gallery/EXIF-edited photo (should be impossible by design — test that the app truly refuses it); (iv) photo-of-screen; (v) reused genuine photo, resized/recompressed; (vi) challenge violation (yesterday's code word); (vii) collusion simulation (verifier + cooperative "shopkeeper" attesting a non-trading premises). **Pre-stated bar: 100% of (i)–(vi) rejected or flagged high-risk before payout; (vii) caught by the two-verifier/audit layer on the sampled high-value subset, or the high-value tier does not launch.** Any class passing undetected stops the pilot clock until fixed and re-tested. Results, including failures, go into the pilot report verbatim.

**Success criteria, stated in advance.** Scale-up is a GO only if *all* hold: (1) *Integrity* — fraud bar met; audit re-visits of a random 5% of accepted attestations find ≥98% materially accurate. (2) *Latency* — ≥90% of in-zone lender queries within 4 working hours; ≥98% same day; FMCG batch inside its window. (3) *Economics* — fully loaded cost ≤ PKR 650 per single, ≤ PKR 350 per batched outlet, at tested prices. (4) *Buyer truth test* — both buyers, shown results *and* the fraud report, agree in writing to a paid second phase at pilot or higher prices; compliments and unpaid LOIs do not count. (5) *Safety/legal* — zero verifier injuries/detentions; zero upheld privacy complaints; counsel sign-off *before* week 1. If (1) or (5) fails, the product does not proceed in current form regardless of (2)–(4). If (3) or (4) fails, the market prefers ICIL's price or its own staff, and the Oracle is shelved or re-scoped to the tier that *did* clear its bar.

**Deliberately not tested:** national coverage, sub-hour response, insurer surveyor work, government tenders, iOS verifiers, photography of people.

### Verdict & conditions

**GO WITH CONDITIONS.** The evidence supports building the Oracle as Pakistan's same-day, API-delivered, spoof-resistant observation network, sold first to buyers who already pay strangers to go and look. The NBP tender proves the category and prices its floor **[official]**; ICIL's product proves the GPS + photo + online-report shape is what banks already accept **[official]**; Street View's landmarks-only presence removes the free substitute that would kill this product in richer-mapped countries **[industry]**; Nielsen's 582,652-outlet universe **[industry]** supplies batch volume a young fleet can serve.

**Conditions (all must hold):**

1. **Same-day first.** Launch tiers: Batch/Same-Day and 4-Hour-in-Covered-Zones. Sub-hour may be marketed only for a named zone after 4 consecutive weeks of measured performance at that latency.
2. **Full anti-spoof stack or no attestation.** In-app capture only, server-verified Play Integrity, cross-location corroboration (incl. the sim_fleet network view), challenge objects, pHash screening, approach tracks, two-verifier consensus above a buyer-set threshold (§(c)). No "lite" tier carrying the Oracle's signature.
3. **Observation, not guarantee.** Every attestation, contract and sales page uses the §(c)/§(d) formulation — never a warranty of legitimacy, ownership or future state. Counsel-reviewed terms before the first paid query.
4. **Premises, not people.** No photography of individuals as a task; face/plate minimisation; no buyer surveillance/publication use; sensitive-site blocklist; verifier safety protocol (daylight residential tasking, no-entry rule, paid abort) from day one.
5. **PDPB-ready data handling now.** On-task-only tracking with explicit consent, Pakistan-hosted location storage, retention limits, derived-observation-only sharing — built to the draft bill's shape before any law compels it.
6. **Pilot gates are binding.** The §(f) fraud bar and success criteria are pass/fail as written, including the stop rule: an undetected spoof class or an upheld privacy/safety failure pauses the product, not just the report.
7. **Do not fight ICIL on its tender.** Compete on speed, API, evidence quality and overflow/small-batch reach. A core-tender bid needs a separate costed case at incumbent prices — assumed unwinnable until proven otherwise.

If conditions 1–3 are unacceptable — if the product *must* be sold as instant, or as a guarantee — the verdict flips to **NO-GO**: that product cannot yet be truthfully delivered in Pakistan.

At 277, blended ARPU is **~USD 1.00–1.18/month** — the "around a dollar" shorthand three independent MVNO analyses use **[industry]**. A consumer MVNO must live inside ~PKR 300/month *total* spend; a business smart number at PKR 5,000–6,000/month lives in a different, funded budget. That asymmetry is the staging logic. On businesses: **~5.2 million SMEs (~40% of GDP, ~30% of exports)** **[official — Senate/SMEDA, 2024]**, or **~7.14 million MSMEs** on newer government definitions **[official — PID/SMEDA, 2026]** — cite the range. Even 0.1% of 5.2 million is 5,200 businesses; Stage 1 needs a few hundred.

### (b) Competitors / substitutes, with live evidence

**Pakistani telco business offerings.** Jazz Business is the strongest enterprise substitute (§(a)), but it does not publicly sell a self-serve per-number smart number with AI receptionist at a small-business price; the offer is solutions-led and quote-only. The host field has just narrowed: PTCL acquired 100% of Telenor Pakistan on 31 Dec 2025 (PKR 108 billion), PTA approved the merger into PTML on 19 Mar 2026, and it closed at the start of July 2026 **[industry]**. There are now **three national groups: Jazz ~36.3%, the merged entity (to trade under e&) ~36.0%, Zong ~26.7%, plus SCO ~1.0%** **[industry — Graystone from PTA June figures; corroborated through Sep 2026]**. Zong, the smallest, fits Graystone's profile from other markets — "the smallest operator almost always has the most aggressive wholesale strategy" **[industry]**. If an operator bundles a smart business number free into postpaid, Stage 1's defence must be the Sendqon layer (AI on the tenant's own data, Gawah, messaging inbox, Rektrs ID), not commoditised telephony.

**Local hosted-PBX/CPaaS players.** voipcom (§(a)) is the price anchor and the proof of the legal pattern Stage 1 must copy: it states plainly it is **not a licensed carrier**, that "public voice and DID numbers in Pakistan come through licensed LL/LDI operators," and that its numbers are allocated through them **[industry — vendor's pages]**. A 2026 industry guide also names Octopus Digital Network, Contegris (Intellicon), Nayatel's cloud contact centre (with ZIWO) and H3's CloudCall **[industry — a directory, not verified pricing; none publishes a tariff]**. Pattern: many competent hosted-PBX shops, none positioning an AI-receptionist-first *number*, none with a messaging tenant base. Sendqon's edge is distribution plus the AI/messaging stack.

**Global smart-number precedents** validate the category and price band: **Google Voice USD 10/user/month Starter (20 Standard, 30 Premier)**; **Quo (ex-OpenPhone) USD 15 annual / 19 monthly Starter**; **Grasshopper USD 14/month annual flat** **[industry — vendor pricing tracked Nov 2026]** — PKR 2,770–5,265 at 277, close to voipcom's PKR 6,000. None sells Pakistani DIDs as its home product; the AI-receptionist layer is where they are thinnest and Sendqon's stack is already built.

**Global MVNO economics — thin, distribution-led.** Juniper estimates **333 million MVNO subscribers globally in 2026, rising to 438 million by 2030 (revenue USD 47B → 54B+)** **[industry]** — ~USD 11–12/sub/month globally, an order of magnitude above Pakistan's ~USD 1. GSMA counted **2,138 MVNOs in Aug 2025, only 96 Full MVNOs with their own core** **[industry — via Avante]**: the world's MVNOs overwhelmingly do not own a core. Benchmarks put a **branded reseller at USD 8–15 ARPU, 15–25% gross margin, break-even 15,000–25,000 subscribers** **[industry — Spenza]**, and **airtime margin alone rarely covers acquisition and care** **[industry — Avante]**. Winners have free distribution: Capitec Connect (a bank's arm) reported R442 million net income for FY2026 on ~1.5 million subscribers; Tesco Mobile ended 2024 with 5.7 million customers; Walmart's Bait alone holds ~20 million lines in Mexico **[industry — company/regulator data as reported]**. Every exemplar starts with millions of existing customers; none starts by buying subscribers in a $1 ARPU market.

**MVNO-in-a-box / MVNE providers — what they publish and what they don't.** **Plintron**, "world's largest multi-country MVNA/MVNE" (Telco SaaS/TaaS: BSS/OSS, provisioning, SIM, charging, CRM/billing; 170M+ connections, ~175 launches claimed) **[industry]**, and **Transatel (NTT)** ("first MVNE in Europe," 200+ launches, **"MVNO-in-a-Box" in 1–3 months**) **[official — Transatel]** publish **no per-subscriber price, setup fee or revenue share anywhere found** — commercials are "talk to sales." Amdocs, Telness Tech/Gigs and Alepo likewise describe the *shape* (setup fee plus per-subscriber / pay-per-user / revenue share) and publish **no amounts** **[industry — Avante, Sep 2026]**; launch claims of 3 weeks–3 months assume a host already connected in your country — in Pakistan it is not (§(d)). The one published-price exception is a small US vendor, MVNOINABOX.AI: **USD 99.95/month + USD 0.75/active line/month (Growth USD 120 + USD 0.50); SIM/eSIM USD 3; wholesale rates quote-only** **[industry]** — a US floor illustration, not a Pakistan quote; even there the real cost (wholesale airtime) is unpublished. Pakistan itself has **no established neutral aggregator and no mature local MVNE** **[industry — MVNO Index; PortaOne]**, and one live project works through a 170-page wholesale contract that does not describe the technical interfaces **[industry — PortaOne]**. **There is no MVNE list price — there is a negotiation, per host, per country. In Pakistan there is not yet even an enabler to negotiate with.**

### (c) Technical feasibility by stage

**Stage 1 — Overlay (sellable now).** SIP trunk in → Asterisk routing/IVR/recording → AI agent as first answerer → human transfer → transcript and callback tasks written back to the tenant workspace. DIDlogic documents this pattern generically **[industry]**, and Sendqon already owns both ends — no platform purchase is implied. Gawah is a PBX feature (announced, consented recording, sealed and certificated). Insaan Check in overlay form is a mid-call challenge (callback/SMS code, voice confirmation) — weaker than the SIM-bound Stage 3 form, and must be sold as such. The trust score in overlay form is a dashboard/receptionist lookup, not yet a property "inside" the number for the world.

*DID sourcing — the reality.* Local geographic DIDs are the real product: 021/042/051 numbers allocated through licensed LL/LDI operators are available via local providers today — 051 same-day, 021/042 in 24–48 hours after KYC **[industry — voipcom]**. Rektrs would not hold the allocation; a licensed operator does. International CPaaS is thin and expensive: DIDlogic lists Pakistan numbers **from USD 8/month, inbound from USD 0.01/min, porting "Not available"** **[official]**; Twilio has **no voice-enabled numbers in this locale** and charges **USD 0.1550/min local / 0.1760 mobile** to terminate into Pakistan **[official]** — ~PKR 48.8/min to mobile, killing any inclusive bundle routed internationally. **Stage 1 must use local licensed routes; international CPaaS is a demo route.** Local SIP per-minute rates are **not published** (contract-only), so the pilot (§(f)) must *measure* landed cost before any inclusive bundle is priced. **[planning assumption: launch on geographic DIDs; mobile-range smart numbers are a later negotiation, not a launch claim.]**

*Feature-phone constraint (every stage):* everything customer-facing must work over **voice (IVR/DTMF + speech), SMS and USSD, missed-call signalling as fallback** — no data, app or link assumed. That does not limit the receptionist (it *is* voice), Gawah or a trust check. It disciplines identity: overlay "cloud identity" means a Rektrs ID account plus voice/SMS re-verification on the new SIM — credible; the Stage 3 form (the number/IMSI itself is Rektrs-issued) is stronger and unavailable until then. Marketing must not blur the two.

**Stage 2 — Aggregator (already tracked; one paragraph, as briefed).** Already researched and running as a separate track (Aggregator Feasibility Report, 2026-11-18, in this goal's files): CVAS Registration for SMS Aggregator services — directly or via resellers first — riding operator/aggregator SMPP binds, with Sendqon's own SMPP server and SIM fleet as the owned edge. For Rektrs Line it is the lawful middle step giving operator relationships, Sender ID/short-code experience, negotiating volume, and an SMS leg for the smart number (confirmations, Gawah certificates, Insaan Check codes) independent of any tenant's fleet. It confers **no** right to issue numbers or provide public voice (§(d)), and must not be sold as if it did.

**Stage 3 — MVNO, and what "cloud-native core" would mean.** A "full" MVNO owns HLR/HSS, policy/charging, IMS for VoLTE, SMSC and BSS, renting only radio. The open-source blocks are real: **Open5GS** (EPC + 5GC, Release 17, VoNR via Kamailio IMS), **free5GC** (Apache-2.0, Release 15+, containerised) and **Kamailio** (SIP/IMS) **[industry — arXiv evaluation 2024; project sources]**. Commercial packagers sell such cores with "zero per-subscriber licence fees," support priced only on scoping calls **[industry; amounts unpublished]**, and the software runs on commodity hardware (one installer: 2 cores/4 GB core-only) **[industry]**. **That is not the cost of a credible core** — that cost is 24/7 operations, lawful interception, CDR retention, DIRBS/EIR, SIM logistics, interconnection testing, QoS reporting and support, none with a published Pakistani price. **[planning assumption: a minimal credible core operation is a multi-person, multi-crore-rupee-per-year commitment before the first subscriber — a planning judgement, not a quote.]** And the decisive point, in §(d): **the Pakistan licence as drafted prohibits an MVNO from installing its own radio access *or core* elements at all**, allowing only care, billing and customised-service systems. An own core is therefore (i) a private-network/lab capability, (ii) a negotiating point about who builds the *host's* parallel virtual core (the "host MVNA" model), or (iii) a future-framework hope — not a Stage 3 build item for public subscribers. Any Stage 3 plan budgeting "our Open5GS core carrying public subscribers" budgets for something the licence text forbids.

### (d) Legal / regulatory — the decisive section

*Research assessment of published instruments, not legal advice. Operative documents: the Policy Framework for MVNO Services (Federal Cabinet-approved, Dec 2025) and PTA's MVNO licence template; where only the April/June 2026 draft/consultation template was retrievable in full, that status is flagged.*

**The instrument — what was actually found.** PTA **opened MVNO applications on 24 June 2026** under the Federal Cabinet-approved *Policy Framework for MVNO Services* (Dec 2025), licences for **15 years**, services under own brand via MNO agreements **[official — PTA press release, 24 June 2026; PTA-hosted framework PDF]**. The full licence text retrieved is PTA's **Consultation Draft Licence, April 2026** **[official]**; guides reading both framework and June 2026 template report the same terms **[industry — PakEra]**. **Caveat: confirm against the final template at application — the April text is marked DRAFT, and PTA publishes no processing time [industry — PakEra].**

| Term | What the instrument says |
|---|---|
| Definition | Operator with **no spectrum assignment** serving its own customers through commercial agreements with licensed MNOs, after a PTA licence **[official — framework via Dawn/PTA]** |
| Initial fee | **USD 140,000 nationwide, upfront**, in PKR equivalent before the Effective Date (draft cl. 4.1.1); conversion at NBP TT selling rate, business day preceding payment (cl. 4.3.8) **[official — draft licence; corroborated Dawn, Business Recorder]** |
| Evaluation fee | **USD 1,000 with the application** **[industry — PakEra reading of the framework checklist]** |
| Duration | **15 years**; renewal request **at least 30 months before expiry** (cl. 1.2) **[official — draft licence]** |
| Annual charges | **ALF 0.5% + USF 1.5% + R&D 0.5% = 2.5%** of gross revenue from licensed services *minus inter-operator costs and PTA/FAB mandated payments*; within **120 days** of year-end; **late +2%/month**; numbering charges extra, in advance through the parent MNO by 30 June (cl. 4.1–4.3) **[official — draft licence]** |
| Eligibility | **Incorporated in Pakistan / SECP-registered**; technical, business and financial documentation **plus a draft agreement with an MNO**; checklist requires a 5-year feasibility file, attested director documents, non-defaulter/insolvency undertakings **[official via Dawn/PTA; industry — PakEra]** |
| Host agreement — the hard gate | One or multiple MNOs; an MNO may host multiple MVNOs; **every agreement needs PTA approval before effect and cannot be changed without PTA's written approval**; must cover service type, QoS, MNP, DIRBS, roaming/interconnect, care/data privacy, disputes, national security, revenue sharing. **Without a willing MNO there is no application.** **[official — draft cl. 1.1, 9.2; industry — PakEra]** |
| May own | Own brand, marketing, customised services, and **solutions/hardware for customer care, billing and customised services/applications only** (cl. 1.1.5) **[official]** |
| May NOT do | **No spectrum; no own radio access or core network elements** (beyond the care/billing exception); **no own roaming/interconnect** (only through the parent MNO, cl. 1.1.6, 1.1.9, 2.5–2.6); **no service in AJ&K/GB**; no direct interconnection outside Pakistan **[official]** |
| Mandatory services | Nationwide: **emergency access, operator assistance, national + international long-distance via the parent MNO's LDI agreements** (cl. 1.1.3) **[official]** |
| Commencement clock | **No service or payment collection until a Service Commencement Certificate**; **launch within 1 year or the licence is terminated**; if the host agreement ends, the licence is **suspended** until a new agreement is submitted (one year to secure one) **[official — draft contents/framework; industry — summaries]** |
| Protections | MVNO may not discontinue mandatory services without **90 days' notice + PTA approval** (cl. 6.2); **parent MNO may not degrade/suspend/terminate the MVNO's service without PTA approval** **[official]** |
| Compliance load | QoS standards; **call records ≥1 year** (incl. IMEI, location); lawful interception, DIRBS, biometric SIM verification, monitoring at MVNO cost, security regulations, complaint system, PTA-approved contract/code **[official — draft Parts 3, 6, 7]** |

**The contradiction that matters most.** Framework summaries say the regime supports models "from basic resellers to full MVNOs with their own core" **[industry — ProPakistani]**. The template says the opposite (cl. 1.1.6.5). MVNO Index (Oct 2026), from the regulator, hosts and applicants, resolves it bluntly: **"Pakistan's MVNO regime does not allow a Full MVNO in the classic sense… The MVNO owns the brand, the customer, the offer and the billing relationship. Everything that switches, stores, intercepts or routes traffic belongs to the host"** **[industry]**. So the licence buys the *customer relationship*, not the core. The smart properties would be lawful as "customised services" on the care/billing side — where Sendqon's stack already lives. Stage 3 adds over Stage 1 only: **own-branded SIMs, own blocks (via the host), MNP identity, a regulated customer relationship** — not network control.

**Why Pakistan has had zero operating MVNOs.** Under the old regime: the 2006 guidelines set **USD 5 million for a 10-year licence** **[industry — Graystone; corroborated 2010]**, and in 2010 **PTA had received no application at all**, cellular companies showed no interest in hosting, and revenue-share fears were cited **[industry — PPF/The Nation, 2010]**. (One 2025 report claims a cut "from $50 million" **[industry — ProPakistani]** — conflicting, treated as **[unverified]**; this section uses **$5M → $140k**.) Under the new regime the host, not the fee, is the blocker. Graystone's *Cooperation Paradox* (Aug 2026) **[industry]**: the framework "carries **no reference offer, no access pricing, no wholesale obligation of any kind**"; "**MNOs are under no obligation to accept MVNO applications, publish wholesale pricing or offer standard commercial terms**"; safeguards are "procedural, not economic," and a badly negotiated agreement is locked in (no change without PTA consent). Entry "depends on finding an MNO partner willing to use MVNOs to reach a segment they can't otherwise serve, on fair terms." PortaOne adds the operational half **[industry]**: with no local MVNE, first licensees duplicate host integrations under a one-year clock, against contracts that may not specify interfaces. A reported ruling that **Onic, PTML's own digital brand, "works like an MVNO and needs this licence or a restructure"** (order unpublished) **[unverified — PakEra]** suggests the first bite may be on operators' sub-brands. **Status today:** applications open since June 2026, candidates discussed **[industry]**, but **no operating independent MVNO was found in any published source** (negative finding). In one line: *2006–2025, priced out and hosted by nobody; 2026–, priced in and still hosted by nobody — yet.* The gate moved to three boardrooms — better, but not Rektrs' to open alone, and not schedulable.

**The adjacent licence ladder.**

| Rung | Fee/term **[official — PTA fee schedule unless noted]** | Permits | Does not permit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CVAS Registration** | **PKR 10,000, no initial/annual fee, 5 years** | Registered services only: **SMS Aggregator, Content, Voice Mail** **[official — PTA CVAS FAQ]**, via agreements with LL/LDI/cellular operators. The aggregator track's rung. | Issuing numbers; public voice; any carrier role |
| **CVAS Licence (Data/Voice)** | PKR 5,500 processing; **PKR 300,000 nationwide** initial; ALF 0.5%; 15 years | Data: internet/data, vehicle tracking. Voice: card payphone, premium rate, trunk radio **[official — PTA CVAS FAQ/IM]**. **Caution:** legal-guide reporting says PTA has **suspended/stopped granting CVAS data/voice licences (ex-vehicle tracking) and required holders to obtain LL** **[industry — Lexology]** — verify grant status before relying on this rung. | Mobile services; mobile number blocks; public switched voice as carrier |
| **Local Loop (LL/FLL)** | USD 500 processing; **USD 10,000 per telecom region**; ALF 0.5%; 20 years | Fixed local services **in one region**, geographic/non-geographic numbers + short codes under the National Numbering Plan. The rung local PBX providers ride on (via the holder). | Nationwide service from one licence; mobile |
| **LDI** | USD 500; **USD 500,000**; ALF 0.5%; 20 years | Nationwide long-distance + international voice — the layer MVNOs must use *through* their parent MNO. | Retail mobile |
| **Cellular** | USD 500; **initial fee by auction**; ALF 0.5%; 15 years | Spectrum + mobile network — the hosts' rung. | — |
| **MVNO** | USD 140,000 + 1,000; 2.5%/yr; 15 years | Own-brand mobile, own SIMs, billing/care, blocks via host, MNP | Own spectrum/RAN/core, own roaming/interconnect, AJ&K/GB |

Reading it: Stage 1 needs **no rung of its own** — it rides an LL/LDI holder's numbers, its SMS position regularised by CVAS Registration. Stage 3 is the only rung making Rektrs an issuer of mobile numbers, and the only one gated by a competitor's signature.

**Number allocation.** Numbering is administered by PTA under the Number Allocation & Administration Regulations and the National Numbering Plan (E.164; leading digit = service/network) **[official — PTA NNP]**; only licensees may apply **[industry — Lexology]**. *Stage 1:* Rektrs gets **no allocation** — DIDs belong to the LL/LDI licensee and are assigned onward, so Stage 1 contracts (who keeps the number if the relationship ends?) matter more than technology. *Stage 2:* no subscriber blocks; Sender IDs/short codes via partners under PTA/operator rules **[industry]**. *Stage 3:* the MVNO **requests numbers/short codes only through its parent MNO; PTA allocates blocks of 10,000 against the parent MNO's NDC (existing or new) to the MNO, for assignment to the MVNO; annual numbering charges are paid through the parent MNO** (draft cl. 2.1.1; PTA's MVNO allocation SOP mirrors this) **[official]**. Numbers are **portable to any other MNO**, MNP a joint MVNO/MNO duty (cl. 2.3) **[official]**. Even at Stage 3 the number's network identity remains the host's NDC — a travelling trust score travels via MNP and Rektrs' records, not via owning the range.

**What an applicant learns only on application:** PTA publishes the framework, fee, checklist and template. It does **not** publish any wholesale price, reference offer or model host agreement; any processing timeline; any final-vs-draft licence divergence; the per-block numbering amount ("along with applicable fee"); or which MNOs will host, on what interfaces. **Wholesale economics — the number that decides Stage 3 — are unpublished by design, discoverable only in negotiation.** Any Stage 3 plan showing a wholesale cost line today is showing an invention.

### (e) Unit economics by stage

**Stage 1 — price vs cost.** *Price:* **PKR 6,000/extension/month** locally for a hosted-PBX number with recording, IVR and inclusive domestic calling **[industry — voipcom]**; global tiers convert to PKR 2,770–5,265 (§(b)). A price at/above the anchor is defensible only because the AI receptionist replaces a human cost — sell against a receptionist's salary, not telephony. *Cost:* international DID rental **from USD 8/month (~PKR 2,216)** **[official — DIDlogic]** is an import upper bound; **the local LL/LDI wholesale DID + trunk price and local SIP per-minute rates are not published** (contract-only) and must be measured; international termination (USD 0.176/min ≈ PKR 48.8) **[official — Twilio]** rules that route out. The AI layer's published 2026 band is **USD 0.05–0.08/min** (OpenAI GPT-Live 1 at 0.05 + backend; ElevenLabs/Deepgram agents at 0.075–0.08, LLM/telephony extra on ElevenLabs) **[industry — vendor pricing, Oct–Nov 2026]** → **≈ PKR 13.9–22.2/min**. A self-hosted cost on Sendqon's stack has **no published benchmark** — producing it is the pilot's job.

*Worked shape* **[planning assumption, from the anchors above only]**: sell one smart number at **PKR 5,000–7,500/month** with a fair-use AI cap (e.g. 300 AI-answered minutes, per-minute overage). At the hosted-AI band, 300 minutes cost PKR 4,170–6,660 in AI alone — the bundle barely survives, hence the cap, short-call design (greet, answer, capture, transfer; target <2 minutes per AI-handled call) and the self-hosted measurement are conditions, not details. At an illustrative PKR 4/min, 300 minutes cost PKR 1,200 and margin is healthy after DID/trunk costs. **The Stage 1 margin question collapses to one measurable — Rektrs' landed cost per AI-answered minute on local routes — which cannot be researched off the web; the pilot exists to produce it.** Until measured, price as base fee + overage off the hosted band, so margin cannot go negative. Calibration: one PKR 6,000 number ≈ **20× consumer ARPU**; ten numbers ≈ 200 consumer SIMs of revenue, with no licence or host negotiation. Stage 1 is the business; Stage 3 is the option.

**Stage 3 — honestly.** Revenue ceiling: blended ARPU **PKR 276–326/month (~USD 1.00–1.18)** **[official/industry — §(a)]**; a 1.5–2× segment premium is a **[planning assumption]** with no Pakistani MVNO to evidence it. Regulatory take (2.5% + numbering) **[official]** is small next to real costs. Platform cost per subscriber is **unpublished for Pakistan and unpublished by every major MVNE** (§(b)); the only published per-line anchor anywhere, a small US vendor's **USD 0.50–0.75/active line/month** **[industry]**, would consume **45–68% of a USD 1.10 ARPU before wholesale airtime, SIMs, acquisition, care or compliance** — not a Pakistan quote, shown to demonstrate the structural problem every $1-ARPU analysis names. At global reseller margins of 15–25% **[industry]**, 20% of PKR 306 is **~PKR 61/subscriber/month** of gross margin for everything; global reseller break-evens (15k–25k subs on $8–15 ARPU) imply **well into six figures of subscribers** at Pakistani ARPU on a like-for-like cost base **[planning assumption — extrapolation, labelled]**. Capital gate, sourced part: **USD 140,000 ≈ PKR 38.78 million** + USD 1,000 **[official/industry]**. Capital gate, honest part: the licence is the smallest cheque. Unpriced and unpublished: host integration + wholesale commitments, BSS/care platform, SIM distribution and biometric-verification channel, the one-year launch burn before revenue, and acquisition against three hosts' marketing. **No credible total Stage 3 capital figure exists in public sources, and this section will not invent one.** Stage 3 economics change character only with the global winners' trait — **distribution already owned** (§(f)).

### (f) Smallest honest pilot

**Stage 1 only. Explicitly.** *Offer:* Rektrs Line (Overlay) — one geographic smart number per tenant: AI receptionist (Urdu/English, tenant's facts, human transfer), announced recording + transcript, IVR, missed-call callback workflow, WhatsApp/SMS follow-up in the tenant's existing Sendqon workspace, Rektrs ID as account identity. On the production Asterisk + AI stack, numbers via a local licensed LL/LDI route, **not** international CPaaS termination. *Who:* **5–8 existing Sendqon tenants** (e.g. school office, clinic, e-commerce seller, services firm). No new-logo sales, no public launch, no consumer offer. **[planning assumption — cohort sized to measure across verticals while concierge-supportable.]** *Terms:* 8 weeks, **charged from week 1 at PKR 6,000/number/month, AI cap + overage in writing** — a free pilot measures politeness, not demand.

*Measure — the four numbers nobody can research:* (1) **landed cost per AI-answered minute**, all-in; (2) **containment** — % handled without transfer, average AI-call duration, tenant-judged transfer accuracy; (3) **missed/unanswered-call rate before vs during**, incl. after-hours capture; (4) **paid continuation at full price** at pilot end.

*Success criteria, in advance:* (i) landed AI-minute cost leaves ≥50% gross margin at PKR 6,000 with the cap, or a repriced bundle that does, arithmetic shown; (ii) ≥60% containment cohort-wide, no uncorrected customer-harming AI pattern; (iii) unanswered rate at least halved for ≥4 tenants; (iv) **≥4 tenants continue paid at standard price.** Miss (i) or (iv) and Stage 1 is a feature to bundle into Sendqon plans, not a standalone product — say so and stop.

*What is NOT being piloted (named):* no MVNO step — no licence application, no approach to an MNO *as an MVNO applicant*, no SIM issuance, no consumer number, no "Rektrs-issued line" claim, no Insaan Check/trust marketing beyond overlay truth, no cloud-core build. Supplier conversations are ordinary DID/SIP procurement.

*Revisit Stage 3 if any one occurs:* (1) a host offers wholesale terms surviving §(e) at a reachable subscriber count; (2) **≥100,000 reachable customers at near-zero acquisition cost** for a Rektrs SIM are evidenced; (3) the final licence/PTA clarification restores a genuine full-MVNO/core model; (4) a funded partner underwrites acquisition/distribution with Rektrs as the smart-services layer. Absent one, Stage 3 stays deferred however well Stage 1 does.

### Verdict & conditions

**Stage 1 — Overlay: GO WITH CONDITIONS.** (1) Numbers/termination only via local licensed LL/LDI routes, with written number-retention terms; international CPaaS termination is ruled out by its published price. (2) The §(f) pilot runs first — charged, criteria fixed in advance; no inclusive-minutes bundle beyond it until the landed AI-minute cost is measured. (3) Language stays at overlay truth: "a smart business number powered by Sendqon," never "Rektrs-issued line"; Insaan Check/trust claims limited to overlay truth. (4) Consent announcements (the Gawah pattern) on every line from the first pilot call.

**Stage 2 — Aggregator: CONTINUE (already running)** — Stage 1's SMS leg and operator-relationship engine under CVAS Registration; it confers no number-issuing or voice-carrier rights.

**Stage 3 — MVNO: NO-GO / DEFERRED**, until *all three* gates clear: **Gate 1 — Host:** a signed, PTA-approvable draft agreement on wholesale terms surviving §(e); today there is no reference offer, no published wholesale price and no MNO obligation to deal **[industry — Graystone]**, in a market just consolidated from four hosts to three. **Gate 2 — Scope clarity:** confirmation against the *final* licence (not the April draft) — on the draft, the cloud-native core is prohibited and Stage 3 is a brand/billing/customer licence on the host's core **[official — cl. 1.1.6.5]**; if that stands, re-decide whether that prize is still the ground the other innovations stand on, or a distribution business wearing the concept's name. **Gate 3 — Capital + distribution:** a funded plan for the unpriced majority of Stage 3 cost *and* a ≥100,000-customer near-zero-acquisition base, without which ~PKR 300 ARPU cannot carry dollar-linked platform and wholesale costs.

The concept is right about direction and wrong — on current instruments — about one load-bearing detail: in Pakistan in 2026 you may become a layer of the network's *customer relationship* for USD 140,000 and a host's signature; you may not become a layer of its *core* at any price the framework names. Build the smart number now — in the PBX, the agent and the trust data — and let Stage 1's tenants and traffic earn the seat from which a host negotiation could one day be won.

## #9 The Rektrs Loop — The Number That Becomes a Bank

**Verdict up front: GO WITH CONDITIONS — as a direction and a map; NO-GO as a near-term product.** The only slice buildable now, with no financial licence of any kind, is a voice-first orchestration layer that helps people use money held and moved by licensed others — a partner bank, microfinance bank (MFB) or Electronic Money Institution (EMI) — over rails the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) already runs, above all Raast. Everything past that line — holding float, issuing e-money, scoring for credit, lending — is a separate licence and a multi-year programme, not a feature. Three lines are never crossed: never hold customer float without an EMI licence or a licensed partner holding it; never use the word "bank" in public-facing naming; never lend without a lending licence and the mandatory bureau checks that attach to it.

The concept has four parts: **(1) the Line** — a smart number as distribution; **(2) the Bureau** — every transaction making the number's trust score smarter (see #1, GO WITH CONDITIONS); **(3) Float + Credit** — scores unlocking micro-credit while small balances sit as float, loans creating more transactions and data; **(4) the Exchange + Agents** — businesses on top, settlements feeding the score again. Its flagship face is the "talking bank": the account *is* the number, the signature *is* the voice (see #6), the branch *is* a Rs 3,000 feature phone. This section tests the whole loop — problem, precedents, the licence-free slice, the law, the money, the pilot. Sibling findings are referenced, not re-derived. Grading: **[official]**, **[industry]**, **[unverified]**; internal estimates **[planning assumption]**. Where nothing is published, this section says so.

### (a) Problem + payer

Pakistan's inclusion statistics disagree by construction; an honest case uses all three measures. Demand-side, the World Bank Global Findex 2025 (survey year 2024) puts account ownership at **27.3% of adults** — **42.3% of men, 11.9% of women** — leaving ~**113.3 million adults without an account**; only 8.0% borrowed formally in the past year **[official]**. Supply-side, SBP's NFIS measure puts inclusion at **69% by December 2025** (target 75% by 2028), women's active accounts up from 20 million (2021) to 37 million (2025) **[official]**. A PIDE brief (Nov 2026) reconciles them: the two series "should not be used interchangeably" — SBP counts reported accounts; Findex asks adults **[official]**. Karandaaz K-FIS 2024 sits between: **35% included**, but **14% of women hold a full-service account vs 56% of men**, **11% own a mobile wallet vs 48%**, phone ownership **46% vs 82%**, and only 9% of excluded adults trust banks **[industry, reporting K-FIS]**. The Loop-relevant reading: the binding gap is now *operating* an account yourself — an account a husband, son or agent operates for you generates no first-party history the Bureau could learn from. Findex ownership among the poorest 40% *fell* (17.6%→13.6%, 2021–24) **[official]**.

Literacy is the constraint the flagship face is designed around. PBS HIES 2024–25: literacy (age 10+) **63% — 73% male, 54% female**; rural 55% vs urban 74%; female literacy 31% in Balochistan **[official]**. The Census basis in the Economic Survey 2024–25 is lower: 60.65% / 52.84% female **[official]**. Either way, ~4 in 10 adults — nearly half of women — cannot read the menus, PIN prompts and SMS confirmations app/USSD banking depends on. Devices confirm voice is not nostalgia: PTA reported ~196 million subscribers by 2024–25 **[official]**; PTA data reported in 2026 put smartphones at **71% of network devices, 2G at 29%** **[industry, reporting PTA]**; late-2025 reporting put **~40% of users on feature phones** **[industry]**. The magnitude agrees — a feature-phone base in the tens of millions, concentrated among the poor, rural and female users targeted, and often *shared* in households (46% female ownership), which shapes the design in (c).

Who pays? In the Loop the system pays itself, so each pool must be sized honestly. **Transactions:** SBP's Q3 FY26 Review records 3.7 billion retail transactions (PKR 168.8 trillion) in one quarter, 92% digital; Raast alone did **742 million / PKR 23.3 trillion** that quarter (664m P2P; P2M up from 36.3m to 55.9m), and FY25 Raast totalled 1.27 billion / PKR 29.6 trillion **[official, via industry reporting]**. But Raast P2P is **free to the user** and SBP frames Raast as *cost-recovery* **[official]** — fees live at the service layer, not the transfer layer. **Float:** SBP Branchless Statistics (Oct–Dec 2025), as reported, show **150.7 million registered accounts, only 66.6 million (44.2%) active, deposits PKR 243.9 billion — average balance PKR 1,618** **[industry, reporting SBP]**. At ~12% yields (see (e)), Pakistan's *entire* branchless float fully invested yields ~PKR 29 billion/year gross, and it belongs to incumbents **[planning assumption — arithmetic]**. **Credit margin** is the only pool with real margin — and the only one needing a lending licence, bureau checks and loss absorption; its price was cut sharply in November 2026 (see (d)). The payer logic is weakest where the concept is most lyrical (float) and strongest where it is most regulated (credit).

### (b) Precedents and substitutes

**(i) Asaan Mobile Account (AMA) — the state already built the USSD version.** Jointly designed by SBP and PTA under the NFIS, with NADRA verification, 13 branchless providers, all mobile operators and one technology operator (VRG, a licensed TPSP/PSO-PSP), AMA lets anyone open a branchless account with any participating bank from any phone, no internet, on one USSD code **[official]**. One correction to our own brief: the concept note gives the code as *228#; SBP/PTA's published launch material and all contemporary reporting give **\*2262#** **[official]** — a small discrepancy, and exactly the kind a voice product cannot afford in market. AMA launched **13 December 2021**, campaign following in August 2022 **[official]**. SBP figures cited then: **3.3 million accounts, 6.1 million transactions worth PKR 40.5 billion** **[official, via press]**; VRG's CEO stated ~**5.7 million accounts** by Oct 2022 with a **~40% female share** (vs ~18% conventional) **[industry]**. **After 2022, no regular official AMA adoption series is published.** That silence is evidence: AMA proved remote, interoperable, no-internet opening works and reaches women at twice the normal rate — then stalled at single-digit millions against 113 million unbanked adults *[inference from absent later figures]*. The lesson cuts in the Loop's favour: AMA removed *access* barriers (branch, paperwork, lock-in, internet) and adoption still stalled, because it did not remove the *operation* barrier — text menus, keying a CNIC number, managing a PIN. **The state has commoditised the channel and the onboarding. Voice must be the differentiator at the operation layer — speak a name and an amount, hear your balance, confirm in your own language.**

**(ii) India: UPI 123PAY + Hello! UPI — voice works as an interface; onboarding is where it dies.** **123PAY** (RBI, **8 March 2022**) extends UPI to ~400 million feature-phone users via IVR, feature-phone app, missed-call and proximity sound **[official]**; the limit was later raised to ₹10,000 **[official]**. **NPCI publishes no separate 123PAY volume series, so reach cannot be verified.** Field research (MicroSave, Oct 2023) found real appeal but slow uptake: onboarding required **two OTPs inside an IVR flow** — a text task smuggled back into a voice product; recognition failed on accents and noise; **no saved payees, no history**; limited-numeracy users needed human help to onboard and set a PIN **[industry]**. The predecessor USSD channel (*99#) had shrunk from 2.44m transactions (2017) to 1.47m (2022) **[industry, reporting NPCI]**. **Hello! UPI** (Sep 2023) added conversational voice payments (Hindi/English, AI4Bharat) — and still completes each transaction with a **UPI PIN**: voice is the interface, PIN the authorisation **[official — NPCI]**. No Hello! UPI volumes are published either. Lessons: (1) a central bank *giving away* the voice channel did not produce mass adoption — distribution and trust are the scarce assets; (2) friction must be removed from onboarding and recovery, not only the transaction; (3) voice-plus-PIN is what a major regulator has actually shipped — the burden of proof on voice-as-signature sits with us (see (c), #6).

**(iii) M-Pesa — the float is a trust, credit came from banks.** Kenya's National Payment System Regulations, 2014 require e-money issuers to hold **all customer funds in a trust fund, never below amounts owed, unmixed with operating funds** (Reg. 25), redeemable **at par** (Reg. 44), and bar a non-bank issuer from **lending or investing customer funds** beyond the regulations (Reg. 45) **[official, via industry legal summaries]**. Hence M-Pesa credit (M-Shwari, Fuliza) is provided by licensed banks (NCBA, KCB), not from balances **[industry]**. CBK told parliament M-Pesa held ~**KSh 250 billion in trust** **[industry]**; trust-income disposition is contested policy — in Sep 2026 former CBK Governor Njoroge proposed distributing part of it to wallet holders **[industry]**. Even at M-Pesa's scale — **KSh 41.68 trillion transacted in FY2026 (~2.4× Kenya's GDP), 40.66 million customers** **[industry, Safaricom disclosures]** — float income is a regulated by-product, not a goldmine. Agents warn stage (4): **333,011 agents in FY2026** shared a flat ~KSh 37.4 billion commission pool, so **average agent commission fell to ~KSh 9,353/month, the lowest in M-Pesa's history**, while each agent self-funds ~KSh 100,000+ of float **[industry, from Safaricom disclosures]**. Pakistan already has **730,000+ branchless agents** **[official — SBP via CGAP]** — the Loop would rent a thin-margin agent economy, not invent one.

**(iv) Sesame Credit — the cautionary tale at the Loop's heart.** In 2015 the PBOC licensed eight firms — including Ant's Sesame (Zhima) Credit and Tencent Credit — to *pilot* personal credit scoring **[industry]**. Sesame built exactly what the Bureau imagines: a proprietary score from platform data, unlocking privileges and credit inside its own ecosystem. **In 2017 the PBOC granted none of the eight a formal credit-reporting licence**, citing conflicts of interest (a platform scoring users for its own lending), data-hoarding between pilots, and score quality; permissions were effectively revoked in February 2018, and the PBOC instead created **Baihang Credit** (36% industry association, 8% each pilot) to pool non-traditional data under state control **[industry]**. Alibaba and Tencent then *refused to hand loans data to Baihang* (Sep 2019) — the data was the asset, the licence the leash **[industry]**. By 2023 most private scoring initiatives had been shut down as the PBOC re-centralised **[industry]**. Pakistan encodes the same instinct more mildly — bureaus are SBP-licensed under the **Credit Bureau Act, 2015**, SBP runs eCIB, and bureau checks are wired into lending law (see (d)) **[official]**. The hard teaching: **a private score that gates the score-owner's own credit is a structural conflict of interest in every regulator's eyes we have evidence for.** The Bureau survives only if built as (a) an internal risk layer whose outputs are *also* checked against eCIB/licensed bureaus when credit is granted, and (b) separable — licensable, spinnable, shareable if SBP requires. A moat the regulator can order drained is not a moat; Sesame's data advantage did not save a licence it never got.

**(v) JazzCash / Easypaisa — real scale, and why the loop is still open.** JazzCash (Mobilink Microfinance Bank) reported **~PKR 9.5 trillion gross transaction value in 2024, ~19.7 million monthly active users, ~350,000 merchants** **[industry, company-reported]**. Easypaisa processed **~2.7 billion transactions in 2024 worth ~PKR 9.5 trillion (~9% of GDP)** **[industry, company-reported]** and on **28 January 2025** Telenor Microfinance Bank converted to **Easypaisa Bank, Pakistan's first Digital Retail Bank** **[official]**. CGAP's 2026 annex concludes growth "consolidated around JazzCash and Easypaisa," whose "scale, brand strength, and vertically integrated agent networks created high barriers" for entrants **[official — CGAP]**. Why they have *not* closed the loop, on evidence: (1) **No bureau** — neither offers a cross-market trust score; their credit (e.g. ReadyCash) is single-lender, in-app, off internal data. No third-party scoring product is published. (2) **The non-reading user is unserved by their primary surfaces** — both are app-first (79.2 million branchless *app* users by Jun 2025 **[official]**) with text-menu USSD secondary; neither publishes a voice channel or usage by non-reading users — **no such figures are published for any provider**. (3) **Float per user is tiny** (average PKR 1,618) — payments utilities with lending side-products. (4) **Parentage is splitting** — Easypaisa moved toward banking under a banking licence; Jazz remains telco + MFB, no bureau, no voice face. The owner's framing survives scrutiny *[this section's assessment]*, sharpened: incumbents close loops by acquiring licences one at a time, as Easypaisa just did. The Loop's window is the operation layer (voice) and the consented data layer — not a permanent vacancy.

### (c) The licence-free slice: voice in front, other people's licences behind

Definition, precisely: **Sendqon never holds, issues or lends money. It operates the conversation — IVR/AI voice agents over its existing Asterisk/telephony stack, plus SMS/USSD fallbacks — that instructs a licensed institution to move money between accounts that institution holds.** Every rupee sits, at every instant, on a licensed balance sheet. Inside that definition the slice is buildable today; outside it, nothing is.

**Rails.** Raast participation is for regulated institutions; SBP "may provide Raast membership" to *commercially licensed* EMIs (Reg. 18) **[official]**. A non-licensed third party cannot be a Raast participant — no such category is published. It *can*, with the customer's mandate, instruct their institution — via its APIs or a licensed PSP/aggregator — to pay. Pricing: **P2P free to the user**; P2M prohibits charging the payer; merchant providers could charge up to **0.25%** under the subsidy scheme that ran Sep 2025 – Jun 2026 **[official, via industry documentation]**. Renewal is **not published**. A voice layer cannot take a cut of the transfer — it charges for the service *around* it (see (e)).

**Partner APIs — verified, not assumed: no public white-label exists.** This research found **no published white-label or partner-wallet programme from NayaPay, SadaPay or any Pakistani EMI** — no developer portal issuing wallets under a partner brand, no published BaaS tariff. What exists publicly is merchant acceptance (business accounts, payment links, checkout) from EMIs and PSP aggregators, plus the regulatory hook: EMI **Reg. 7(I)(h)** expressly permits an EMI to "offer services via secured APIs … to other Financial Institutions, Third Party Service Providers and other Fintech," on 30 days' prior intimation to SBP **[official]**. The law contemplates exactly the partnership needed; the commercial product must be negotiated bilaterally and cannot be priced or promised from public material. JazzCash/Easypaisa business APIs likewise exist for merchants under the sponsoring bank's onboarding **[industry]**. **Pilot consequence: the first partner conversation is the critical path, not the voice build.**

**Agents.** EMIs may use agents — including the existing branchless network — with prior SBP approval and a board-approved agent policy (Reg. 17) **[official]**; branchless agents serve under the sponsoring bank/MFB. The lawful licence-free pattern is the *partner's* agents doing cash-in/out and biometric verification for the *partner's* accounts, with Sendqon providing orchestration and agent-assist tooling. Sendqon handling cash for other people's money collapses the definition and is out of the slice.

**Voice UX, on evidence.** From (b)(i)–(ii): (1) Urdu-first, spoken, short — the working pattern is one number to call (or the system calls you), balance and last transaction read aloud, a saved payee named, an amount spoken, read-back, confirm. (2) Saved payees and history are core, not extras — their absence measurably hurt 123PAY; create payees agent-assisted or from history ("the same person as last time"). (3) Onboarding is a human-assisted event — NADRA verification, biometric where required, PIN-setting need an agent, school/clinic front desk, or trained family member present; design for that instead of pretending a call does it. (4) Shared-handset discipline — no balances read on speaker by default; per-user PIN separation. Sendqon's assets (Asterisk, AI voice agents, school/clinic tenants) map well onto (1)–(3) **[planning assumption]**. Urdu speech-recognition accuracy on noisy narrowband calls is **not published** for any Pakistani deployment found; it must be measured in the pilot (see (f)).

**Voice as "signature" — what SBP actually accepts.** Today that line is an aspiration, not a regulatory fact. The authorisation instruments in the published framework are **PIN/OTP and two-factor authentication** (EMI Reg. 12 requires at least 2FA; cash withdrawal requires biometric verification or 2FA, Reg. 14) and **NADRA biometric verification** for higher tiers and agent cash-out **[official]**; India's shipped voice product is **voice command + UPI PIN** **[official]**. This research found **no SBP instrument accepting voice biometrics as a standalone payment authorisation factor**. Whether SBP would accept voice as *one* factor in a 2FA design is for #6 and a direct SBP/partner conversation — **not established here and not to be quoted as established**. The slice therefore ships as **voice interface + keypad PIN/OTP authorisation** (digits are the one "text" almost every phone user knows), with voice biometrics, if used, only as an added risk signal under the partner's approval.

### (d) Legal and regulatory — the decisive section

The Loop is **four regulatory objects stacked** — money, credit, bureau, line — each with its own regulator and capital. This section covers money and credit from the primary instruments.

**Money: SBP EMI Regulations (1 Apr 2019; revised 21 Jun 2023)**, under the Payment Systems and Electronic Fund Transfers Act, 2007 **[official]**:

- **Capital — the figure, actually found:** start-up capital **PKR 200 million** (~USD 722,000 at PKR 277/USD), with ongoing capital on a slab tied to average daily Outstanding E-Money Balance (OEB): PKR 200m to PKR 4bn OEB; +5% of OEB above PKR 4bn (to 10bn); PKR 500m + 7.5% above PKR 10bn (to 20bn); PKR 1.25bn + 10% above PKR 20bn (Reg. 11). Plus a **security deposit of ≥10% of required capital at SBP-BSC** — 5% non-remunerative current account, 5% government securities under lien **[official]**. Published application fee: PKR 200,000 **[industry, summarising the Regulations]**.
- **Process:** four stages — Initiation, In-Principle Approval, Pilot, Commercial/Go-Live (Reg. 8); SBP decides In-Principle within **60 days of a complete file**, but no published clock governs pilot/commercial stages **[official]**. By Feb 2025 six EMIs were commercial (incl. NayaPay, SadaPay), one in pilot, three in-principle **[industry]**.
- **May do** (Reg. 7(I)): issue/distribute/redeem e-money; acquire others' instruments; routing/switching; payment/bill aggregation, payment initiation, account information; e-commerce escrow (separate approval); API services to FIs/TPSPs/fintechs **[official]**.
- **May NOT do — the sentence that breaks the naive Loop:** "EMIs **shall not conduct the business of banking including the acceptance of funds from public for the purpose of lending**, investments (other than that required under Para 16)" or speculative/virtual-currency activity (Reg. 7(III)) **[official]**. No interest/returns to customers (Reg. 7(V)); issue and **redeem at par, redemption free of charges** (Regs. 7(VI), 15) **[official]**. *Float + Credit cannot live in one EMI* — credit needs a different licence in a different (or affiliated) entity.
- **Float:** no co-mingling; funds in a **trust account at a licensed bank rated 'A' or better**; ≤**50% with one trustee** once OEB exceeds PKR 300m; trust costs **may not be passed to customers** (Reg. 16) **[official]**. And the provision the float thesis depends on: an EMI **may invest up to 75% of the last three months' daily average OEB in government securities maturing within one year** (Reg. 16(VII)) **[official]**. So float income *is*, in this capped form, the EMI's to keep (it cannot be shared as interest) — and the float can never fund lending, with ≥25% kept liquid in trust. Float yield is a treasury return on safeguarded balances, not a loan book.
- **Wallet limits** (Reg. 14, commercial): monthly load **PKR 50,000 on CNIC (VeriSys) verification; PKR 400,000 on biometric**; pilot lower (50,000/200,000); **enhanced wallets to PKR 1,000,000** only with income proof, CNIC–SIM pairing and enhanced monitoring **[official]**. The money layer is a small-balance system by law — consistent with the concept, fatal to any plan imagining large merchant settlement balances inside EMI wallets.
- **Conduct from day one:** NADRA-verified CDD and screening against designated/proscribed persons before activation, 2FA, real-time alerts, STR reporting to the Financial Monitoring Unit, AML/CFT compliance (Regs. 12, 22), board/fit-and-proper governance, reporting, inspection, exit plan (Regs. 10, 24, 25) **[official]**.

**Branchless banking — why JazzCash sits inside a bank.** SBP's Branchless Banking Regulations (revised 30 Dec 2019) mandate a **"Bank-led Model … all the responsibilities of [the] program shall rest with the financial institution,"** open to commercial/Islamic banks and MFBs **[official]**. A telco cannot hold branchless deposits; it owns or partners with the bank that does — hence JazzCash inside Mobilink MFB **[official + industry]**. The Digital Bank Framework is the other bank-shaped door (Easypaisa Bank, Jan 2025 **[official]**); its heavier requirements are **not costed here** and must be priced separately — EMI numbers must not be borrowed for it.

**Lending — three licensed doors, none of them the EMI.** (1) **Microfinance bank (SBP)** — deposit-taking micro-lending at scale, bank-grade prudential regime; this research relies on no single published capital figure and **does not invent one** — take current minima from SBP's prevailing MFB regulations at decision time. (2) **NBFC with Investment Finance Services licence (SECP)** — the non-deposit-taking lending door, and the licence class behind Pakistan's legal lending apps **[official]**. (3) **The digital-lending conduct regime (SECP)** binds whoever lends digitally: Circular 15 of 2022, consolidated in **Circular 12 of 2024**, tightened by **Circular 26 of 2026 (16–18 Nov 2026)** **[official]**. Current nano perimeter: **max PKR 50,000 per borrower, tenor ≤90 days including rollovers; no automatic rollovers, no compounding; aggregate nano exposure across all NBFCs ≤PKR 100,000 per borrower; APR cap cut from 274% to 90% (new borrowers) / 70% (repeat) with all fees inside the cap; total recovery ≤2× principal**; mandatory **Key Fact Statement** in English *and Urdu* before disbursement; cost calculator required; borrower data held in Pakistan; contact-list access prohibited **[official, via Dawn/Business Recorder reporting of Circular 26 of 2026 and the Circular 12 of 2024 text]**. Older summaries (PKR 25,000 per-app cap; "10× policy rate" ceiling) describe the superseded 2022–23 regime — do not quote them. Google Play adds a private gate: one lending app per licensed NBFC, licence proof required **[industry]**. **Bureau checks are mandatory:** SBP's eCIB FAQs state FIs must obtain a Credit Information Report before extending/renewing *any* facility; membership/reporting binds banks, DFIs, NBFCs and MFBs; private bureaus are licensed under the **Credit Bureau Act, 2015** (MFB rules later aligned so a licensed-bureau report can satisfy the inquiry — the duty to *check* stands) **[official]**. A Loop credit stage scoring *instead of* checking would be non-compliant on its face; the Bureau (see #1) is a supplement that makes the mandatory check smarter, never a substitute. SECP is also building a cross-lender borrower database to police the caps **[official]** — the state is closing the "our own data is enough" route from both sides.

**The word "bank"; AML/CFT.** Banking Companies Ordinance, 1962, s.5(b) defines banking as accepting public deposits for lending/investment; **s.8 bars any company other than a banking company from using "bank" or any derivative in its name, or any word calculated to indicate it is a banking company**, absent SBP authorisation, enforced by SECP at incorporation **[official]**. "The talking bank" is therefore unusable as a public product name, tagline or campaign for any non-bank Loop entity — internal concept name only. Likewise "deposits": EMI balances are e-money claims on the issuer; branchless balances are deposits *of the partner bank* — the voice script itself must say whose balance sheet holds the money, in Urdu, out loud. On AML/CFT: an unlicensed orchestrator does not itself become an SBP reporting entity — the partner carries the obligations for its accounts **[official — structure]** — but partners will flow down screening, monitoring-cooperation and audit duties contractually, and Sendqon must never structure orchestration to *avoid* partner controls (no splitting, no unhosted wallets, no off-rail value). On taking any licence, the full stack — CDD, sanctions screening, transaction monitoring, STR/CTR filing to the FMU via goAML, record retention — becomes a direct obligation **[official]**.

**The Line (detail in #8).** PTA's MVNO Policy Framework — Cabinet-approved, notified Jan 2026, applications opened Jun 2026 — prices "our number" for the first time: **15-year nationwide licence, initial fee USD 140,000 (~PKR 38.8 million), annual fee 0.5% of gross revenue plus USF 1.5% and R&D contributions**, on a host MNO's network, own brand/billing/SIMs, no spectrum, no own radio/core **[official, via Dawn and the published Framework]**. An order of magnitude cheaper than the EMI stack it would feed — which matters for sequencing.

### (e) Unit economics

**Capital gate as economics.** EMI entry is PKR 200m paid-up capital before the first wallet, plus the 10% SBP-BSC deposit and the full compliance build. The capital figure is **[official]**; the all-in cost to launch is **not published** for any Pakistani EMI and is not invented here. Ongoing capital *grows with success*: OEB above PKR 4bn drags 5–10% of itself in extra capital (Reg. 11) **[official]** — float growth consumes capital; it does not release it.

**Per-wallet arithmetic, on the system's own numbers.** Average branchless balance **PKR 1,618** **[industry, reporting SBP]**. Invest the permitted 75% at the 11 Nov 2026 T-bill cut-offs (**3-month 12.00%, 12-month 12.41%**; policy rate **12.00%**) **[official]** and gross float yield is ~**PKR 145 per wallet per year** **[planning assumption — arithmetic]**. Against that: NADRA verification, mandatory real-time alerts, agent commissions, monitoring, support — plus *voice minutes on every transaction*, a cost no app competitor bears. Fees do not rescue it: P2P is free; P2M pricing is capped ~0.25% in the only published scheme, on an average FY26 ticket of ~PKR 5,450 **[official, via industry computation]** — ~PKR 14 gross, shared. **A small-balance wallet on float + fees alone does not pay for a voice-first cost structure at any plausible early scale.** No Pakistani EMI publishes per-wallet profitability — **not published**.

**Credit, repriced mid-research.** Under Circular 26 of 2026 a PKR 10,000 nano loan earns at most **~PKR 24.66/day (90% APR) or ~PKR 19.18/day (70% repeat)** — ~PKR 740 over 30 days, all fees in, recovery ≤2× principal **[official]**. Against that stand acquisition, bureau/NADRA checks, collection — and **defaults, for which no official Pakistani digital-nano loss series is published**, so any default figure in pitch material is **[unverified]**. At the new caps the arithmetic works only with low-cost *repeat* borrowers scored on real repayment history — after the Bureau has data — and low-single-digit losses, which cannot be assumed for first-cycle thin-file lending. Do not build the endgame on a margin the regulator just removed.

**Orchestration vs own-EMI.** Licence-free, Sendqon earns a *service* margin — per-transaction voice fees paid by the institution or merchant whose money moves (a school collecting fees, an employer disbursing, a partner buying a voice channel it lacks) plus tenant SaaS fees — with near-zero capital at risk and revenue from the first tenant. Own-EMI captures the (thin) payments margin and (capped) float yield directly, for PKR 200m+ capital and the full conduct stack. **The orchestration margin is smaller per rupee moved and larger per rupee invested, by an order of magnitude, at every scale reachable in the first years** **[planning assumption — on the official figures above]**. The EMI becomes rational only when measured volumes prove a wallet base whose rents clear the capital's opportunity cost — the threshold in (f).

### (f) Smallest honest pilot

**Design:** one existing tenant segment, one partner licence, one job. **School-fee payment and enquiry by voice call for parents of existing Sendqon school tenants.** Sendqon already sends these parents the fee SMS, holds their numbers with consent, and knows the exact amount owed from the school's ledger — the hardest data problem in payments ("how much, to whom") is pre-solved — and the payers include exactly the feature-phone, low-literacy parents this programme exists for. Flow: parent calls (or is called by) the Sendqon voice number → hears, in Urdu, the child's name, fee due and date → pays from their *existing* JazzCash/Easypaisa/bank account or partner wallet → confirms by keypad PIN/OTP under the partner's authorisation → the partner executes over Raast/its own rail → voice confirmation + SMS receipt; the school ledger auto-reconciles. Cash payers are served at the school desk under the *partner's* agent model — Sendqon never handles cash. Fallback if no partner API lands in time: voice *enquiry only* (fee due, attendance, results), payment by existing channels — it builds the voice dataset without touching money.

**Pre-registered measures, not vibes:** (1) ≥70% of first-time low-literacy users complete a payment unaided after one assisted onboarding **[planning assumption — our threshold]**; (2) Urdu ASR accuracy on live narrowband calls, by accent and noise — if >20% of users need >2 retries on name/amount, the design fails and is rebuilt before any scale claim; (3) PIN-entry success/abandonment — the 123PAY lesson says this decides adoption; (4) fully loaded cost per payment vs a price a named payer actually signs at; (5) zero unresolved misdirected payments; women's usage tracked against the K-FIS baseline (11% female wallet ownership).

**Thresholds to even start the EMI conversation — all of:** (i) ≥12 months operating, ≥50,000 completed voice-initiated payments, ≥40% of payers repeating (≥3 payments) **[planning assumption — thresholds]**; (ii) partner-verified data showing *projected* OEB and payments rent would cover EMI running costs at Reg. 11 capital levels — computed from pilot data, not projections of projections; (iii) one licensed partner willing in writing to continue/scale; (iv) a legal opinion on the pilot facts mapping the EMI application and the separate lending entity. If (ii) fails — as (e) predicts it may at small-balance scale — the money stage stays partnered permanently and the Loop still wins as distribution + data + orchestration. That outcome must be acceptable before the pilot starts; a pilot that permits only one answer is marketing, not research.

**Pilot never-dos:** no Sendqon-held "wallet," "balance" or advance-collection account, even temporarily; no lending, advances or "fee on credit"; no "bank" in name, scripts or SMS; no voice-only authorisation; no credit scoring of parents from pilot data outside #1's consent and governance framework.

### Verdict & conditions

**GO WITH CONDITIONS — as a direction and sequencing map; NO-GO as a near-term product; GO now for exactly one build: the licence-free voice-orchestration slice in (f).** The evidence supports the Loop as an *order of operations* — distribution and consented data first (Sendqon today; the Line via MVNO when priced in), orchestration over licensed partners second, money (EMI) third and only on measured evidence, credit last inside its own licence with mandatory bureau checks, the Bureau built throughout as a separable supplement-not-substitute (the Sesame condition). It does not support the Loop as a single launchable invention: Pakistani law deliberately prevents one body from holding float *and* lending it (Reg. 7(III)), reserves "bank" to banking companies (BCO s.8), caps wallets (50k/400k/1m) and nano-loans (PKR 50,000; 90 days; 90%/70% APR), and mandates the bureau check the Bureau would like to replace. The closed loop exists nowhere in Pakistan partly because nobody has built it — and partly because the architecture ensures no single licence *can* close it. It can be closed only by a group of separately licensed entities under common ownership, assembled over years. That is still worth doing. It is not worth misdescribing.

**Conditions:** (1) *Sequence is law* — no EMI application before (f)'s thresholds are met on measured data; no lending entity before a money layer and repayment dataset exist; no credit without eCIB/licensed-bureau checks on every facility. (2) *Partner first* — the critical path is one signed API agreement under Reg. 7(I)(h); no public "partner wallet" claim until a partner signs — none is publicly offered today. (3) *Voice is the interface, PIN/OTP is the authorisation* — until SBP and the partner accept a voice factor in writing (see #6). (4) *Name discipline* — "bank" never appears publicly for a non-bank entity; balances are always described as held by the named licensed partner. (5) *Bureau separability* — the score is built to feed mandatory checks, never replace them. (6) *Float honesty* — float income is never a founding revenue line (~PKR 145/wallet/year gross at system averages); credit projections use post-Nov-2026 caps and carry defaults as unknown. (7) *AMA humility* — external material acknowledges AMA and Raast as the state's rails; the Loop's claim is the operation layer for non-reading users and the consented data layer, nothing larger.

**Never-do lines (absolute):** never hold customer float without an EMI licence or a licensed partner holding it; never use the word "bank" publicly; never lend without the lending licence + eCIB/licensed-bureau check; never authorise a payment on voice alone under current published rules; never let a pilot touch cash outside the partner's agent framework.
