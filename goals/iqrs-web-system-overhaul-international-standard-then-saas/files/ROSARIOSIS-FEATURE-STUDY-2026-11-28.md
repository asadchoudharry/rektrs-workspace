# RosarioSIS Feature Study — what RosarioSIS does, how it compares with Gibbon, and what our two school systems can learn from it

**Date:** 2026-11-28 · **Type:** STUDY-ONLY feature reference · **Subject:** RosarioSIS (github.com/francoisjacquet/rosariosis, rosariosis.org), the open-source K-12 student information system maintained by François Jacquet and in production use in schools internationally since ~2013. This is study #2 of the education batch; study #1 (Gibbon) is `GIBBON-FEATURE-STUDY-2026-11-28.md` in this same folder, and this study deliberately builds on it rather than repeating it.

## 1. What RosarioSIS is, in one paragraph

RosarioSIS is the mirror image of Gibbon. Where Gibbon organises a school around *teaching*, RosarioSIS organises it around the **school office**: enrolment, scheduling, attendance, billing, discipline and the cafeteria are the centre of gravity, and the classroom layer (gradebook, assignments, a quiz add-on, a simple lesson-plan add-on) is competent but plain. It is a single-instance web application (PHP with PostgreSQL or MySQL) that can hold several schools in one install with shared teachers, offers responsive web access rather than native apps, and surrounds its ten core modules with a large add-on shelf — library, hostel, entry/exit checkpoints, messaging/SMS/email, ID cards, Moodle synchronisation and more. Its design instinct throughout is *configuration over customisation*: custom fields and tabs on student/user records, a configurable discipline form, configurable attendance codes and grade scales, so a school bends the product to its paperwork instead of the reverse.

## 2. RosarioSIS's proven workflows, area by area (our own words)

**Scheduling — the standout.** The school defines subjects, then courses, then course periods (a course as actually taught: teacher, room, period, marking period). Students (or staff for them) lodge **course requests**; the built-in **scheduler** then places requests into course periods, and can place a whole group or a single student. Two details make it operationally serious: the full run is fast enough to repeat several times a day while the timetable is being settled, and every time an individual student's requests are saved, the scheduler quietly re-runs *in test mode for that one student*, so a counsellor sitting with the student sees clashes immediately and can fix them on the spot. Courses carry practical flags — whether attendance is taken for them, whether they count toward honour roll and class rank, seat restrictions — and the reporting side can print student schedules, class lists and request lists, and can specifically hunt down **incomplete schedules and unfulfilled requests** so nobody falls through the cracks. A separate add-on imports timetables built in the FET timetabling tool.

**Gradebook.** Each teacher runs a continuous gradebook per course period. Work is organised in two tiers — assignment *types* (homework, tests, quizzes…) and the assignments themselves, with assigned/due dates and point values. Weighting is flexible in three ways: by raw points, by giving each category a percentage of the final grade, or by weighting individual assignments. Zero-point assignments act as extra credit. The teacher configures rounding behaviour and the score cut-offs behind each letter grade. The decisive workflow link: when final grades are due, the teacher pulls the gradebook's computed result straight into the final-grade entry screen instead of re-typing marks — and for a short *progress period*, only assignments due inside that window count. Students can submit work through the system, and teachers can print a progress report for any student at any moment, not just at term end. An **anomalous-grades** report exists purely to surface results that look wrong, as an error-detection pass over entered data. An Activities module derives **eligibility** from the gradebook: whether a student is passing (and therefore may take part in sports or other activities) is computed, not reported by hand.

**Report cards, transcripts, GPA.** Final grades flow into report cards with school-configured grade scales and comment codes; transcripts accumulate a student's record across years; the system computes weighted and unweighted GPA and class rank, and produces honour-roll lists and certificates. An add-on layers *graduation paths* on top — credit requirements per subject tracked year over year and printed on the transcript (a US-shaped construct; see §4 row 13).

**Attendance.** Teachers take attendance per course period on configurable attendance codes; the office can amend records afterwards when information arrives late, and completion reporting shows which teachers have not yet submitted. Reporting covers daily summaries, average daily attendance and absenteeism patterns. An add-on (Staff Absences) closes the other half of the loop: a staff absence is recorded once, affected course periods show up in a cancelled-classes report, and the parents/students of the affected classes can be notified from the same act.

**Discipline.** This is a *referral* system, not a notes system. A staff member files a referral against a student on a form the school itself configures (fields, categories and consequences are school-defined, and custom fields can be added); administrators are alerted to new referrals when they log in; and the analysis side breaks referrals down — by category, by referrer, over time — with discipline logs printable and emailable to parents. The shape is office workflow: incident → referral → office visibility → statistics, rather than a teacher's private behaviour diary.

**Student Billing and Accounting.** Fees are assigned to students (individually or in bulk; a premium add-on automates monthly recurring fees and instalments), payments are recorded against them, and balances, daily transactions and printable statements are always one step away; invoices and receipts print in the premium tier, which also adds PayPal/Stripe collection. A companion Accounting module keeps the school's own books at register level — income and expenses, staff salary payments, and student/staff/school balances side by side. A Billing Elements add-on turns the same rails into a small **store**: books, trips or courses are defined as purchasable items, shown to students/parents as a shop, paid from their balance — and buying a course enrols the student in it automatically.

**Food Service.** A full cafeteria operation inside the SIS, for students *and* staff: menus are built from items and published per day; every user holds a food-service **account with a balance and a barcode**; serving a meal is a scan-and-serve act that debits the account; statements, transaction history, activity reports and printed balance reminders follow from the same ledger. Premium depth adds meal reservations (which can pre-select items when the student is scanned at serving time, via the QR/NFC actions add-on) and recipes/ingredients for the kitchen.

**Students, health and records.** The student record gathers enrolment history, demographics, photo, addresses and contacts (families share an address record), plus a structured **Medical tab**: the student's physician, free-text health comments, registers of immunisations and physicals, medical alerts, and a log of nurse visits. Access can be sliced by profile — the vendor's own examples include a nurse profile that sees only the medical side. Parent accounts can be generated from student contacts, and year-end **rollover** promotes students and carries the structure into the next school year; a school can also be copied as the template for a new one.

**Portals.** Students and parents get real portals, not a notice board: a parent sees each child's information, schedule, assignments, gradebook grades, attendance, eligibility, and can generate the child's progress report as a PDF on demand and view GPA/class rank. The school publishes notes and polls to the portal, and an internal Messaging add-on (with email notifications in premium) connects admins, teachers, parents and students.

**Communication automation.** Beyond bulk Email/SMS add-ons (SMS via a choice of external gateways), the interesting part is *triggered* messaging: absence notifications (instant for late/absent, or after a threshold count of absences), payment reminders fired a configurable number of days before or after a fee due date, birthday greetings, new-assignment notifications — and an Email Alerts add-on that lets an administrator define conditions over attendance, grades, discipline referrals or balance and be emailed when a student crosses one.

**Entry, exit and identity.** An Entry and Exit add-on records comings and goings at named checkpoints (and package deliveries and evening leaves), and a QR/NFC Actions add-on turns a scan of a student's card into a configured act — show the student's info, serve their meal, record an entry, lend a library document. A Student ID Card add-on designs and batch-prints cards with photos and substitution fields.

**Moodle integration.** A first-party plugin keeps a Moodle LMS in step with the SIS automatically: students, parents and teachers are created, updated and deleted in Moodle; subjects, courses and course periods likewise; enrolment in Moodle follows scheduling and drops in RosarioSIS; calendar events and assignments, and even portal notes, are mirrored into Moodle. Registration add-ons can also create the Moodle account at the moment a self-registering student completes payment.

**Add-on shelf (the rest, briefly).** Library (catalogue, loans, barcodes, overdue notifications), Hostel (buildings/rooms/capacity, room rental fees posted into student billing, a visits register on the student record), Quiz (question bank, auto-graded question types, per-question difficulty analysis), Lesson Plan and Class Diary (deliberately simple rich-text records per course period, visible to students/parents, with reminders to teachers who have not filled them in), a Reports add-on that builds saved, calculated reports over student data for state-style reporting, bulk import tools for students/users/grades/timetables, Google and Microsoft single sign-on, and Jitsi video rooms.

## 3. RosarioSIS vs Gibbon — the two directions

### 3.1 Where RosarioSIS is AHEAD of Gibbon (verified, and extended beyond the brief's three)

1. **Scheduling (brief-named — confirmed, and it is the biggest single difference).** Gibbon displays timetables and, via an add-on, lets students choose courses and drafts *suggested* timetables. RosarioSIS has an actual scheduling engine: requests in, placements out, test-mode conflict checks at the moment requests are saved, incomplete-schedule hunting. Gibbon has nothing comparable.
2. **Discipline records (brief-named — confirmed).** Gibbon keeps behaviour notes with descriptors and follow-up. RosarioSIS runs an office referral workflow — school-configured referral form, administrator alerted at login, referral breakdowns and statistics, logs sent to parents. Different shape, more operational.
3. **Food service (brief-named — confirmed).** A complete cafeteria ledger — accounts, barcodes, serve-and-debit, statements, reminders — inside the core product. Gibbon has no food service at all.
4. **Health operations.** Gibbon holds medical information on the profile; RosarioSIS runs it as registers — immunisations/physicals, medical alerts, nurse-visit log — with profile-based access slicing (a nurse who sees only medical). Ahead on the operational side.
5. **Eligibility automation.** Activity eligibility computed from the live gradebook. Gibbon has no equivalent construct.
6. **Checkpoint entry/exit + scan-driven actions.** Recording movement at checkpoints and making a card scan *do* things (serve a meal, record an entry, lend a book) is a whole interaction pattern Gibbon lacks.
7. **Billing breadth at register level.** Gibbon invoices fees well, but RosarioSIS pairs student billing with a school accounting register (income/expense, staff salary payments) and the purchasable-elements store. (Neither matches our own fee depth — see §5.)
8. **Multi-school instance.** One RosarioSIS install can run several schools with shared teachers and copy-a-school setup; Gibbon is strictly one school per install. (Our ERP's RLS multi-tenancy still goes further — §5.)

### 3.2 Where Gibbon stays AHEAD of RosarioSIS (the pedagogy core)

1. **Planner and curriculum.** Gibbon's units → lessons → reusable content blocks, shared with students/parents, with learning outcomes attached and coverage rolled up by department, is a curriculum system. RosarioSIS's Lesson Plan and Class Diary add-ons are rich-text boxes per course period — a logbook, not a planner. No outcomes, no coverage mapping, no scope-and-sequence.
2. **Assessment depth.** Gibbon's markbook is matched by RosarioSIS's gradebook at the *recording* level, but Gibbon adds what RosarioSIS does not have: interactive **rubrics** whose results accumulate as skills tracking, **formal assessment** with external/baseline results normalised to one scale and a **value-added** signal, longitudinal **tracking** graphs, and **crowd (peer) assessment**. RosarioSIS's only analytical extra is spotting anomalous grades.
3. **Individual Needs / IEP.** Gibbon has longitudinal individual education plans with SEN indicators surfacing in the planner and profile. RosarioSIS has nothing in this space — its Medical tab is a clinic register, and a clinic register is not an IEP (the same distinction the Gibbon study drew for our own medical module).
4. **Surrounding teaching workflows.** Gibbon's Data Updater (parent-submitted corrections into an approval queue), Meet the Teacher slot booking, activities sign-up programme, and resources bank inserted into lesson plans have no RosarioSIS counterparts of similar depth (its portal is view-and-download; its activities module is eligibility reporting, not sign-ups).

**Net read:** the two products are almost perfectly complementary. Gibbon = classroom depth, office-thin. RosarioSIS = office depth, classroom-thin. Our systems are office-shaped like RosarioSIS — which is exactly why the Gibbon study's flagged list was pedagogical, and why this study's flagged list is mostly about *operational machinery we also lack* (a scheduler, scan-driven cafeteria/entry actions, referral analytics).

## 5. ⭐ The adoption shortlist — LACK and worth it

1. **The scheduler + course requests** (both systems) — the single biggest idea in RosarioSIS, and the one neither Gibbon nor we have. Requests → engine placement → test-mode conflict check at save time → incomplete-schedule hunting. In PK terms: matric subject-group (science/computer/biology) placement, section balancing, and teacher-period allocation are currently manual exercises repeated every session. This is a programme item, not a quick win — but it is the only item in either study that removes an entire category of office labour.
2. **Food Service completion for the ERP; depth for IQRS** — the ERP's cafeteria is a known missing module, and RosarioSIS's account-with-barcode, scan-and-serve, statement-and-reminder shape is the proven template to implement independently. On the IQRS side the module exists; the borrowable pieces are staff accounts, statements/reminders, and serving by scan (which pairs with item 4).
3. **Discipline referral workflow + breakdowns** (IQRS especially) — keep the behaviour records already built; add the office shape on top: a configurable referral form, administrators alerted at login, and breakdown statistics by category/referrer/period. In PK schools discipline is an office-and-principal function; the referral shape matches how it actually escalates.
4. **Scan-driven actions + entry/exit checkpoints** (both, minor) — one scan framework behind the ID card both systems are building anyway: record an entry/exit at the gate, serve a meal, lend a book, pull up the record. Gate management (late arrivals, early departures, visitor-linked exits) is daily PK reality currently kept on paper registers.
5. **Eligibility auto-reporting** (both, minor) — a read-side rule over gradebook data: who is passing, therefore who may play/represent. Tiny build, ends a recurring paper exercise — and it presupposes the gradebook from the Gibbon shortlist, which is the correct order to build them in.
6. **Anomalous-grade spotting** (both, minor) — a pre-publication error-detection report over entered marks. Cheap, and aimed squarely at the most error-prone act in a PK school office: bulk manual marks entry before result day.
7. **Health registers on the student record** (ERP side) — immunisations, medical alerts and nurse visits as three small registers with role-sliced access, completing the ERP medical partial. IQRS already has this shape built (FC-5) and needs nothing here.

**Deliberately not shortlisted:** the triggered-messaging pack (row 12) and the hostel fee/visits pattern (row 16) are worth having but are *completions of engines both systems already own*, not new capabilities — they belong in the ERP's existing completion blocks and the IQRS communication lane, not in an adoption programme. GPA/graduation credits (row 13) and Moodle sync (row 14) are rejected on PK-context grounds, as tabled.

## 6. Balance — where RosarioSIS is weaker than both of ours

For fairness, RosarioSIS's absences overlap Gibbon's, and several are exactly our strengths:

- **Transport: RosarioSIS has none** — no routes, vehicles or drivers anywhere in core or add-ons. IQRS has transport built; the ERP has routes/vehicles/boarding/GPS ingestion (partial).
- **Payroll depth:** its Accounting records staff salary *payments*; there is no payslip computation of the kind both our systems run (IQRS built incl. payslips; ERP source-complete incl. bank export).
- **Pakistan rails: absent.** No JazzCash/Easypaisa, no local SMS-gateway story beyond generic gateways, no WhatsApp channel, and none of the board-pattern exam machinery (date sheets, admit cards, BISE-style mark sheets) that IQRS runs daily. Its fee model — assign fee, take payment, show balance — is visibly simpler than PK fee practice (fines, discounts, instalments, arrears management), which both of ours model in depth.
- **Native mobile apps: none** — responsive web only. Ours are published on Google Play (IQRS Connect + Workspace) with offline sync and QR attendance; the ERP has an API contract and a Flutter shell.
- **Multi-tenancy:** multi-*school* in one instance is not SaaS multi-tenancy — no tenant isolation model comparable to the ERP's forced row-level security, provisioning and entitlements.
- **Digital ID depth:** its ID-card add-on prints designed cards, but the governed issue/revoke/replace register with QR verification that IQRS built in FC-17 goes further.
- **Classroom layer:** as §3.2 records — no planner worth the name, no rubrics, no outcomes, no IEP, no peer assessment. A school choosing RosarioSIS for teaching depth would be disappointed; that is not what it is for.

**Bottom line:** Gibbon taught us classroom ideas; RosarioSIS teaches office machinery. Its lead over us is concentrated in *scheduling automation and scan-driven daily operations* — the scheduler, food-service accounts, entry/exit checkpoints, referral analytics — while our lead is in *local depth*: PK fee practice, payroll, transport, board exams, mobile apps and (for the ERP) real multi-tenancy. The adoption shortlist in §5 therefore borrows RosarioSIS's machinery to complete our operational platforms, implemented independently under our own licences — and it stacks cleanly behind the Gibbon shortlist, which should still be built first where the two interact (gradebook before eligibility).
