# Kolibri Feature Study — the offline-first learning platform, and what our two school systems can learn from it

**Date:** 2026-11-28 · **Type:** STUDY-ONLY feature reference · **Subject:** Kolibri (github.com/learningequality/kolibri), the offline-first learning platform by the non-profit Learning Equality — current release line 0.19.x (0.19.5, July 2026) — together with its companion tools Kolibri Studio (content curation) and the Morango sync engine.

> **LICENCE LINE — read first.** Kolibri is licensed **MIT**. This study is nevertheless a *feature and workflow reference only*: it describes what Kolibri does, in our own words, so we can decide which **ideas** are worth building ourselves. **No Kolibri code has been copied into any Rektrs/IQRS tree in this batch, and none was installed or run.** (MIT would legally permit reuse with attribution; that is a separate, explicit decision for the owner, not something a study lane does silently.)

**Our two systems, as mapped for comparison:**
- **IQRS** = the live Smart School system as reshaped by the PreSkool overhaul. Its offline story is the strongest thing it owns: the Workspace mobile app and the in-build JavaFX desktop app write to an on-device **outbox queue** and push through the **Staffsync** idempotent write endpoints (34 writes — attendance incl. QR, homework, notices, fee collection where one client transaction key produces exactly one posting, and an offline fee takes a provisional receipt whose final number is issued on sync); reads come back through the planned **Staffpull** delta service (generic `(updated_at, id)` cursor, full snapshot first, deltas after; marks are delta-blind, so marks reconcile by per-pair SHA-256 hash). Local store is one encrypted SQLite database per login. Sync is **device ↔ cloud server only** — there is no device-to-device leg and no content-distribution leg.
- **Rektrs ERP** = `rektrs-school-erp-lms` per `REKTRS-SCHOOL-ERP-GAP-AUDIT-2026-11-23.md` (17 source-complete / 13 partial / 4 missing of 34 PreSkool areas; 0 acceptance-complete). Its LMS line is **PARTIAL** (quiz attempts, assignment grading, SCORM quarantine, xAPI/LTI ingestion in source; no standalone question-bank library; mobile is a shell whose offline sync is listed as 3–5 weeks of future work in the audit's Block D).

## 1. What Kolibri is, in one paragraph

Kolibri inverts the cloud LMS: the **server lives in the classroom**. One modest device (a Raspberry Pi, a spare laptop, a low-end PC) runs Kolibri on the school's local network and serves content, exercises, lessons and quizzes to browsers and to a companion Android app over Wi-Fi — with **no internet connection involved at any point in teaching**. Content arrives in packaged **channels** that are imported once (from the internet, from another Kolibri on the LAN, or from a USB drive) and are then local property. Learner progress is recorded locally and synchronises peer-to-peer between Kolibri devices whenever they meet — in the classroom, on a teacher's visiting laptop, or eventually to a cloud data portal when a connection exists. Where Moodle's centre of gravity is the online course, Kolibri's is **distribution and survival without infrastructure**: content logistics, offline identity, and progress that cannot be lost just because the network is down.

## 2. The Kolibri feature universe (our own words)

**Content channels — the packaging unit.** All learning material travels as a *channel*: a curated tree of topics (folders) and content nodes (video, audio, document, HTML5 app, exercise), assembled with metadata (language, level, licensing, learning activity) and published as one versioned package. Channels are the unit of import, export, update and deletion — a device either has a channel (or a chosen subset of its tree) or it doesn't, and updating a channel replaces its tree coherently rather than file-by-file. Every channel carries a human-readable **token** (used in the import UI) and a 32-character ID (used by the CLI). Public channels are browsable by anyone; unlisted/private channels import only with their token, and a device can be told whether its unlisted channels may be re-exported to LAN peers.

**Kolibri Studio — where channels are made.** Studio is Learning Equality's hosted, free curation tool (also self-hostable in principle). A curator builds a topic tree in the browser, uploads files, pastes YouTube/Vimeo links, adds exercises, tags curriculum alignment, and **publishes** — publishing freezes a version and makes the token live. Two properties matter for us: (a) Kolibri itself **cannot ingest loose files** — a PDF on a USB stick is useless until it has been through Studio (or a script) and become a channel; (b) for bulk/programmatic work there is a scripted path — the *ricecooker* Python framework ("sushi chef" scripts) that builds a channel tree from a local corpus and uploads it to Studio with an account token, which is how the big library channels (Khan Academy etc.) are produced and refreshed.

**Getting content onto a device — three doors.** (1) **From Studio**, when the server device has internet: browse public channels or enter a token, then select the whole channel or prune the topic tree to save space. (2) **Peer import**, from another Kolibri on the local network or a known address: devices auto-discover each other on the LAN (zeroconf), and a school server can pull channels from a district laptop that visited with them. (3) **Attached drive**: channels exported from another Kolibri onto USB/HDD are imported from disk — the documented pattern for never-connected schools is a *USB shuttle*: download/export somewhere with internet, physically carry, import. Imports are resumable, task-managed, and selective down to individual resources.

**Content formats and the exercise engine.** Renderers ship as plugins; the current set covers **video** (MP4/WebM, with subtitle files), **audio** (MP3), **documents** (PDF), **e-books** (EPUB), **slideshows**, **Bloom books**, **HTML5 apps** (a zip of web content served sandboxed in an iframe — the escape hatch that carries simulations like PhET and any interactive web material), and **exercises**. Exercises run on **Perseus**, Khan Academy's question framework: questions are JSON documents rendered by rich widgets (choice, numeric/expression input, drag-and-drop, graphing and more), so an exercise is *software*, not a scan of a worksheet. Kolibri wraps Perseus in a **mastery model**: each exercise declares a mastery rule (e.g. 2/3/5/10 correct in a row, M-of-N, or 100%), the engine cycles questions until the rule is met, and every attempt is logged — which is what makes the coach reports below possible. Quizzes assembled by coaches draw their questions from the exercises already inside imported channels; there is also a **QTI** renderer in the current line for standards-packaged items.

**Lessons and quizzes — the coach's assembly layer.** A **lesson** is a coach-built playlist: resources picked from any channels on the device, ordered, and assigned to a whole class, a learner group, or individuals, with visibility switched on/off. A **quiz** (called Exams before v0.12) is assembled from exercise questions on the device — the coach picks source exercises, a question count, ordering and sectioning; each learner can receive a shuffled/randomised draw. Report visibility is configurable (learners see results immediately on submit, or only when the coach ends the quiz). Lessons and quizzes are *assignments over local content* — they reference channel resources rather than copying them, which is why they stay tiny and sync cheaply.

**Coach dashboard and learner progress.** Per class, the coach gets a home view (recent lesson/quiz activity, who started/completed, who *needs help* — flagged when a learner repeatedly misses questions on a resource), a **Plan** area (build lessons/quizzes, manage groups) and **Reports**: per-quiz (average score, per-learner progress and score, a *difficult questions* view aggregating where the class stumbled, down to each learner's individual attempts), per-lesson (per-resource completion), and per-learner (channels/topics touched, last active, exercise attempt history with the mastery streak visible). Reports print and export to CSV. On a classroom server this is effectively live, because learners are working against the same device; on learn-only devices it is as fresh as the last sync — and the class home view even shows each learner device's last-sync time, making staleness visible instead of silent.

**Users, facilities, devices.** Kolibri's tenancy unit is the **facility** — a school's dataset of users, classes and progress — organised as a collection tree (Facility → Classroom → LearnerGroup). People are facility users with roles scoped to collections: facility **admin**, **coach** (and "assignable coach"), **learner**; above them sits the device-level **super admin**, and *device permissions* (who may import/export content, manage other users' permissions) are a separate axis from teaching roles. Practical touches: bulk user import from CSV, optional guest browsing without sign-in, an option to restrict learners to only their assigned content, an admin PIN on learner devices, and multiple facilities can coexist on one device. The Android app can run as a **learn-only device**: it holds a *subset* of one facility (one or a few learners), auto-discovers the classroom server, downloads assigned resources automatically, and syncs progress back in the background whenever it is on the same LAN.

## 3. The hardware envelope

Kolibri's documented server minimum is deliberately humble — about **1 GB RAM on Linux** (2 GB on Windows) and a ~1 GHz CPU; the docs state plainly that for a facility with **fewer than ~30 computers, a Raspberry Pi works fine as the server**. Field guidance from Learning Equality's community puts a Pi 4 at roughly **25 concurrent active users**, with the honest caveats that video-heavy use costs more than exercise use, the Pi's built-in Wi-Fi is the bottleneck (an external access point is the standard fix), and an SSD plus the optimised `kolibri-server` package (multi-core + caching) is the recommended Pi build. Storage is the real sizing question, and it is content-driven: channels range from a few hundred MB (storybook channels) to ~5 GB (Khan Academy maths only) to ~30 GB (full Khan Academy English). The pattern this creates is a **one-classroom appliance**: a Pi-class box + AP + a preloaded drive serves a class with zero recurring connectivity cost; a whole school is either a bigger x86 box or several classroom boxes that sync with each other.

## 4. ⭐ The sync model — Morango, and how it differs from ours

Facility data (users, classes, assignments, progress logs) syncs through **Morango**, a purpose-built replication engine, and its design is the most instructive part of this study:

- **Partitioned, certificate-scoped datasets.** Data is partitioned (by facility, and further by user for subset sync). Devices hold certificates that scope exactly which partition a given sync may carry — a learn-only device is *cryptographically entitled* to only its learners' slice, not merely filtered by the server's goodwill.
- **A store, not the live tables.** Every syncable record is also serialised into a sync *store* with a **per-record version history** (a chain of version stamps naming the devices that touched it). Sync computes differences from these histories, which is what makes it cheap over low-bandwidth links and resumable across sessions.
- **Fast-forward vs conflict, decided from history.** If one side's history contains the other's, the merge is a fast-forward — apply directly. If both sides changed the same record since their common ancestor, that is a true conflict and Morango **leaves the strategy to the application**; Kolibri's practical answer is architectural: the bulk of learning data is **append-only logs** (attempts, interactions, progress events are immutable, UUID-keyed inserts that can never conflict), so genuine conflicts are confined to a small set of mutable records, and unresolvable conflicting payloads are retained in the sync buffer (visible as `conflicting_serialized_data`) rather than silently overwritten.
- **Deletions are data too.** Soft- and hard-deletion are first-class sync citizens with their own propagation records — a delete on one device is an event other devices apply, not an absence they must infer.
- **Topology: true peer-to-peer, store-and-forward.** Any two Kolibri devices holding the same facility can sync — server↔server, device↔cloud **Kolibri Data Portal**, or a subset device↔full device — and syncs can chain: a roving laptop can carry a facility's updates from school A to school B without either school touching the internet (the Jordan/UNHCR deployments ran exactly this pattern, including staff carrying syncs to learners' homes). Discovery is automatic on a LAN (zeroconf), sync can be scheduled at intervals, subset devices queue for permission to sync with a busy server, and since 0.16 the resources assigned to a learner are **auto-downloaded** to their learn-only device as part of the same meeting — content distribution rides the sync fabric instead of being a separate import errand.
- **Sync-when-online** is the same machinery pointed at the Data Portal: an organisation's schools sync up when a connection appears, giving central visibility without the schools depending on the connection day-to-day.

**Against our design, honestly:** our outbox + Staffsync push is *stronger* where money is involved — Kolibri has no equivalent of "one client transaction key = exactly one posting, provisional receipt, final number on sync", because it never moves money. Our Staffpull cursor is simpler than Morango and adequate for a single authoritative server. What Kolibri has that we do not: **(1)** no peer leg at all in ours — every IQRS sync presumes the cloud server is reachable, so a school whose internet is down cannot even sync phone→desktop inside its own office; **(2)** no content leg — our sync moves records, never learning content; **(3)** hash reconciliation tells us two copies *differ* but not *how they diverged* — Morango's histories make divergence itself inspectable; **(4)** deletes in our world ride `updated_at` cursors awkwardly (a hard-deleted row leaves no cursor trace), where Morango treats deletion as a propagated event; **(5)** subset sync by entitlement — our per-login database is a storage boundary, not a sync partition with credentials scoped to the slice.

## 5. ⭐ Question 1 — Urdu content: what actually exists (verified, not vibes)

Three different things are often confused here; they have three different answers.

**(a) The Kolibri interface in Urdu — YES, real.** Kolibri's own FAQ language table lists **Urdu (Pakistan) ✓** for the Kolibri app (not for Studio), and the current download page (v0.19.5) lists Urdu among supported interface languages. A school can run the whole learner/coach UI in Urdu today.

**(b) Urdu *content* channels in the Kolibri library — THIN, and no Khan Academy Urdu channel could be verified.** The facts, with their evidence:
- Khan Academy Urdu **exists online**: KA's help centre (updated July 2026) lists اردو among its *full* sites; the KA Urdu project (started Aug 2020) passed 1,000 dubbed videos in its first year on YouTube/ur.khanacademy.org; Khan Academy Pakistan currently advertises **Urdu Math (Grades 1–10)** and **Urdu Science (Grades 4–12)**; a Pakistani startup's Urdu AI voices began dubbing further KA videos in 2025.
- But existence online is not existence in Kolibri. Kolibri's Khan Academy channels are built per language through Learning Equality's content pipeline, and across the public catalog, the Kolibri docs and the Learning Equality community forum, **this study found no "Khan Academy (Urdu)" channel** — the one direct community question asking exactly this ("Are there translated/dubbed content channels specifically for Urdu language?", Jan 2019, from a Pakistani school implementer) received **no answer** and no channel pointer. EdTech Hub's platform comparison likewise rates KA's Urdu offering as a *limited subset*, against full localisations elsewhere. Treat any claim of a ready Urdu KA channel in Kolibri as **unverified until someone imports it by token and shows the tree**.
- The largest Urdu curriculum library in Pakistan is **Sabaq Foundation** (free, grades 1–12, aligned to the Pakistani curriculum, 21,000+ videos taught in Urdu) — but Sabaq distributes through its own site/YouTube and **has no Kolibri channel** that this study could find. The Urdu content that does exist inside the Kolibri library is scattered (storybook/early-literacy and general channels with some Urdu items), not a curriculum a school could timetable against.
- **Net:** a Kolibri box deployed in a Pakistani school today teaches mostly in **English** (with an Urdu UI), from international curricula — valuable, but not a substitute for board-aligned Urdu-medium instruction.

**(c) Publishing OUR OWN Urdu channel — the path is open and cheap in fees, real in effort.** Studio accounts are free; a curator assembles the topic tree in the browser, uploads MP4 (≤480p recommended)/MP3/PDF/EPUB/HTML5, authors Perseus or simple-format exercises, and publishes — after which the channel token imports into any Kolibri, including fully offline ones via USB export. For a corpus of any size the ricecooker script path is the sane route (build the tree from files + a spreadsheet of metadata, upload, republish on change). The cost shape is therefore: **zero licence/hosting cost; the real costs are (i) content rights** — e.g. Sabaq's or KA-Urdu's videos can only be channelled with the owner's permission, however open their mission; **(ii) curation labour** — mapping existing videos to our class/subject/chapter structure and writing metadata, days-to-weeks for a subject, not months; **(iii) exercise authoring** if we want practice, not just video. A realistic first artefact: one subject, one or two classes, a few hundred videos already Urdu and curriculum-aligned, packaged as an unlisted channel under our token — a small project, gated on a content partnership, not on technology.

## 6. Question 2 — Fit for load-shedding and weak-connectivity Pakistani schools

**Where Kolibri's model beats a cloud LMS, decisively:**
- **The school day never depends on the internet or, mostly, on the grid.** Once channels are imported, teaching runs on LAN power: a Pi + AP on a small UPS/solar outlasts load-shedding that kills a cloud session mid-lesson; our cloud systems (Smart School web, the ERP) simply stop at the school boundary when connectivity or power does.
- **Bandwidth is spent once, at import, not per learner per day.** A 5 GB maths channel imported once serves a class for a term; the same class streaming video daily is not a serious option on Pakistani school connections.
- **Progress cannot evaporate.** Append-only local logs + peer sync mean a term of learner evidence survives dead routers, expired data packages and a server that only meets the internet monthly.
- **Cost floor.** No per-seat hosting, no recurring bandwidth bill; the recurring cost is hardware care.

**Where it does not beat a cloud LMS:**
- **Nothing live.** No live classes, no fresh internet content, no same-day publishing — a notice, a new worksheet or a corrected video waits for the next import/sync cycle. Kolibri is a library and practice engine, not a communication channel (it has no parent messaging at all — our Sendqon/SMS world has no counterpart here).
- **Update logistics are a job, not an event.** The USB-shuttle and peer-import patterns work, but someone must own them: channel versions, storage pruning on small drives, and Pi/AP hardware failures land on the school, not on a provider. Community threads also show facility sync can occasionally stall and need log-level diagnosis — acceptable for an NGO field team, a real support burden for a small SaaS vendor unless the box is managed.
- **Curriculum and language fit (see §5)** is the binding constraint in Pakistan specifically: the platform is ready before the Urdu, board-aligned content is.
- **Assessment ceiling.** Coach quizzes are excellent for practice and formative checks, but this is not board-exam machinery — no paper generation, no marks-entry-against-register, none of the exam-centre depth both our systems carry.

## 7. Question 3 — Sync ideas worth borrowing

**For IQRS (desktop/mobile offline programme):**
1. **A LAN peer leg for the school office.** Kolibri's zeroconf discovery + device↔device sync is the missing middle of our design: Workspace Desktop and staff phones in the same office should be able to exchange outbox/pull traffic over the school LAN when the internet is down, with the cloud remaining the authority they reconcile with later. This is a topology addition, not a rewrite — our idempotency keys already make multi-path delivery safe.
2. **Deletion tombstones in Staffpull.** Adopt Morango's stance: deletes propagate as explicit events in the delta stream, ending the cursor-blindness that today forces hash reconciliation to catch what a cursor cannot see.
3. **Divergence you can inspect.** Keep the (updated_at, id) cursor for transport, but record a minimal per-entity version stamp (last-writer device + counter) on the entities that reconcile by hash today (marks above all), so a mismatch arrives with its history attached instead of as a bare "different".
4. **Visible staleness.** Kolibri's coach view shows each device's last-sync time as a first-class fact. Our staff surfaces should do the same for desktop/mobile sync state — most "sync is broken" school complaints are actually "sync is three days old".

**For the Rektrs School ERP + LMS:**
5. **The channel as the LMS content-distribution format.** The ERP's LMS is PARTIAL and its mobile offline sync is unbuilt (audit Block D). Kolibri's lesson here is to package course content as a **versioned, importable bundle** (tree + files + exercise items + metadata, one token/ID) that a tenant school can pull once and serve locally — rather than streaming course media from the SaaS. This pairs naturally with the xAPI ingestion the ERP already has: the bundle carries content down, xAPI carries evidence back up.
6. **Learn-only-device thinking for student tablets.** A learner device holding only its owner's partition — with assignments auto-delivering their resources — is the right shape for the ERP's future student app, and its certificate-scoped partition is the clean way to enforce it under the ERP's existing row-level-security tenancy (facility ≈ tenant school is a direct map).
7. **Data-Portal pattern for multi-school operators.** Groups running several schools (the ERP's natural SaaS buyer) get central progress visibility from *sync-when-online* aggregation, without schools depending on the connection — a selling point a pure-cloud LMS cannot copy.

**Not worth borrowing:** Morango itself as an engine (heavy, Django-shaped, and our single-authority topology doesn't need multi-master merge); Perseus as a format (our question-bank direction is our own — the *mastery-loop* idea, however, is worth taking into the ERP's quiz design: cycles-until-mastery with attempt logging is a small, high-value behaviour neither of our quiz engines has today).

## 8. Verdict — where Kolibri belongs for us

**Companion content appliance beside our LMS — piloted, not adopted wholesale; and a feature source for sync design (ideas already harvested in §7).** Kolibri does not compete with either of our systems: it has no admissions, fees, attendance registers, payroll, parent communication or board-exam machinery, and both our platforms are built exactly there. What it has — and neither of ours does — is a **zero-connectivity classroom content + practice layer** with a decade of field hardening. The concrete shape that fits: a managed Kolibri box (Pi-class or a school PC) offered alongside IQRS/the ERP for schools whose connectivity is the binding constraint, preloaded with the English library now and with **our own Urdu channel** (§5c) as the differentiator if a content partnership (Sabaq, KA Pakistan) lands. For the ERP specifically, Kolibri is also the standing proof that the offline-bundle direction in §7.5 is a product, not a theory.

**The trigger that would change this verdict:** (i) a verified, importable **Urdu curriculum channel** appearing (ours or a partner's) would move Kolibri from "pilot appliance" to a headline feature of the school offer in Pakistan; (ii) conversely, if the ERP's own offline bundle + learn-only sync (§7.5–7.6) ships and proves itself in a real school, the appliance role shrinks to schools that want the *global library* rather than offline capability as such; (iii) a paying multi-school customer demanding offline classrooms *now* would justify productising the managed box before either of the above.
